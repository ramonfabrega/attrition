Reading A is `docs/CITIES.md` as first written (five readers, one per
sub-area: placement, construction, city levels, garrisons, capture, all on
Fable 5, their reports in the session's scratch directory). Reading B is the
blind re-derivation: five fresh readers given only the entry points and the
export, forbidden from opening the document, the implementation or A's
reports. The adjudicator (the main thread) compared the two sub-area by
sub-area and took every disagreement back to the decompiled function, the
disassembly (`llvm-objdump`) or the PE bytes (`vt.py` over `rise_z.map`'s
`??_7BuildType@@6BType@@@` = `0x00b42b94`).

Verdicts: **A** the document right, **B** the second reading right, **both**,
**neither**, or **open**.

## Must change — landed

All seven: C2 (the re-baked clock), C9 (the 32-frame period), P10 (the even
circle), L5 (the early return), P5 (the fort region test), L7 (no remask),
L6 (`CityData::pop`). Each is marked inline in `docs/CITIES.md` with a
"second reading" note, `crates/sim` was corrected, and a test added for C2,
C9 and P10. The document's §13 lists them.

## Doubly confirmed

Everything else in the five tables: the arithmetic of every sub-area branch
by branch, and — this time — the predicates too. The first pass over seven
mechanics found the predicates wrong in several places; here the five-way
split of the first reading, with each reader reading only one area in full,
seems to have done what the second reading usually does. What the second
reading still found was at the **edges of the first reading's scope**: a
caller the first reader did not grep for (`calc_wall_stats`), a table it
took on trust (`even_circle_*`), a branch condition read with the sense
inverted (the rush-rules gate), an early `return` ten lines into a long
function. The lesson of the tech audit ("read the loaders") has a sibling:
**grep the writers of every field you call frozen, and the callers of every
function you call once-only.**
