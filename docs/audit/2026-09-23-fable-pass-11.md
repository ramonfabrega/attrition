## What it means

- **The tranche is real, and the price fell where entry 46 said it
  would.** Twenty-three of twenty-nine moved a word at 33 USD each; the
  Great Lakes frame cost twenty times less than at the tenth pass, and
  the reason is the one written then — the word left its fight and
  between fights a landing buys hundreds of frames (518: 351; 497: 279;
  506: 235). Opus 5.5 ran the twenty landings that did most of it, at a
  lower price per landing than Opus 5's nine.
- **The rules track is half closed.** Three of the four captured
  chapters run in lockstep to their traces' end, and the fourth — the
  namesake — is pinned at 1,277 after a capture reached it for the first
  time and found five wiring gaps and no arithmetic error, which is the
  test every Phase 1 claim deserves (552). Two chapters of the design
  have no capture yet; §14's run reservation was spent on Great Lakes
  runs, and a chapter takes its run number at booking now.
- **The loop's own rule went unapplied for forty landings, and only a
  pass could see it.** Entry 41 §1 — the lower map first — was written
  precisely so a single-map chase could not run on past the other map.
  It did, by 2,300 frames, because the rule lived in a queue line that
  named a map and nothing compared the line to the words. Parked 444
  had stated the item. This is entry 42's lesson about prose one level
  up: a rule about *which* item, not a rule about an item. It is a
  guard now, and it was red on the tip.
- **The instrument-defect share fell** from 10 of 14 to 9 of 29 — the
  coverage guard and the both-sides rule took two shapes off the table
  — and the shape that remained is *scope reported, not measured*: a
  ledger with a module missing from its own list, a stand-up read
  counted as per-frame coverage, a first-parting map read as a state.
  Two of the three are guards below; the third (527) wants a recorder in
  the comparators and is named for the next pass.
- **The count was wrong and nothing checked it.** The opener said
  twenty; twenty-nine had landed. A commander that clears mid-tranche
  counts from zero, and the rule said "its landings". Fixed as a
  clause — the count is since the last pass, by `git log` — and not as
  a guard, because a guard that shells out to git inside a test is the
  kind of guard that fails for the wrong reason.
