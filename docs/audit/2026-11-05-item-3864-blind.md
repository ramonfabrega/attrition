# Item 3864 — the diagonal-transition arm, a blind second reading

*2026-11-05, Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5[1m]`), lane `att-3864`, on base `707c3351`.*

The question: does any cell a v1 game lists — at the start, or after a
mid-game rebuild through `Terrain::spot_update_land@0084fa50` and
`Terrain::spot_update_land_batch@0084d540` — reach the one call of
`TerrainOut::check_for_diagonal@008595f0`, in
`TerrainOut::create_transition_renderables@00862c60`? Answered from the
decompile export (`~/ghidra-projects/decomp`), the listing (`llvm-objdump -d
--x86-asm-syntax=intel` of the shipped `riseofnations.exe`), the PDB's type
stream, the install's `Data/tilesets.xml`, and scratch counts over the
traces and dumps on disk. **Part 1 was written and committed before MAPGEN
§19, item 3863's journal and parked 3480a, 3493a and 3863a were opened**;
part 2 adjudicates.

Notation: a cell is `(land, land_sub)` — `WData +2` (`char land`) and `+3`
(`uchar land_sub`), the PDB's `WData` record, 0x1c bytes, the array at
`WorldData::wdata` (`+0x134`). `land` is `TileSetLandTypes`
(`eTILE_FERTILE` 0, `eTILE_COASTAL` 1, `eTILE_OCEAN` 2, `eTILE_NUM_TYPES`
3, `eTILE_TREES` 4; PDB type 0x2715); the dump prints it through
`land_key[]` as `BASELAND`, `SANDY`, `OCEAN`, `NONE`
(`dynamic_initializer_for_'land_key'@004058a0`, printed by
`WData::log_data@006af7e0`). `combo(a, b)` is
`TileSet::is_combo_acceptable@0087abe0(a.land, a.sub, b.land, b.sub)`,
whose returns are `eTILE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` 0, `eTILE_BLEND_OK` 1,
`eTILE_BLEND_NOT_OK` 2. "`b` accepts `a`" below means `combo(a, b) == 1`.
The neighbour order is the `move_x`/`move_y` tables at `0x00adcaf0` and
`0x00adc400`, entries 1–8, read off the PE: NW, N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W.

## Part 1 — the blind report

### The call and its predicate

`create_transition_renderables` walks the transition list
(`ArrayBase<int>` at `TerrainOut +0x66f0`: count `+0x66f4`, data
`+0x6700`) and, for each cell, builds three words with
`TerrainOut::build_trans_flags@00864160` — blocks (shift 6, `[ebp-0x1c]`),
connects (shift 3) and fills (shift 0) — then calls
`TerrainOut::check_blocks@0085b3e0` (blocks),
`TerrainOut::check_connects@0085b790` (connects, reading blocks) and
`TerrainOut::check_fills@0085b2c0` (fills, reading blocks). The gate is
integer, not the `float` the decompiler prints:

```
862d97: mov  edx, dword ptr [ebp - 0x1c]   ; the block word
862d9a: test edx, edx
862d9c: jne  0x862db4                      ; skip
862dac: call 0x8595f0                      ; check_for_diagonal
```

**The call is taken iff a listed cell's block word is zero after
`check_blocks`.** `check_connects` and `check_fills` take the block word
as `param_4` and write only their own `param_3` (both bodies read), so
the word at the gate is `check_blocks`' output.

**The block word.** `build_trans_flags(x, y, 6)` reads bits 6–8 of the
cell's four edges: north and south from the `+0x49a0` grid
(`[(xs+1)·x + y]`, `[… + 1]`), west and east from the `+0x499c` grid
(`[(ys+1)·y + x]`, `[… + 1]`), packed north `0xe00`, east `0x1c0`,
south `0x38` (bit-reversed), west `0x7` (bit-reversed).
`check_blocks` then, edge by edge for an in-map neighbour `nb`: a **full**
edge (all three bits) is cleared iff `combo(cell, nb) == 1`; any other
edge is set full iff `combo(nb, cell) == 1`, and otherwise left as it is.
An out-of-map edge is left as it is. The tail ORs (`0x800 → 1`,
`0x200 ↔ 0x100`, `0x40 ↔ 0x20`, `8 ↔ 4`, `1 → 0x800`) only add bits. So
**the word is zero iff every edge ends empty**: each edge is either empty
and its neighbour does not accept the cell, or full and the cell accepts
its neighbour.

### Every writer of the edge grids' block bits

The grids are allocated and zeroed by `TerrainOut::create_nodes@0085bc70`,
freed by `TerrainOut::clear_nodes@0085bd50`, `TerrainOut::close@00864830`
and `Terrain::init@00850f70`. Bits 6–8 are written by
`TerrainOut::set_edge_x@00859500` and `TerrainOut::set_edge_y@00859410`
with `param_4 == 6` (`*e |= v << 6`; that call also clears `v << 3`), and
by nothing else: every other store into either grid —
`TerrainOut::mark_trans_flags@008625a0`'s tail and
`TerrainOut::check_for_tendrils@00859eb0`'s raw stores (`|1`, `|2`, `|4`,
`|0x38`), and the `= 0x38` resets in both `spot_update_land` paths — touches
bits 0–5. The `param_4 == 6` callers are two:

- **`mark_trans_flags`**, in its orthogonal arm, with `v = 7`: on the edge
  toward the cell's *first* orthogonal neighbour (in N, E, S, W order) that
  accepts it, and only when `check_for_tendrils(cell)` returns 0. "First"
  is `ArrayBase<int>::add_unique@0047adb0`, which returns −1 when the cell
  is already listed: listing, the connect stores, the tendrils call and
  the block store all sit under its success, so a later accepting
  neighbour writes nothing. The listing confirms the decompiler's three
  `is_combo_acceptable` calls all push `(nb.land, nb.sub, cell.land,
  cell.sub)` (`8626ac`, `8626cd`, `8626f7`).
- **`check_for_tendrils`**, with `v ∈ {4, 1, 5}` — **never 2**. With
  `N, E, S, W` and `NW, NE, SE, SW` the neighbours that accept the cell
  (the same `combo(nb, cell) == 1`, out-of-map counting as no): if not N,
  then `NW ∧ ¬W` puts 4 on west and north, and `NE ∧ ¬E` puts 4 on east
  and 1 on north; if not S, then `SW ∧ ¬W` puts 1 on west and 4 on south,
  and `SE ∧ ¬E` puts 1 on east and south. Then `E ∧ W ∧ ¬N ∧ ¬S` puts 5
  on north and south, `N ∧ S ∧ ¬E ∧ ¬W` puts 5 on west and east, and
  exactly three of the four put 5 on the missing side; each of those three
  returns 1, otherwise it returns 0.

**So the middle bit of an edge's block field (`2 << 6`) has one writer:
the `v = 7` store of `mark_trans_flags`, by one of the edge's two cells.**

### Who is listed

`mark_trans_flags` lists a cell two ways:

- **(A) the orthogonal arm**: some in-map orthogonal neighbour accepts it
  (`add_unique`);
- **(B) the tail** (`86289f`–`8628aa`): every in-map orthogonal neighbour
  refuses it (`[ebp-4]`, the refusals, equals `[ebp-0x28]`, the in-map
  orthogonals) and at least one diagonal accepts it (`[ebp-8]`). It is
  appended if absent, and `check_for_tendrils(cell)` runs either way
  (`862c44`).

`TerrainOut::mark_nodes@0085bc20` zeroes the count and marks every cell,
row by row; `TerrainOut::generate_transitions@00861960` is
`clear_nodes; create_nodes; mark_nodes; create_transition_renderables`;
`Terrain::init` calls `mark_nodes` and `create_transition_renderables`.
**The walk empties the list when it ends** (`LAB_008638d2`: `+0x66f4 = 0`).
The only other writer is `Terrain::verify_land@0084d8a0`'s
`SimpleArray<int>::remove`.

### Why an (A) cell never reaches the call

Let `nb0` be the first accepting orthogonal. Its edge is either full —
cleared only if `combo(cell, nb0) == 1` as well — or not full, and then
set full because `combo(nb0, cell) == 1`. So an (A) cell reaches the
call only if it and `nb0` **accept each other**. Reading
`is_combo_acceptable` for each `== 1` arm:

- same land, `b.sub == a.sub + 1` — never both ways;
- `a` fertile, `b` coastal, `a.sub == coast_base` — the converse arm
  (`a` coastal, `b` fertile) returns only 0 or 2;
- `b` ocean, `a` not ocean;
- `b.land > a.land`, `b.land ≠ 2`, outside the fertile–coastal pair,
  `b.sub == 0` and `a.sub == num_tiles[a.land] − 1`.

Mutual acceptance needs the last arm one way and the third the other: one
cell ocean at its last tile, the other of land 3 or 4. **No v1 cell has
`land > 2`**: none in the dumps (below), and no writer outside the
scenario editor stores one (every store into `WData +2` the export
prints). So (A) cells never reach it. *Falsifier*: a `WORLD` dump cell
printing `NONE` (or a `land_key[]` index past 3).

### What a (B) cell needs

A (B) cell `c` has an accepting diagonal `D`, and neither orthogonal
flanking `D` accepts `c`. So its own `check_for_tendrils` puts 4 or 1 on
both edges between `c` and those two flanks, `A1` and `A2`. Each such
edge must end **full**: empty is impossible, and a partial one survives
`check_blocks`, since its neighbour does not accept `c`. Full needs the
middle bit, and with `c` in the tail its only writer is the flank's own
`v = 7` store. **Both flanks must therefore have `c` as their first
accepting orthogonal, must return 0 from their own tendrils, and must be
accepted by `c`** — the last for `check_blocks` to clear the edge.

The shipped tilesets fix the arms (`Data/tilesets.xml`, `TILESET`, its
`BASELAND`/`COASTAL`/`OCEAN` `BASE` children and `TERRAINGROUP/COAST_BASE`,
read by `TileSet::init@0087b370`): **all 17 tilesets** have 4 fertile, 1
coastal and 1 ocean base; `coast_base` is 3 in sixteen and 0 in
`SW_Desert`. `num_tiles` taken as the `BASE` count is a reading of the
loader, not traced. With coastal and ocean single-tile, "`b` accepts `a`"
is only: fertile `s−1` into fertile `s`; fertile at `coast_base` into
coastal; and fertile or coastal into ocean. Ocean accepts no ocean, and
nothing but fertile accepts fertile. So:

- **`c` ocean** — `c` accepts nothing (ocean→ocean needs `sub+1` on one tile).
  Out.
- **`c` fertile at `s`** — `D` is fertile at `s−1`; a flank `c` accepts is
  fertile `s+1`, coastal (with `s == coast_base`), or ocean. Fertile `s+1`
  beside `D` at `s−1` is a gap of 2. Coastal beside `D` at
  `coast_base − 1` is a fertile–coastal pair off `coast_base`. Both
  `combo(·,·) == 0`, which the start grids never carry (below), and which
  `verify_land` repairs (it walks all eight neighbours of every changed
  cell, recursing). That leaves both flanks ocean.
- **`c` coastal** — `D` is fertile at `coast_base`; both flanks ocean.

In both surviving cases **`D` is fertile and touches two ocean cells
orthogonally** (the flanks), and `D` accepts each flank. So each flank has
two accepting orthogonals, `c` and `D`, and must list `c` first. With `c`
at the origin and `D` at `(dx, dy)`: flank `(dx, 0)` lists `c` first only
for `dx = −1, dy = +1`, and flank `(0, dy)` always for `dy = +1`. **`D`
can only be `c`'s south-west neighbour**, the flanks `c`'s west and south.
The rest is satisfiable: the west flank's own north (`c`'s north-west)
must not accept it, so must be ocean, and both flanks' tendrils must
return 0.

**So a (B) cell reaches the call only on this pattern, P**: `c` coastal
(or fertile at `s` with `D` at `s−1`); fertile `D` at `c`'s south-west;
ocean at `c`'s west and south; ocean at `c`'s north-west; neither
orthogonal of `c` accepting it; and, at the start, `D`'s orthogonal
contact with ocean. That contact is allowed at the start only when
`combo(ocean, D) ≠ 0`, i.e. `D.sub == num_tiles[0] − 1 == 3`. For the
coastal case that is `coast_base == 3` (sixteen tilesets; never
`SW_Desert`). For the fertile case it is `D.sub == 3` with `c.sub == 4`,
which no tileset has.

**P's keep-out predicate: no fertile cell is orthogonally adjacent to an
ocean cell.** *Falsifier*: a start dump with a `BASELAND` cell orthogonally
next to an `OCEAN` one, or a traced `create_transition_renderables` frame
whose `GLOG_TERRAIN` detail `0x24` lines appear (`check_for_diagonal`'s own
`Log::say`, detail 0x24, `GLOG_TERRAIN` under `[End Frame]`), or a `HIT`
record for `0x008595f0`.

### The start, and the mid-game rebuild

**At the start.** The grid `Terrain::init` marks is the one the map
generator leaves. The rivers come after (`Terrain::init` →
`RiversOut::calculate_all@00888820` → `River::calculate@008858e0` →
`spot_update_land_batch(list, 0, riverbase)`). Rivers only make cells
fertile, so the pre-river grid's fertile–ocean pairs are the post-river
grid's, plus any whose ocean cell a river took (`RiverOut::ensure_ocean_end`
says a river ends at the sea). That one gap is a reading; the dumps are
post-river.

**After a rebuild.** Both spot paths rewrite the changed cells, run
`verify_land` on them, collect the 3×3 around every change into the
update list (`TerrainOut::add_update_wcoord@008738a0`, `+0x671c`, count
`+0x6710`), reset all four edges of each collected cell to `0x38` (block
bits zero), re-mark each with `mark_trans_flags`, and walk the transition
list, which the last walk left empty. **The rebuild therefore lists only
collected cells, and their edges carry only collected cells' writes.**
That is a subset of what a fresh marking writes, and (B)'s two full edges
still need both flanks collected and re-marked. So a rebuild reaches the
call only on P, on the grid as it stands after the change.

Who calls each land writer that feeds a rebuild:

- **`Terrain::object_placed@00850c40`** — the one v1 mid-game writer.
  Its v1 caller is `Wall::start@0063e810`, a building placed; the other is
  `ScenarioEditor::remove_invalid_objects`. It acts only on a cell whose
  land is fertile, and rewrites only its sub, to the tileset's
  `weatherbase`.
- **`Terrain::convert_beach_to_sandy@0084b8e0`** (a cell to coastal 0) —
  its one caller is `Terrain::init`.
- **`Terrain::check_and_set_halfland_wcoord@0084dec0`** — a cell to
  coastal 0, or **an ocean cell to fertile at `coast_base`** when it marks
  no half-water. It is reached from `Terrain::check_and_set_halfland`,
  called by `Terrain::init` (before `mark_nodes`; the grids are freed
  then, so its `spot_update_land` does no rebuild) and by the scenario
  editor.
- **`River::calculate`** (cells to fertile `riverbase`, through the batch)
  — its callers are `RiversOut::calculate_all`, which only `Terrain::init`
  calls, after `mark_nodes`, so its batch is the start's one rebuild; the
  developer console; and the editors (`RiverEditor::place`,
  `TerrainMap::river_stop`, `ScenarioEditor::init_terrain`).
- **`verify_land`** — changes subs. In every branch a v1 grid reaches
  (its `combo(center, nb) == 0` arms) it stores the cell's existing land
  or fertile into a cell already fertile, so it never changes a land.

**So the start dumps are the grid both start passes mark**: half-land
runs before `mark_nodes`, and the river batch's rebuild runs on the
post-river grid the dumps print. The one v1 mid-game writer keeps every
land as it is. **No v1 mid-game change creates a fertile–ocean contact.**
One other writer could plant P: the developer console,
`ConsoleWin::run_cmd@007d6a70`, which reloads the tileset, stores fertile
3 at a cell, and can recalculate a river. The scenario editor's writers
are cut from v1. **FABLE: is the developer console in v1?**

Mid-game the start's sub constraint is gone. `verify_land` repairs only
`combo(changed, nb) == 0`, and `combo(fertile, ocean)` is always 1, so a
fertile cell next to ocean could carry any sub after `object_placed`. That
would reopen the fertile case of P. **But the land contact itself is the
keep-out, and no v1 mid-game writer creates it.**

### Verdict

**Unreachable in a v1 game.** The call is taken only for a listed cell
whose block word ends zero. A cell listed through the orthogonal arm needs
a neighbour it accepts both ways, which needs land 3 or 4. A cell listed
through the tail needs P: a fertile cell that touches ocean orthogonally,
on the south-west of a coastal (or fertile) cell. That cell needs ocean at
its west, south and north-west, and both ocean flanks must list it first.
**The keep-out predicate is: no fertile cell is orthogonally adjacent to
an ocean cell.**

- **At the start**, it holds on every start grid on disk (0 of 42), and
  the edge model reaches 0 of 80,798.
- **Mid-game**, the one v1 writer (`object_placed`, from `Wall::start`)
  never changes a land, so it cannot create the contact.

What is not settled:

- The predicate is shown on the corpus's grids, not proven for every map
  the generator can draw. Its proof would be a reading of the coastline
  writers (`Map::make_coastlines` and the half-land pass). The `cover=1`
  sweep of every style (item 3493's, by the queue) is the run evidence.
- The pre-river grid of `Terrain::init` is not printed, so its contacts
  are read, not counted.
- `num_tiles` is read as the `BASE` count.
- The developer console can plant P (**FABLE**).

*Falsifiers*: a start dump with a fertile–ocean orthogonal contact; a
`HIT` on `0x008595f0` in any trace; `GLOG_TERRAIN` detail-0x24 lines from
`check_for_diagonal` under `[End Frame]`.

### Settled

- **The predicate.** Both readings derive it independently, to the same
  corner, the same chains and the same tileset arithmetic: P1 ∧ P2 keeps
  the arm out.
- **The start.** No start grid on file holds a P1 or P2 pair, in either
  corpus. The pre-beach initial pass is counted on four lobbies, and
  measured nowhere else.
- **Mid-game P2.** No v1 writer creates a fertile–ocean contact.

### Not settled

**Mid-game P1, through `verify_land`'s depth leak (parked 3863a), which
both readings now hold open.** Neither bounds a v1 flood below 500, and
neither shows one reaching it.

- *Killer for the arm's unreachability*: a late `WORLD ≥ 3` dump holding a
  P1 pair near a building.
- *What settles it without a run*: a reading that bounds the recursion
  depth of every v1 `verify_land` flood on a 60×60 grid —
  `convert_beach_to_sandy`, the river batch, `check_and_set_halfland_wcoord`
  and `object_placed`.

**FABLE**: is the developer console (`ConsoleWin::run_cmd@007d6a70`) in
v1? It is the one other writer of P2.

### What moves

**Nothing.** The readings agree that the arm is unreachable *while P1 and
P2 hold*, and agree that P1's mid-game hold is unread. That is not two
readings saying unreachable, so `TerrainOut::check_for_diagonal@008595f0`
stays an open `blind::NEVER` row, not `RESIDUE`. `Blind:` stays **never 262
· residue 213 · open 49**. Once 3863a is killed (the flood bounded below
500), the row becomes `RESIDUE` on this audit with no third reading: its
reason is P1 ∧ P2, the start counts above and the bound.

**Part 1's two errors are not in the predicate**, which both readings
derived alike. They are in which grid the predicate is evaluated on, and
in whether the repair that keeps it holds. Both are cases of
`docs/audit/README.md`'s "grep the writers of every field you call frozen":
part 1 called `depth` balanced without reading its early return, and
called the start dump the marked grid without ordering `Terrain::init`'s
calls.
