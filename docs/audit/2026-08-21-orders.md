# Audit: orders — the second reading

Reading A is `docs/ORDERS.md` as first written (seven readers, one per
sub-area, on Fable 5) and `crates/sim/src/orders.rs`. Reading B is the blind
re-derivation: seven fresh readers on **Opus 5**, given only the entry points
and the traps, forbidden from opening the document, the implementation,
`docs/audit/` or each other's files — the first time the blind side has been
run on a different model from the first reading, which is the trial
`README.md` had recorded as pending. Seven adjudicators, also on Opus, took
every disagreement back to the decompiled function, the listing
(`llvm-objdump`), the PE bytes or the PDB (`llvm-pdbutil`), and the main
thread re-verified every verdict that changed Rust before it was applied.

The reports, the briefs and the adjudications are outside the repo at
`~/ghidra-projects/reading/orders-2026-08-21/` (`docs/DECISIONS.md` entry 7).

**The run did not go smoothly, and the record should say so.** The first
attempt at the adjudication put seven long agents on Fable at once; six hit
the account's Fable limit within about ten minutes of each other and died,
five of them before writing a single verdict. Only R5 survived. The second
attempt ran on Opus, in waves, with each adjudicator told to append **per
table row** and to mark what it could not settle `**FABLE:**` rather than
guess. Both changes earned their place: nothing was lost the second time, and
the marker was used — sparingly and correctly — on five rows across the seven.

## The headline

**The first reading's arithmetic held; its *prose about scope* did not.**
Where the two readings disagreed, the blind side won more than twice as often
as the document — and in R3 and R5 the document did not win a single
contested row. But the pattern is narrower than that sounds. Almost nothing
the first reading computed was wrong. What was wrong was **which branch a
rule sits in, which end of a list a walk starts from, and which of two
readings of a gate is the live one** — and, twice, a rule the first reading
stated in one section and contradicted in another.

Three findings stand out because **neither reading had them**:

- **`check_build_order` walks a cursor; it does not kill.** The listing at
  `0x6036d0` has no call between the action-bit test and
  `mov eax,[eax+4]; mov [esi+0xd4],eax`. So an intervening `MOVE_TO` is
  stepped over, not removed. Both readings had described a run of kills, and
  the first had hedged the contradiction as "the decompile's loop variable is
  stale; medium" — the hedge was the tell, and it was pointing at a real
  mechanism rather than a decompiler artefact. (R3 C2.)
- **The site the scan stops on keeps its order**, because the `break` jumps
  out before the `repath; kill` — so it ends the pass with a duplicate, which
  the next `do_build` disposes of through its "already finished" branch.
  Found by reading the jump targets rather than either prose account.
  (R3 C5.)
- **`is_gathering_at` matches on the action, not the front of the list**, and
  that one was a live bug. `do_gather` inserts its walk as a `QUEUE_FIRST`
  move *without* the action bit, so during every walk-out and walk-back leg
  the front order is a move and only `get_action` still finds the `GATHER`.
  Under the front-order reading a woodcutter **stopped counting as a gatherer
  the moment it set off, and `check_gatherers` pruned it out of its own
  chain**. The first reading's own §3.3 had the right rule; §6.1's shorthand
  contradicted it, and the implementation followed the shorthand. (R4 G16.)

## Third pass — the Fable ratification, 2026-08-23

The adjudication above ran on Opus by deliberate arrangement — the first
Fable wave died at the account limit, and the goal was to keep moving and
mark what needed Fable eyes rather than lose the run. This pass is those
eyes: every `FABLE:` row taken back to the binary, and every one of the ten
Rust corrections re-verified from its own citation, by Fable on the main
thread.

**The verdict on the run: nothing to retract.** All ten corrections stand —
the two settled in the listing were re-read from the listing
(`check_build_order`'s cursor advance at `0x6036fd` with no call after the
action-bit test; `add_repair_order`'s tail at `0x5e51f0` with no `QueuePos`
compare, where the next function in the image has one), and the eight settled
in the decompile re-read there, down to `is_gathering_at`'s `strict` byte
(`+0x27` = `been_there`) and the off-map fall-through. The `FABLE:` markers
held five honest gaps and zero disguised errors, which is the discipline
working.

**The flagged rows, settled:**

- **R2 O2 — confirmed as §4.6 states it.** `find_upath@00682f30`'s failure
  tail calls the current order's `+0x14` — which the PDB's `UnitOrder` field
  list pins as `is_move` — and, if true, `+0x40` (`update_move_order`), and
  skips the `Unit::kill_current_order(unit, 0)` only when the returned
  `MoveOrder`'s `+0x1c` — `retry` in the PDB — is nonzero. The one open item
  that could have changed sim behaviour changes nothing: the document had it
  right.
- **R3 F3 — settled: `leader_flags & 4` set is human.**
  `LeaderData::is_human@006ec170` is literally `return leader_flags & 4`.
  `find_repair_spot`'s gate therefore reads "deny an AI below effective
  difficulty 2"; a human always searches, with the stance range-doubling on
  the human branch, exactly B's structure. §5.5 rewritten.
- **R1-14 — settled from the PDB, and the anomaly was real.** `UnitOrder`'s
  field list (`LF_FIELDLIST 0x1A0D7`, each `LF_ONEMETHOD` carrying its
  `vftable offset`) gives all 74 slots. §1.1 had the banks crossed: the first
  bank (`+0x3c`–`+0xb0`) is `update_*_order`, the second (`+0xb4`–`+0x124`)
  the `get_*` const twins — and the twins are not uniformly `+0x78` apart
  because the `get_*` bank declares `patrol` before `attack`. `+0x50` is
  `update_attack_order`; `get_attack_order` is `+0xcc`. §1.1 corrected.
- **R6 48 — confirmed.** `CommandPackage::process_all@0094c500` read in
  full: the XOR de-obfuscation (16-bit key from `info.seed >> 8`) and the
  `Random::get(&local, 0, 2)` per-command padding both sit under the network
  semaphore bit, and the padding RNG is a local seeded from `info.seed`, not
  the sim stream.
- **R3 D8 — strengthened.** The opcode-scan caveat is retired by a
  decompile-wide sweep of `UnitData+0x68` bit 0's writers: the one semantic
  writer is `SpellType::cast_create_decoy@00674370`, plus the generic
  script-facing toggle `Group::action_unitmask@006fcb90`, which can set any
  mask bit. The name "decoy" stands.

## The lesson, for the next mechanic

The tech audit's was "read the loaders". The cities audit's was "grep the
writers of every field you call frozen, and the callers of every function you
call once-only". This one's is narrower and sharper:

**A hedge in the first reading is a finding waiting to be made.** Every place
this document said "medium", "the decompiler's local is stale", or "by
analogy", the second reading found something — and in the two worst cases the
hedge was pointing directly at the mechanism it had failed to see, not at a
decompiler artefact. `check_build_order`'s "stale loop variable" *was* the
cursor. Grep your own document for its hedges before you call a mechanic
done.

And its sibling: **when a document states a rule twice, in two sections,
check that the two agree.** §3.3 had `get_action`'s walk right and §6.1
paraphrased it as "first order"; the implementation followed the paraphrase
and carried a real bug for a day. The contradiction was visible in the
document alone, without the binary.
