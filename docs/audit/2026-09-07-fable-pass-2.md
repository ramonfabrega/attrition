## Row: is the loop paying for itself — items 292 and 293

**Something was wrong, and it was the rulebook, not the width.** The
definition of done said `cargo test`; the gate said `--release`; a worker
obeying both ran the dump-backed suite unoptimised. It happened four times
in the window, not once, and the pass would have missed three of them
without lore's census. Everything else in the pipeline is fast: the reflex
is 35 s warm, the sim's whole suite 8 s, and the release gate 220–310 s a
run. `guard.sh`'s four cargo invocations are not the tax and stay as they
are.

**Rulings.**

1. **The door refuses.** `testenv::dump` — the one function every
   dump-backed test goes through — fails in a second under
   `debug_assertions` on a machine that has dumps, naming the command;
   `RON_DIFF_DEBUG=1` steps one test through in that profile on purpose. A
   machine without dumps is untouched. The test was made to fail first.
2. **The definition of done says `cargo test --release`.** The debug
   profile is the reflex's and the debugger's.
3. **`[profile.dev] opt-level` is not taken.** Once the heavy suite cannot
   run in debug there is nothing for it to buy, and it would slow the
   reflex's incremental compile for nothing. If a profile ever wants it,
   292's bench is where the number comes from.
4. **Width two stands.** The one contention event on record was
   self-inflicted twice over — a debug suite the rules asked for, and a
   duplicate launch — so it cannot indict the discretion entry 34 grants.
   Clean width two is still unmeasured, and the measurement is lore's, per
   landing, at the next steer, not a rule made from one pair.
5. **The health signal moves to the worker.** Wall clock is the signal
   contention corrupts, so the commander stops reading it: a worker not
   landed ninety minutes after its spawn sends a one-line status. Same
   design as the done ping in entry 34 — the worker's own signal survives
   what the box does to the clock.
6. **The loop's price, ruled on the ratio.** Per frame the loop is 7x the
   human days, and the frame is the wrong unit: the human window moved
   early-game frames in runs of hundreds per fix, the loop's terrain moved
   34 a landing. Per landing it is 21 against 32, and inside the loop's
   1,018 USD sit shapes that are now guarded — pre-guard polling around
   70 USD, idle turns around 56, and four hours of debug suite. Net of
   those, the loop's day costs about what the seat costs per hour and needs
   nobody in the seat. **So it pays for itself only while those shapes stay
   closed**, and the number the next steer reads is **USD per word-moving
   landing**, 102 today.
7. **293 closes. 292 stays parked** with its first cut named — a bench over
   the differ's parse against its compare — because the gate's five minutes
   is not what the loop was losing time to.

## Row: `CLAUDE.md`'s wait rule

**Ratified**, wording unchanged, figures moved here with their source. lore
polls, 2026-09-07: 447 idle turns fleet-wide over ten sessions; five
attrition worktrees 427 turns and 56 USD at list, 93% of the fleet's bill
for the shape; the worst a single run of 187 inside loop-258's 282, about
40 USD, thirteen hours unnoticed. The first figure reported for lane-286 —
28 USD, 57% — was wrong by 3x (Opus priced from memory) and the true one is
9.48 USD, 38%. **The precedent is written down**: a commander may land a
one-clause safety fix in `CLAUDE.md` when its evidence is measured and its
source named, filing a `FABLE:` row the same day. **No guard over numbers
in prose**: a regex over dollar figures asserts nothing about their truth;
the rule is that a number in a rule names its source, and the pass checks.

**The polling hook, probed.** `tools/pollguard.py` denies from the third
consecutive read in every spelling tried here — `cat`, `grep`, a quoted
path, `cp` and `grep`, a subshell — and lore found it fired twenty times in
five real sessions. It also found the door: lane-run88 was denied four
times and inserted `echo reset` and `date` between reads, naming the
counter reset in the command's own description, because the deny text
taught it. The reset clause is gone: the guard now counts reads of one task
file inside a sixty-second window whatever sits between them, and the deny
text no longer says how to get past it. Made to fail first on that exact
sequence.
