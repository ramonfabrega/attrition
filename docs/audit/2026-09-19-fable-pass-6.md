## What was found

- **The per-landing price held and the per-word price doubled**, because
  the share of landings that moved nothing rose from six of twenty-six to
  ten of nineteen. Every one of those ten was booked with a mechanism in
  its title, and thirteen of nineteen briefs named a mechanism the worker
  then refuted — the market (362), the step-6 purchases (368), the ring
  (392), `Group::target_opportunity` (395), the parked-collider tolerance
  (405), the two named for 391, both suspects of 386. The frame named was
  right every time. This is parked 377, three times over, and it is the
  whole of the price story.
- **The width-two stop measured a lane that never ran.** DECISIONS 40's 78
  was for a parked value-diff lane; the second lane became the rules track
  instead, and it is the cheaper track (45 against 83). The stop is not
  applied to it.
- **The AI track's price per word is 83, past the stop**, and it is the
  wrong-mechanism rate, not the lane, that put it there. The fix is the
  booking convention, and 408 — widen the frame before naming a cause — is
  that convention applied to the current word.
- **The census moved only where the day worked**: `Unit` +3, `Leader` +3,
  `Group` +1, and nothing in the order family. Combat was worked through
  `Unit::think`, `find_melee_pos` and `take_damage`, not through an order
  class, so the six uncited order classes are still six.
- **The held-out map generalises at one tick**: the AI's scout picks a
  wrong destination on frame 1 of Himalayas. It stays undebugged by rule;
  what it says is that the two scored maps' floors are the fit, not the sim.
- **The dead list has a reading trap of its own**: the section that
  enumerates the fifteen dead functions goes on to name the two *live*
  inlined copies in the same paragraph, and a parser that takes the
  section whole reads seventeen. The guard reads the enumeration alone and
  asserts it is fifteen.
