## Headline

Three things, and the first is the one that matters.

**1. The document's largest declared seam has no justification left.**
`docs/GROUPS.md` §6.4 declares `Form::compute`'s slot table a seam partly
because "the arithmetic involves the export's only floats in this family …
but the whole 1,200 lines would have to be read to know where else".
Reader A swept it (A.29) and the sweep replays exactly: over
`00704990`–`00708000`, `00708000`–`0070b9f0`, `0070b9f0`–`0070ea70`,
`0070f8f0`–`00710000`, `00711540`–`00715400` there are **zero** float
instructions, and over `0072cba0`–`0072ed30` there are **fourteen** — two
identical seven-instruction sequences at `0072d00a`–`0072d036` and
`0072d846`–`0072d872`, both computing `trunc((rows[c] − 0.5f) ·
depth[c] · k)` where `k = 2 − (x != 0)` ∈ {1, 2}. The constant at
`0xb694c0` is `00 00 00 3f`, read out of the PE: exactly `0.5f`. The whole
expression is `((2·rows − 1) · depth · k) / 2` truncated toward zero, exact
in `f32` for anything a 128-member group can reach. **The float exposure is
one expression, and it is integer-reproducible.** Whatever else keeps the
slot table a seam — cost, and the fact that no capture pins its output —
the float argument is void and §6.4 must stop making it.

**2. `rise_z.map` and the PE settle four of §13's five guessed vslots, and
three of the guesses were wrong.** Read straight out of the PE's vtable
arrays (`UnitData::vftable@00b41b08`, `BuildData@00b426dc`,
`WallData@00b43054`, `AnimalData@00b412cc`): `+0x48` is **`is_seen`** — not
"the one `action_attack` reads to decide a member must walk rather than
shoot"; `+0x20` is **`is_build`**, 1 for a Build and **0 for a Wall** — not
"can attack"; `+0x1c` is 1 for both Build and Wall; `+0x108` is
`get_stance_type` (the decompiler devirtualises it by name in
`action_stance` itself). And the type-vtable `+0x10c` is settled outright
by `ObjectData::is_siege@0046ef90`, whose entire body is
`this->ptype->vtable[0x10c]()`, not by the weaker `COUNT_SIEGE` argument
§13 offers. The document's "read from their uses, not from a type record"
was never necessary.

**3. Both readers missed something the other had, and the document was
right about the largest of them.** B.29 reports `action_siege_attack_to`'s
anchor as `find_leader(&g2)` and **misses the Manhattan scoring loop
entirely** (`0070ddb0`–`0070dd34`); the document has it, correctly, down to
the per-term `>> 10` and the strict `<`. Conversely the document's §8
building branch, §9 human arm, §10 skip predicate and §3.1 singleton test
are each wrong in a way one of the readers has right. Nine verdicts change
Rust.
