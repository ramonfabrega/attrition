## What was found

- **The loop ran unattended, and the tranche is real.** Fourteen
  landings on two lanes with no human turn, seven word moves, every
  branch merged and reaped, no amend under a live lane. The ninth pass
  ruled three clauses and did not build a guard; the measure it named
  was the landing times, and they answer it.
- **The mechanism-survival number fell and stopped mattering.** Three
  of twelve against five of six — but every death cost twenty minutes
  and no item, because DECISIONS 42's convention put the widening
  first. The convention was written so that a wrong hypothesis is cheap,
  and it is; the rate measures how often the previous worker guessed
  right, which is not the loop's job. It is reported here and retired
  as a thing to steer on. What replaced it is the price per landing,
  which rose a third, and the count below.
- **Ten of fourteen landings turned on an instrument that agreed because
  it was not looking, and the dominant shape was a printed field nothing
  read.** The eighth pass named the family and built four guards for
  four instances; this tranche found six more, and three of them were
  the same one — a key the original prints on every frame, quiet
  everywhere, because the parser never asked for it. No widening of a
  window can find that; parked 488 said so and asked for the guard that
  enumerates the dump's fields against the comparator's. Built here.
  Its first run says the two headline windows print **239 keys on
  twenty paths that nothing reads**, and that three families —
  `DEATH_OBJS`, whose `first_frame` item 485 read off the raw text; the
  frame-level `GUY` list, of which only the three animation-clock keys
  are read; the per-frame `WORLD` totals — have never been opened at
  all. That is the count of blind spots on the frames the loop is
  standing on, and it is the number the next pass reads.
- **Both words are inside a fight and the frame is twenty times the
  price.** 0.19 USD a Great Lakes frame at the eighth pass, 4.07 here;
  the rules word the same. A fight is where every frame draws, so a
  residue costs a landing and buys a frame; between fights a landing
  bought 651. Entry 41's estimate was written at 1,500 frames a day and
  measured 328, 0, 722, 44. It is not a rate; it is two regimes, and
  what ends the expensive one is the instruments — 485 moved nothing
  and closed three rows by reading a record type.
- **The rules slot was empty and nobody said so.** Item 485 found the
  two sides of 683 — the original's death draw at `Unit::close` and this
  crate's puncture from an arrow that lands on nothing — named both by
  frame and by printed field, and **parked** them (491, 492), while the
  commander's `Golden:` line read "484 next" after 484 had landed. The
  queue's own rule is that a finding on the headline's frame books, and
  a worker cannot book; the commander is the one who reads the parked
  file at the merge. Booked here as item 491.
- **Two queue items named no score.** 476 (three value rows on 10234, a
  citizen's flee and a gather field, forty-three blocks under the word)
  and 342 (a scholar seated on the wrong university, re-pinned three
  times, no frame) are parked; 477 stays, as the one value row on the
  word's own squad, written as a takes-chain candidate and not a cause.
- **`combat::share` was written, documented and never called** — a third
  shape, a function rather than a field. Nothing cheap catches it: a
  `pub fn` in a library crate warns for nothing, and the document that
  cited it was right about what the code said. Noted, not taken; if it
  recurs the guard is a document-to-caller check, not a lint.

## Rulings

- **488 — every key the dump prints is read or pinned.**
  `crates/rondata/src/diff/coverage.rs`, on a read recorder in
  `gamelog::reads` that notes every key a parse asks a block for, keyed
  on the block and never its name — the same `OBJECT` under `UNITDATA`
  and under `WALLDATA` are two rows, which is exactly 484's defect. The
  printed side is read off the text by the indent rule and not off the
  arena, because the parser's both-candidates rule records an ambiguous
  field on two blocks and the arena would hold the reader to keys it
  never printed there; the first draft did that and reported every
  `UNITDATA` key as unread on `OBJECT`. The driver is the harness's
  frame decode plus the three walks `initial` folds over every frame
  and the three per-frame readers the widening tests call; a reader
  added to the harness is added there or its keys read as unread, which
  is the right failure. The pin is exact both ways: a key that arrives
  unread fails until read or pinned, a key that is read fails until its
  row shrinks — parked 449's stale-by-success, refused at the pin. A
  family with a parser of its own (`AMMO`, `diff::ammo`) is named and
  left alone. Made to fail first on an empty pin (twenty paths) and on
  the synthetic 484 shape, which a name-keyed recorder passes.
- **480 — struck text is not live text.** `docs_guard::sections` no
  longer counts the bytes inside a `~~…~~` span; the markers count, a
  `~~~` fence is not a marker, and a span is credited to the section its
  opening marker falls in and never past that section's end. A section
  at its pin gains exactly the room it strikes, which is the room an
  amend-in-place correction needs and nothing else. Made to fail first
  on a two-section fixture and on the live table: four pins fell and
  one row was under the ceiling.
- **491 is booked** on the rules track, folding 492 — one frame, two
  draws, one worker; the `Golden:` line names it. **476 and 342 park.**
- **Two clauses in `CLAUDE.md`**: a quiet field is checked against the
  reader before it is called agreeing, with the guard named; and a
  `git checkout <file>` is never chained onto an edit that can fail
  (487 lost both its changes that way and replayed them from its patch
  scripts).
- **313 stays** on the Loop list; it is ccc's verb, not this repo's.
