# Item 443 — the producer was the search all along, and the radius still isn't it

*2026-09-21, Opus 5, `worktree-att-443`. No capture; run113 still unspent.
No score moved: chapter two's word is 616 of 901, sequence 616, first
value disagreement 617 — the three numbers 441 left. `docs/COMBAT.md`
§30, strikes in §27–§29 and `docs/GOLDEN.md` §6, and one new test.*

The brief was narrow: **which producer of an attack order runs on a
cheat-spawned captain's birth frame and does not write `near_o`.** It took
twenty minutes to find out that the second half of that question was
unanswerable, and the rest of the day to make the first half worth
something.

## The probe, and what it said in one run

`#[track_caller]` on `Simulation::add_attack_order`, an env-gated
`eprintln!`, and chapter two's existing walk. Six lines:

```
PROBE f=615 u=1/6 target=Unit(59) pos=New from orders.rs:1600   # think's search
PROBE f=615 u=1/7 target=Unit(59) pos=New from orders.rs:1734   # captain_mirror
PROBE f=615 u=1/8 target=Unit(59) pos=New from orders.rs:1734
PROBE f=620 u=0/9 ... orders.rs:1600
PROBE f=620 u=0/10 ... orders.rs:1734
PROBE f=620 u=0/11 ... orders.rs:1734
```

The producer is `think`'s step-3 search — the one §26–§28 spent four items
on. The two members mirror on the captain's own frame, and the original
does the same thing at 621, so the mirror's timing is right and only the
captain's frame is wrong. The slinger rows are identical in producer and
in frame to the original's: the same code is correct five frames later.

## The half of the question that could not be asked

"and does not write `near_o`" assumes this crate writes `near_o`
somewhere. It does not. There is no such field on `sim::Unit`, no writer
anywhere under `crates/sim`, and `crate::gamelog` leaves the whole
`ObjectData` half unparsed on purpose. So §29.3's "on 616 `near_o` stays
−1 on both sides" was the original's −1 set beside nothing at all, and the
inference it carried — that 616 is not the search — had nothing under it.

That is the same shape as the three findings it corrected: §26.2's
visibility, §27.1's remembered-target arm, §29.2's ring. The difference is
only that this one explained the *absence* of a number instead of a
number, which made it harder to see. Four items' worth of radius work was
retired on it.

## What the field actually says

`Object::find_nearby_target@00648da0` writes `near_o`/`near_who` for every
candidate that clears `valid_target` and `check_target` and is nearer than
the best so far — and the write sits **above** the `param_1` test that
decides whether the candidate may be attacked. So an order out of that
function implies a `near_o` write, and `near_o = -1` with an enemy 7.25
tiles away (well inside the 20-tile reset) means the search found nothing
acceptable.

That answers the item's other question, the one it said the dump could
settle: **never produced**, not produced-and-dropped. `ObjectData +0x34`,
frame 616, value −1.

## Two corrections, and then the measurement

`Unit::think_attack@005f5a80` passes **−1** on every path into
`find_melee_target`. The listing is four instructions: the AI-driven
branch calls `add_to_army` and then `orl $-1, %esi` anyway. So the branch
§28.2 settled selects `add_to_army`, not a radius, and
`find_melee_target`'s `param_1 == 0` block — §27.2's two grids and their
128- and 64-tile sweeps — is unreachable from this caller. §28.3's
"the multiplier that fits is the one neither path applies to this unit"
had been reading a table that never runs.

Then the thing that actually closes it. `find_nearby_target` does not test
a distance to decide where to look; it walks the object grid by **cell**,
768 units, four tiles. And in cells:

| frame | searcher, cell | looking at | that cell | `near_o` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 616 | `1/6` at `(3,10)` | bowmen `0/6`–`0/8` | `(1,10)` | **−1** |
| 621 | `0/9` at `(1,10)` | `1/6` | `(3,10)` | **6** |
| 635 | `1/6` at `(3,10)` | slinger `0/10` | `(1,10)` | **10** |
| 635 | `0/6` at `(1,10)` | `1/6` | `(3,10)` | **6** |

One cell pair, three searches across it, one refusal — and the refusal is
the word. `1/6` never leaves `(2424, 7800)`; the bowmen never leave their
seats; and with the range now known to carry no frame-dependent term, the
cells it scanned at 616 are the cells it scanned at 635. **The bowmen were
scanned and refused.** No radius does that.

I had spent an hour before this bracketing `unit_respond_range` again,
which the brief told me not to do, and the cells took five minutes and
ended it.

## What I did not do

Name a cause. `check_target`'s distance tests are all inapplicable here
and the loop's sits below the `near_o` write, so the survivor is a
target-acceptance predicate: `valid_target`, or `poor_target`'s
type-record arm — its main arm needs the target to be *moving*, and the
bowmen are standing still. Two readings, both written with what would kill
them, neither booked. §26.2, §26.4, §27.1 and §29.2 were each one fetch
short, and I would rather hand over a bracket than be the fifth.

The capture I did not take, named as the brief asks: not a new window —
the searcher's fields are already printed. It would need the *target's*
side, `UnitTypeData +0x9a` per unit over `[614, 640)`, so a Bowman and a
Slinger can be compared on the one byte `poor_target`'s surviving arm
reads. Nothing on disk answers that.

## What I would tell the next item

Grep this crate for the field before reading the original's. `near_o` cost
four items in §26–§28 and one more in §29, and `grep -r near_o
crates/sim/src` — which returns a doc comment and an unrelated local —
would have stopped the second one.
