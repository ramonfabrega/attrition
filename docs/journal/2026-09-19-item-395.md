# Item 395 — the squad's target comes from `Unit::think`'s first line, not from the group

Rules track, Opus 5, its own worktree. The brief booked
`Group::target_opportunity` as what hands a squad its captain's target. It
cannot be: that function's member loop only enters a member which is itself a
**captain** (`o_up < 0`), and group 64 has exactly one — the asker. The real
mechanism is `Unit::think@005f6e40`'s **first statement**, and the golden word
moved **619 → 624**, value diff 620 → 625.

## Re-measuring came first, and it named the mechanism in twenty minutes

The standing warning is that ten booked mechanisms in two days were wrong
while the frame was right. This is the eleventh, and the dump found it before
any reading did. Widening the six units' whole record over blocks 616–622 —
`o_up`, `near_o`, `type/ox/whom`, `orders_x/orders_y`, `idle`, `recharging` —
gave two facts the brief did not have:

- **all three of who=1 already carry `type 10 ox 7 whom 0` at the end of
  frame 615**, the frame they are born, with `near_o 7/0` on `1/6` alone. So
  the handing happens inside the spawn frame, not on a fifteen-frame cooldown;
- **who=0's `0/7`/`0/8` take their retaliation order at 617**, one frame
  behind the captain — and who=0 has `group -1`. No group is involved at all.

A mechanism that works for an ungrouped squad and fires on the spawn frame is
not `Group::target_opportunity`, whose cooldown is 15 frames and whose loop
wants captains. Reading `Group::target_opportunity@007107d0` confirmed the
loop's `is_captain` gate, and what it actually does on this frame is hand the
target back to the **asker**, by the `00710964` arm: `1/6` has no action yet,
`order_type` is `NONE`, so it runs its own `find_melee_target` with
`add_order = 1` — which is where the captain's own ATTACK order comes from.

## The mechanism, and it is five tests and a return

`Unit::think`, before the citizen mask-clear, before both cadence gates,
before everything:

```text
if (!is_captain(this)) {
    a = get_action(units[who][get_captain()]);
    if (a == 0) return;
    if (a->get_type() != 10) return;          // must be an ATTACK order
    if (!valid_target(this, a->o, a->who)) return;
    add_attack_order(this, a->o, a->who, QUEUE_NEW, a->mandatory, 0);
    return;
}
```

**A squad member's whole think is this.** It never searches, never reaches
`think_attack`, never reaches `think_peasant`, `think_caravan`, `think_fish`
or the army joins. `docs/COMBAT.md` §21 is the specification.

The listing was worth the five minutes because the width of the change rests
on it: `5f6e6a jne 0x5f6f7d` puts the **captain** on the far jump and the
non-captain on the fall-through; the three early returns land on `0x5f761e`;
`5f6f69 pushl $0x2` is QUEUE_NEW; `5f6f64 movzbl 0x1c(%eax)` is the captain's
`mandatory`; and `5f6f71 call 0x5e5410` is followed by the function's **own**
epilogue at `5f6f76`. Nothing is inferred.

It also gave a thing nobody was looking for: the shared epilogue at `5f761a`
clears `SubObjectData.flags & 0x10`, the "could not reach" bit the cadence
gate reads, and the mirror's arm lands one instruction past it. This crate
never clears it at all — §21.5, with its falsifier, unimplemented.

## The lesson

The brief's title was the previous worker's successor note, and the previous
worker had read `Group::target_opportunity`'s listing and written it into
`docs/GROUPS.md` §13 — correctly. What nobody did was ask whether that
function could reach a *member*, which its own `is_captain` gate answers in
one line. Widening the record first is what made the question obvious: a
mechanism that fires on the spawn frame and works for an ungrouped squad
cannot be the one with a fifteen-frame cooldown on a group.
