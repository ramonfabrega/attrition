## Step 1: what the guard compared, and the fix

`issue_line` read eleven bytes at the issuer's entry and compared them with
the shipped prologue. `arm_all` had already written `E9 rel32` over the
first five, pointing at the entry's stub, whose copy of the displaced
instructions is the coverage table's `code`. For every one of the
seventeen issuers `funcs.py` displaced six bytes (`push ebp; mov ebp, esp;
sub esp, N`), copied verbatim with no fixup. So the compare could never
pass under `cover=1`.

There were two fixes on offer: read the displaced bytes, or take the
issuers out of the coverage table. The second makes the issuers the one
family an issuer chapter could not record, so I took the first. The guard
moved to `tools/trace/issue_guard.h`, together with the verb → issuer table
and the prologues it checks. Below `orig_len` it reads the table's copy,
and after it the live bytes, but only when the live `jmp` lands on this
entry's own stub and the copy has no rewritten branch. A foreign `jmp`,
another build's bytes or a rewritten copy are still refused. `tracer.c`
finds the entry by binary search on the sorted table.

**Made to fail first.** `issue_guard_test.c` includes the header on the
host. It builds the shipped image and the armed image (the `jmp` to its own
stub), plus five images that must be refused, and with the install's
`rontrace.funcs` it arms each of the 21 verbs from the table's own record.
Against the old compare, which I first wrote into the header, it refused
the armed image and all 21 verbs with code 2, which is run314's result. With
the fix it passes. `rondata::blind`'s
`the_issuer_guard_reads_through_the_coverage_jmp` builds and runs it: it
failed once with the stub branch disabled, and the harness caught a
weakened guard that accepted a `jmp` to another stub.
