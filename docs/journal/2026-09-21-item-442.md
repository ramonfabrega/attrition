## How it was found, which is the part worth keeping

**The brief forbade fitting, and the answer came from reading anyway.** 438
had two target numbers, a ratio of 1.1768, and a warning that it had caught
itself fitting three times. The temptation was to work backwards from
1.1768. The thing that worked was to stop looking at the targets and read
`006c40a0`'s scholar arm top to bottom — where the decompile shows

```c
if (filled < total)       pAVar18 *= 0x3c;
if (filled < total*2/3) { local_20 = 1; pAVar18 *= 10; }
if (filled < total)       pAVar18 *= 5;
```

The first and third conditions are **identical**, which is exactly what a
decompiler artefact looks like, and is the reason nobody had believed it.
Ten minutes with `llvm-objdump` over `006c528c..006c52f5` said it is real:
the third `jge` at `006c52ec` jumps over one instruction and both arms land
on the same label.

Then the arithmetic, in a scratch Python before a line of Rust changed:
`k=6` → 180,000,000 → the tail wraps twice → **5,755,741**; `k=3` →
90,000,000 → wraps once → **4,891,136**. Both exact, first try, no fitting.
**Two independent targets hit by one reading is what a fit cannot do** — a
fitted factor reproduces the ratio and misses the values.

## 1.1768 was a wrap, and §49.2 had already said so

438 recorded the ratio as evidence that `val ∝ k` is not how the per-city
variation works. It is exactly how it works: the inputs **are** 2:1.
90,000,000 and 180,000,000 wrap differently in `wm(fac, wm(want, val) /
divisor)` — one negative then back positive, one not — so 2:1 in goes
1.1768:1 out.

§49.2 wrote the rule for this in so many words: *a number downstream of a
wrap may have no arithmetic relationship to its inputs at all.* It was
applied to the ratio's **numerator** and not to the ratio. The lesson is
not "distrust ratios" — it is that a derived quantity inherits the
meaninglessness of its inputs, and a ratio of two wrapped numbers is two
inputs' worth of it.

**438 was still right to refuse the landing.** It measured −925, said the
pair had to fall together, and left a falsifier standing that passed on
this item's first run. The number it declined to book is the reason this
item knew where to look.

## The clamp erases the evidence, which is why this took three items

`run114_s_offers_are_the_original_s_own` compares the offer before it
reaches the clamp, which is why it is the assertion this item is booked on.
