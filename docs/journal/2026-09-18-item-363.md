# Item 363 — the golden record's first staged run

Capture lane, Opus 5, its own session. The rules track's opening move
(`docs/DECISIONS.md` entry 41 §5): stage a game with the AI off, get the three
answers, and close parked 341 on the way. The capture ledger is
`docs/RUNS.md`, run101–run105; the runbook is `docs/ORACLE.md`, "The click-free
lane needs a window"; the script is `tools/gamelog/golden/chapter1.cmd`.

## What took the time, and it was not the captures

The captures were cheap — half a minute each for a digest, under three minutes
for a 295-block window — and five of them plus two throwaway probes fit in
about forty minutes of wall clock. Two things ate the rest.

**The lane would not start, and the reason was disguised.** The first smoke
test died in 3.8 s: exit 5, zero frames, and a `rontrace.log` whose last
records were `INFO 176 0xa2c457` — the same teardown-fault address the lab's
autostart note already documents as an open failure. Everything pointed at the
tracer. `wine.log` said otherwise, a hundred lines under MoltenVK's banner:
`nodrv_CreateWindow`, "The graphics driver is missing." No GUI session, so
`winemac.drv` refuses a window, so the game dereferences one it never got.

The tempting readings were all wrong and all expensive: a broken prefix, a bad
`d3d11` override, a DXVK regression — and MoltenVK enumerates the GPU and
creates its `VkInstance` *before* the refusal, so every diagnostic in that
direction looks healthy. The fix was already in the tree: `viadriver.sh`
launches through LaunchServices, which is how the clicked lane keeps its TCC
grants and, incidentally, how it gets a window at all. `golden_capture.sh` is
the four-line shim that lets that launcher hand the job to a Python runner.

This is the thing most worth carrying forward, and it is why it went in the
runbook beside the launch line rather than only here: **the click-free lane's
independence is from TCC and the cursor, not from the desktop.** The next lane
to hit this will read `nodrv_CreateWindow` as a renderer failure and spend an
hour on the prefix.

**The first parse answer was vacuous and I nearly reported it.** `rondata
--gamelog` came back exit 0 with `units 0, buildings 0, leaders 0, cities 0`,
which reads as a parse that found nothing. It was a staging artefact: the
report's counts come from the `[Start Game]` block, and I had forced every
`[Start Game]` category to 0. run105 restages with the start set on and the
same command reads 52 units, 13 buildings, 4 leaders, 2 cities. A green exit
code over an empty input is exactly the shape the queue's rules keep warning
about, and the tell was that the numbers were *zero* rather than *wrong*.

## The three answers, and what would have falsified each

All three came back, and the detail is in `docs/RUNS.md`'s section. The shape
worth keeping is that each one needed a second run to be a measurement rather
than an observation:

- **Auto-engage survives AI-off.** Three squads engage on the frame they are
  born, all six by frame 620, three deaths by 899 — but that only means
  anything if `ai off` took, and a returning `INFO cmd` proves the parse
  accepted the line, not that the game changed. run104 is chapter one with
  that single line deleted: **899 of 901 frames differ, first at frame 2.**
- **Two launches give one draw stream.** 901 frames in common, 0 differing.
  The trap the brief named is real — `samegame.py` exits 0 on an empty
  intersection — so the dump comparison is reported as *21 blocks in common,
  0 differing*, and the 21 was re-derived rather than carried over.
- **A late-age dump parses.** 252 unit-frames compared with every order list
  and path stack read. The two things the harness says about a staged game are
  both item 364's brief, not bugs: a cheat-spawned unit comes back `unlinked`
  because it had no production order, and the **first order disagreement is
  the auto-engage itself** — `Kind { ours: 7, theirs: 10 }` on the three
  squads at frame 616. The chapter found its own first divergence unprompted.

One structural check fails and its premise is what is wrong: it asks whether
every starting citizen's gather target matches, and a windowed golden record
never opens on the citizens leaving the capital. That belongs to 364.

## Three things about the chapter itself

**`age` is not `library`.** `age who=0 8` lands `ages_get() 7` — the last age —
and leaves `epochs_get() 0`. The brief said `age` and `age` is what the script
uses, but a chapter that wants a genuinely late-age leader wants `library`,
which moves all four epochs and the age together.

**The coordinate argument did not calibrate the way the runbook says.** ORACLE
has `internal = arg × 768 + half a footprint` from a 2026-08-20 probe; here
`4,40` landed at tile ~4.6, 40.6 and `5,40` at ~7.1, 41.6. That was fine for
this chapter — the two groups landed 2.5 tiles apart on good ground, which is
all it needed — so I placed, read back, and moved on rather than re-deriving a
transform the chapter does not depend on. A chapter that needs an exact
distance will have to.

**A re-run at three times the detail is the same game.** run101 (20 blocks),
run103 (295 blocks) and run105 (295 plus the whole start dump) agree on all 901
frames. That is entry 41 §2 measured, and it is the load-bearing one: it is the
licence to throw a window away and re-take it.

## What I changed, and what I deliberately did not

Parked 341 closes: `--log-window`, `--detail`, `--cover`, `--cmd-file` and
`--ffwd-minute` reach `live_session.stage`, `unattended_capture.py` takes
`--map` so a golden run is one map rather than a pair, and every staged value
lands in the run's `receipt.json` so a capture is read back from the run and
not from the command that asked for it. Two guards were written by making them
fail first: a category the ini does not have is refused (the failure it catches
is a correctly-numbered window whose blocks come back empty because
`end:UNIT=3` was a typo), and a window past the `!quit` frame is refused.
`test_live_session` was not in the gate's offline suite and is now.

`rngcmp.py` grew absolute paths and an empty-intersection guard, with tests —
it had `samegame.py`'s bug and none of its warning, and a golden run lives
outside the archive it hardcoded. I did **not** touch `viadriver.sh`, which
would have been the tidier place for a Python entry point: it is the clicked
lane's load-bearing script and the shim costs four lines.

I did not chase the `add` coordinate transform, and I did not try to give the
diff harness a golden-record mode — both are 364's, and both would have been
scope I could not finish well.

## The held-out third map, after the first commit

The lane was behaving, so the second half of the brief ran as its own commit:
`docs/RUNS.md`, run106.

**Choosing the map was part of the item and nothing named one.** Entry 41 says
"the held-out third map" and no document picks it; `captures.txt` has only 14
and 18. I took **9, Himalayas**, on the argument that both scored maps are
water maps — lakes and islands — and the terrain neither tests is mountains.
The reasoning is in the run section so a later pass can disagree with the
choice rather than reconstruct it. The alternative I weighed and rejected was
picking something closer to the training pair to keep the number comparable:
that defeats the purpose, and a held-out set that resembles the training set
measures nothing.

**The number is 1 tick and 0 orders**, against floors of 1,851 and 1,772. I
ran the diff once and stopped, which is the whole discipline — there is a very
strong pull, looking at `who 1 o 0: Move { field: "x", ours: 42744, theirs:
39672 }` at frame **1**, to go and find out why, and that is exactly the pull
the held-out set exists to resist.

What I did do is read what the same single report already said, because that
costs nothing and burns nothing:

- The one structural check that **derives** rather than compares — every
  starting citizen's gather target, computed from the map without reading the
  log — is **exact for all ten citizens on both players**. That is the single
  most encouraging line in the report, and it would not have been visible from
  the headline number.
- The ticks figure is a **minimum over sixteen units**, and one unit at frame 2
  sets it. Individually, units track for 209, 237, 323, 344, 461 and 501
  frames. "1" and "nothing works on a new map" are not the same claim.
- The disagreements are 23,472 `gather` and 11,591 `move` — the two families
  eight months of items have worked on. A fit looks like this; a missing
  mechanic would look like a family with no rows at all.

**The number's provenance is weaker than the floors' and I have said so rather
than rounding it off.** This is the click-free lane's lobby, not run33/run39's
`-config check.ini`, and the window opens at frame 1 rather than matching the
score runs' shape. Comparable in kind, not in provenance. Making it strictly
comparable means a run33-shaped capture on map 9, which is a second capture and
a second decision, and not mine to take unasked.

**One lane trap, and it cost the capture once.** `--timeout` defaults to 180 s
and the give-up truncates rather than stops: the first attempt returned 593 of
1,900 blocks in a 62 MB file that reads as perfectly normal. The receipt caught
it — `success: false`, `TimeoutExpired`, settings restored anyway — which is
the tool working, but only because someone reads the receipt before the dump.
It is the same shape as `captures.txt`'s `poll_max` warning and it is now in
the run section next to the number it nearly spoiled.
