## The reading

`Ammo::check_hit@00678d90` is one call, `ObjectsData::find_unit@0065ca80(landing, SEARCH_ALL, −1, 0x180, 0, …)`.
The decompiler shows `vector_dist(unaff_EDI, unaff_ESI)`, so the listing settles it:

- `65cf16`..`65cf3c` measure the distance as `|x − px|`, `|y − py|` from the object's own XOR-ed point.
- `65cf44` (`jg` past the store) keeps a candidate when its distance `<= find_dist`. The circle walk's copy is at
  `65cd2f`.
- So a tie goes to the **last** candidate the walk meets.
- The walk is the lists, leader by leader in `o` order, while `total_units < circle_radius[(0x180 + 0x2ff) / 0x300]`,
  that is `circle_radius[1]`, 9. Otherwise it is the ring's cells in circle order, each cell's chain from its head.

This crate's `check_hit` scanned every unit by slot and kept the first on `<`. `air.rs`'s `nearest_of_own_type` and
`site_recruit.rs`'s `find_idle_citizen` already model the two walks and the last-of-tie rule, each for its own caller.
