# Item 4638 — Booking the client clock on a busy host

GPT-6, direct sequential work on `codex/nov24-handoff` from `dc14bfc51`.
The benchmark uses Great Lakes, the release client, all 600 frames and the
existing ceilings. Parity starts at 50 of 63 games and packet lines at
951 of 1,053. This item changes test admission and retry timing.

## Where the clock spent its time

On November 22–23 (Panama), 66 booking logs recorded nine steps and a
clock verdict. Their printed clock minutes sum to 157.4. The median was
0.2 minutes. The 21 cases over two minutes used 145.3 minutes. Of the
17 that escalated, the recorded budget timings total 130.6 seconds waiting
for the first lease, 2,710.3 seconds running under it, 4,120.4 seconds
waiting for the exclusive lease and 148.7 seconds running under it.
Failed first attempts logged 2,325 seconds explicitly waiting for the
one-minute host load to fall. Successful tests do not print the internal
wait to the archived libtest logs. These figures measure cost on main.

## Controls

Four competing four-slot jobs each run a real off-screen lobby client and
three bounded CPU workers. Each job finishes its work before requesting a new
lease. Sustained contention lasts up to three minutes. The short-burst
control stops submitting jobs eight seconds after the first clock violation,
while a separate one-slot lease stays alive for 150 seconds. Elapsed time
starts when the gate requests its first measurement and ends at the verdict.
Warm-up and waiting for the competing jobs to finish are outside that timer.

Main took 177.3 seconds on the first sustained run. An immediate second
measurement took 155.7 seconds with the old admission code. Its exclusive
lease waited 124.5 seconds. Between drain polls, new clients acquired slots
that a previous job had just released. With priority for the whole-host
lease, the immediate policy took 45.2 seconds. The same policy took
157.7 seconds on the short burst because it requested exclusive admission
while the unrelated lease still held one slot.

The exact interleaving is a test in `tools/explore/test_isolated_tests.py`.
Releasing a job and requesting a new ordinary lease before the next drain
poll admitted it on main. The booking drain now has priority at that point.
A cancelled drain releases priority. Partial reservations can still share
the host. Worktrees with older resource supervisors can still race the drain.

## Bounded retry

The booking probe gives a broken first session up to 30 seconds for the
one-minute load to fall to the core count. All attempts remeasure a break.
The lane, standalone clock and exclusive booking attempt keep the 600-second
bound. Every session checks all four ceilings and the slowest frame on its
own fresh trace.

| Case | Main elapsed | Bounded retry elapsed | Bounded retry p99, in order |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Quiet, one session | 7.99 s | 7.71 s | 13.17 ms |
| Four recurring jobs, trial 1 | 177.30 s | 73.20 s | 48.83, 34.56, 13.87 ms |
| Four recurring jobs, trial 2 | 215.38 s | 64.25 s | 35.41, 39.27, 14.22 ms |
| Short burst beside a 150-second idle lease | 44.77 s | 49.8 s | 39.74, 13.09 ms |

The bounded retry cut mean sustained-case elapsed time by 65% in these
two comparisons. On main both sustained cases passed on their second
session after the competing work finished. On the branch both passed on
their first exclusive session. Twenty-five newly submitted jobs completed
between the exclusive request and admission with the old supervisor in the
immediate-retry trial. Zero did in either bounded trial.

The short-burst main and bounded runs both passed before the idle lease
ended. The load average still read 23.37 on 16 cores when the bounded
retry started its second measurement. A burst outlasting the grace period
can still send the gate behind an already-running long lease.

The extra exclusive session costs CPU. On the timed main sustained run,
the clock commands used 10.80 s user and 8.79 s system with maximum RSS
296,665,088 bytes. The bounded runs used 16.52/11.11 and 16.35/10.88 s
user/system with maximum RSS 290,324,480 and 293,715,968 bytes. The quiet
pair used 4.00/2.45 → 3.98/2.44 s and 281,362,432 → 285,245,440 bytes.
These are clock-step observations on one host. The historical booking
gates have different mixes of work and do not provide a fleet-wide speedup.

## A slower client

The negative control delayed every twentieth off-screen frame by 35 ms
inside the measured frame span. Main and the bounded policy both exited
101 after two small-lease and two exclusive sessions. Main measured p99
51.41, 51.58, 51.58 and 51.41 ms. The bounded policy measured 51.50, 51.25,
51.39 and 51.33 ms. The restored source rebuilt the original client
byte for byte. Its first session passed in 7.92 s at p99 12.89 ms.

The 26 admission and gate tests and all four standalone clock unit tests
passed. The first paperwork guard caught a missing booking in the direct
handoff. The entry now uses the same parenthesized item number as its
predecessors. The corrected guard exited 0.
