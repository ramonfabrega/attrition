## The site first

`Guy::inc_time+0x1ed` is the attack-category arm of the wrap loop. An
attack whose clock reaches `end_time` asks for `set_anim(DEFAULT, 0, 0)`,
and the idle roll at `+0x97a` is the draw. So the draw means only "an
attack ran out". Its inputs are the slot's category, the step and
`end_time`. `set_anim`'s tail writes `end_time` from the packet's frame
count, or 3 for a slot the packet has no id for. Ours was `anim 13`,
`end_time 3`, so the question was never the clock. It was why a trireme
held a slot three frames long.

## The packet, then the listing

`unit_graphics.xml`'s Trireme packet names `CHAR_ATTACK1` and
`CHAR_ATTACK2`, both the one `Trireme Attack1` file, and no
`CHAR_ATTACK3`. So this crate's 3 was right for the slot it played, and
the question was whether the original ever plays that slot. The attack
arm's listing answers it in four instructions. After the roll it calls
`get_animobj(slot)`, and a null result `cmove`s `0xc`, `CHAR_ATTACK2`,
into the slot. The idle arm and the walk arm had their fallbacks in this
crate already. The attack arm was the one without.

The brief's lead was `starttime / 67` biting a short attack. It did not
apply: the length is the `.bha`'s frame count, not an event time, and the
slot had no animation to measure.

What would have killed the reading, written before the build: the 742
round off a full swing's release frame (it is 737 + 5, `400 / 67`), a
draw left on 739, or a record moved under the old word. None of the three
happened.

plus `1/0`'s explore-order `facing` on 847, which is the formation mirror
`OrderMismatch::scores` already declares non-scoring (parked 275), the
same row Great Lakes' `1/0` carries on 8002. The widening now pins it by
