# 495: the rolled arrow comes down, and three other things had to come with it

**Booked** as the rules headline: golden chapter two's word at 725, and
`damage 1/7` on 686 (ours 0, theirs 19) is §42.5's rolled arrow, which this
crate flew to its cap and dropped. Item 510 had measured the payoff from
the far end: forcing the bowmen's facing moved the word to 743. It was booked
as the first hard-constraint item, a float parabola against a float
surface. The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` §46.

**The word moved 725 → 762** (+37, past 510's 743). Chapter one (626),
Great Lakes (10834) and East Indies (9711) are unchanged.

## What would have killed the reading

Written before the build: *if the arrow lands and `damage 1/7` still parts
on 686, the reading was incomplete.* It was incomplete. The arrow landed on
the original's step and dealt 19. `damage_frac` stood at 0 against 5, and
the bowmen still took `1/7` on 696. The frame was right, and so was the
booked mechanism, but it was the first link of four.

## The representation came first, and it was simpler than the brief feared

The brief's premise, from §42.5, was that `find_data_z` reads "a float
height surface this crate does not carry … a different quantity" from
`master_land_heights`. It is the same array: `TerrainOut` is `TerrainData`
behind a 0x40-byte prefix, so `this+0x4a4` is `master_land_heights.list`.
`tools/ghidra/README.md` has recorded that trap since the road search fell
into it. Grepping the repo for `0x4a4` found that in one step.

The listing answered the rest in about ten minutes. The arc and the
surface are scalar SSE, so every operation is correctly rounded and the
whole thing is a pure function of its operands' bits. `GRAV_Z` is written
once before the first frame. `find_data_z` is called with 0 as its fourth
argument, so the water arm never runs. `v1z` is computed from integers.
The only operand that is not exactly recoverable is the grid itself. The
dump prints `%f`, and below 16 a single's spacing is finer than a
millionth. On Great Lakes 2,058 of 58,081 corners are ambiguous, all under
16, and none is within 17 tiles of the fight. I stopped and asked, as the
brief said to. The commander approved "nearest single, count every read of
one, and pin the count per window". The guard is a test-only `Drop` on
`Built`, keyed by the test's thread name, which defaults to zero. The
whole suite passes with the table empty.

## Four things, each read off the frame the last left

1. **The arc.** [`combat::arc_z`] against [`World::data_z`], both in
   `Single`. The surface is diff-backed on every figure run112 carries:
   5,310 figure-blocks, and a figure's `z` *is* `find_data_z`.
2. **The pool's slot order.** With the arc in, `damage_frac` was 10
   against 5, dealt by `0/7` and not `0/8`. Both shots fire on 677 and are
   due on 683, and whichever is stepped first kills `1/8`. The original
   walks its pool by slot. This crate `swap_remove`d, which §24.4 had
   named as a residue "with a consequence" three days earlier.
3. **The height bonus.** With the right bowman it came to 57 against 58.
   Step 23 compares the two objects' `z`, and this crate passed 0 for
   both. The sweep showed that a unit's `z` and a building's are
   `find_tcoord_z`, i.e. `tile_z`, so the bonus is now live.
4. **`compare_target`'s bearing.** With `damage 1/7` agreeing to the
   sixteenth, the bowmen *still* took `1/7`. So 510's middle link, "a
   wounded `1/7` outranks `1/6` on §33's damage weight", was a reading,
   and it was wrong. The decompiler prints the ranking's `get_damage` call
   as `find_angle(0, 0)`, and §18.1 and §33.2 both took that on trust.
   `find_angle` takes its pair in `ecx`/`edx`, and the listing loads the
   target's position less the attacker's. With the real bearing the
   captain scores `1/6` at 27,307 against `1/7` at 24,967.

The chain reads as a lesson. The named mechanism was right, and it was
still one link of four; each of the other three was found by printing the
value that stood on the frame, not by reading ahead. The fourth overturns
a decompiler reading that two sections had carried as fact. `CLAUDE.md`
already says that when the decompiler prints a local that cannot be
right, the listing settles it. `find_angle(0, 0)` *could* have been right,
and that is why nobody checked it.

## The mistake, so the next worker does not repeat it

I wrote a software single from scratch and saved it over
`crates/sim/src/single.rs`, which already existed (the aircraft bank's) and
had every operation I needed. I had grepped for `F32` and `f32_` and not
for the *type*. The Write tool did not refuse; it said "updated". I caught
it only because `lib.rs` already declared `pub mod single;`, and restored
the file from git before anything was built on it. The lesson is in
§46.8: grep for the type, not for the name the brief used.

## Draft for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (for the commander to book: an amendment to 19 and 31)

> **Amendment to 19, 2026-09-22 (item 495).** The second mid-frame float
> the port reproduces is a rolled shot's arc and the ground under it:
> `Ammo::inc_time`'s rolling arm and `TerrainOut::find_data_z`, scalar SSE
> throughout. They are reproduced in `sim::single::Single`, the aircraft
> bank's software single, operation for operation. `GRAV_Z` is a pinned
> bit pattern (`0xc127cccd`), because its one writer runs before the first
> frame. Entry 19's argument holds unchanged: every operation is correctly
> rounded, so the original is deterministic here and the port is exact.
>
> **Amendment to 31, same day.** The height grid stays in the millionths
> the dump prints, and a single is recovered from each corner as the one
> single whose six-decimal print it is. Where two to four share a print
> (2,058 of Great Lakes' 58,081 corners, every one under 16 in magnitude)
> the nearest is taken, a load-time choice at most a few ulp off. That is
> not arithmetic in a frame. A terraformed corner is no longer known to be
> the original's single, for the reason 31 already gives. Every read of
> either kind is counted (`Sim::ground_inexact`) and held to a per-test
> pin of zero, so the day a read lands on one fails a test by name. The
> upgrade 31 anticipated, carrying the grid as singles and the terraform
> in `Single`, stays local to `crate::terrain` and is owed when that
> count first moves.
