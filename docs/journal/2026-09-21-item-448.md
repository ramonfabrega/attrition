# Item 448 — the widening the word owed, and the field a residue was hiding

*2026-09-21, Opus 5, `worktree-att-448`. No capture: run100 already covered
the frame. No score moved — this is the widening 442's word owed
(`docs/DECISIONS.md` 43). `docs/AI.md` §54, a new test in
`diff::harness`, and `WIDENINGS`' Great Lakes row repointed from `None,
448` to the test that landed.*

## What I did before writing any code

Grepped the disk first, as the rule says. `tools/gamelog/captures.txt`'s
run100 stanza: `[9340, 10899]`, 1,560 blocks, no gap, `UNITS=3, BUILDS=7,
CITIES=5, GUYS=4, DEATHS=1, LEADERS=1` — the word's own block was already
on the disk, and a `GUYS=4` capture prints the animation clock, which is
where the pin said the word was. **No capture booked**; I told the
commander so when the lane came free mid-item.

Then I wrote the falsifiers down. The pin said 10161 is
`Guy::set_anim+0x97a`, seven draws against eight, `Guy::inc_time+0x271`
here against `Unit::move_step+0x4e2` there. So:

- if the guy clock agrees on the word's block, the draw-stream reading is
  wrong and the word is somewhere else in the record;
- if more than one unit parts, "an animation clocked off the wrong caller"
  is too small an account;
- if the queue, city or build records move on the frame, §47's purchase
  shape has come back and the animation is downstream.

None of the three fired. The clock parts on exactly one unit and nothing
else on the block moves at all.

## The two rows that only a widening could find

The interesting part of this item is not the answer; it is that **two of
the fifteen rows were invisible to the harness as it stood**, and each was
invisible for a different structural reason.

- **`half_step`** — `unit_masks & 0x100000`, dumped on every block of
  every capture. `compare` reads it inside the collision block, which is
  gated on the two positions already agreeing. On the word the positions
  part, so the gate closed on the exact frame the field mattered. The gate
  is right for counting residue and blind at a parting, which is the
  general shape: **a green frame is not a green input**.
- **`order:coll`** — `MoveOrder::coll_x`/`coll_y`, the point the last
  collision refused, and the single most direct statement in the record of
  what happened. It *is* compared. But my first probe keyed order rows on
  `(who, o)` the way `unit.rs`'s `rows()` helper does, and `1/38` has
  carried a `GROUPORDER` `id` residue since block 9340 — so the coll pair
  was filed as "this unit's order already parted" and never printed. I
  found it only because I went to the dump to check what our `coll` held
  and saw `(42774, 22584)` sitting in the original's record.

The second one is the better lesson. "Diff the whole record" has been in
`CLAUDE.md` since a dumped record went nine tenths uncompared for a month,
and this is the same failure one level down: **a key that collapses a
record's fields hides all but the first of them, for the rest of the run.**
The landed test keys every order row by its own field name.

## The answer

`1/38` is walking an `ATTACK_TO` leg. On 10161 both sides hold the same
waypoint, `(42774, 22584)`. The original probes it, refuses it, writes the
point into `coll_x/coll_y`, stamps `collide_guy 0`, consumes the waypoint
without a step and rolls the idle. This crate steps onto it.

Every field of `docs/COLLISION.md` §5.4's snap arm is in the dump, and they
are all there: `collide_o`/`collide_who` back at −1, `collide` and
`collide_frame` untouched, `dest` 1 → 0, `length` 2 → 1, `last_speed` 0,
`cur_anim` 0 with `end_time` 33. `sim::anim::SITE_SNAP_BLOCKED` is
`move_step+0x4e2`. The record and the draw stream say the same thing, and
it is the signature item 360 landed for Great Lakes 9134 — the second
capture ever to reach that arm.

## What I did not do

Name the collider. `collide_o` and `collide_who` are cleared two
instructions after the probe returns, so the dump **cannot** say which unit
refused the step; four group-mates stand within half a tile and both §4.3's
group-mate soft arm and the corner rule fit. I wrote the candidates and the
capture that would separate them into §54.4 and stopped there. The brief
said the widening is the deliverable and that a mechanism named one fetch
early is the failure this queue keeps booking; the `half_step` row is
exactly the bait — ours 1, theirs 0, and it reads like "we called it soft,
they called it hard". It may be. It is also true 754 frames earlier on
`1/31` with no consequence whatever, which is in the same test's output,
and that is the sentence I would have skipped if I had booked the
mechanism.

## Cost

blocks and 1.98 M rows cost 6.5 s in release. The disk work — slicing
`1/38`'s record out of a 770 MB capture — was 3 s a pass with a streaming
reader. No capture, no reading, no subagent.
