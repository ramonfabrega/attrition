# Item 518 — the cap's third writer

*2026-09-22, lane att-518, Opus 5.5. Great Lakes' long word **10834 →
11185**, 351 frames. One capture, run120.*

## What was booked, and what it turned out to be

515 left `1/28` one world unit out on block 10242 and booked the frame as
an order-stack item: `1/40` holding an `ATTACK` at the head of its stack
where the original holds the `GROUP_MOVE` it is dumped with, so it never
reaches `do_group_move` and never reports a 25. The brief named no
mechanism and said so.

The frame was right for the tenth time and the framing was wrong. Reading
the dump's `1/40` record on 10242 by hand, the stack prints `type 19` and
then `type 10`, and `OrderList::log_data` writes newest first, so the
**last** block — the `ATTACK` — is the current order. The original's
slow squad is fighting with its group move underneath, exactly as ours is,
and `compare_orders` was right to be quiet. I printed both sides before
trusting it, as the brief asked, and they agree.

So nobody reports on either side, and yet the original's cap drops. That
turned the question from "which member" into "which writer", and the
export has a short list of writers of `GroupData +0x40`.

## The order the evidence came in

**The step sizes first**, from the dump's own position deltas: `1/28`
steps 26 on blocks 10237..10241 and 25 from 10242. Our cap, through a
read-only accessor added to the debug line, is `(26, 26)` from 10236 to
10246 and never moves.

**Then who ungroups**, by a temporary print on every ungroup site: `1/27`,
blocked in formation, on frame 10240 — before `1/28` runs, so the leader
never reports again either. The trace's own draw on that frame
(`Unit::move_step+0x823`) is the same blocked stand.

**Then the writers, read before the run and written down with what would
kill each**: `push_group`'s `equals_group` normalizing the player's last
slot; `find_nearby_target:499`; `repath` → `kill_group_move` (already dead
by the dump — it would have killed `1/40`'s head); a membership change (no
`group` field moves on any unit in 10231..10249); none of these.
`near_o` could not decide the search reading, because it is written only on
a nearer candidate, which is COMBAT §44.2.1's shape exactly.

**Then run120**: the same game with per-frame coverage over 10236..10244.
About four minutes end to end, all five checks green. Not one
of the named writers is entered on 10240 or 10241, and `Groups::process` is
entered on every frame. It was in GROUPS §3.3 all along, described as a
per-frame reset of "one slot of each in-use leader", and §18 never counted
it among the cap's writers.

The cursor is `f mod 64`, and 10241 is slot 1's frame: group 65.

## The half that was not in the brief

A reset keyed on a slot number needs the slot numbered, and this crate had
never numbered one: its seam table said "one group per army, never
recycled". So the item grew a small allocator, `get_open_slot`'s first rule
with `last_group` and the equality reuse, and an army takes its slot the
way `Army::add_unit` pushes its first squad.

A replay of that allocator in Python over this crate's own push sequence
gave army 64, raid 65 and scout 67 over run100's window, which are the
dump's. The early game does not match: the scout cycles 65/64/66 in run69
on pushes this reading calls equal. That is written down as not
established rather than chased, because a one-unit group's reset cannot
change a cap. `UNITDATA`'s `group` field, printed on every unit and read by
nothing, became a widening row, and it parts on no unit in 1,509 blocks.

## One guard narrowed, with the measurement

`the_east_indies_ladder_is_pinned` asserts that an object number `extra`
on both rungs is the same type, because the rungs are one simulation seen
1,088 frames apart. It went red on `1/62`: a Transport Barge on rung C and
a Citizen on rung B. Watching `1/62` along rung B answered it. Both rungs
hold the same barge, rung B then walks on, the barge dies on 15466, and a
new Citizen takes the number on 15763, in a new slot. A barge's number is
not an identity, so barges are excluded from the shared set, with that
measurement in the comment. Seven shared numbers remain, all agreeing,
above the guard's floor of six.
