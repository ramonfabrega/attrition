## The booked capture was answered by a grep

§27.6 named a `LEADERS=9` window as the one question here a reading cannot
answer, and the commander reserved run113 for it. It was not needed:
**run112's own dump already carries `leader_flags`**, printed immediately
*before* each `BEGIN LEADERDATA` rather than inside it, which is why my

capture already on disk, and the screen never used.

That is the grep-before-booking rule catching a capture the commander and I
had both agreed on.

## `leader_flags & 4` is not what the documents say it is

Measured on three captures — run112, run105 and the AI-on control run104 —
identical in all three, so it is a lobby property:

- who=0, the **human**: `0x00000007`. `& 4` **set**.
- who=1, the **computer**: `0x03000013`. `& 4` **clear**.

`docs/INPUT.md` §11.4 reads `Unit::think`'s `(leader_flags & 4) != 0` as
the computer-leader gate, and run104's note says who=1 "still carries
`leader_flags` bit 2 at frame 899". The human carries it and the computer
does not. I have not established what the bit *is*; §28.1 says so and flags
every claim resting on the old reading as owed a re-check. That is a
correction to two documents I did not write and it came out of a table I
built to answer something else.

## The listing, and the artefact it caught

The decompiler prints the branch's test as
`*(byte *)(leaders.list + who) & 2` — a byte index into an array whose
element is nearly 29,000 bytes. It cannot be right, and `CLAUDE.md` says
the listing settles that in a minute. It did:

```
movzbl 0x9(%ebx), %eax       ; who
imull  $0x6eec, %eax, %eax   ; who * stride
testb  $0x2, 0xe3a390(%eax)
cmovel %edi, %esi
```

Proper indexing. The stride is 28,396 where the type record gives
`LeaderData` 28,388 — eight bytes I cannot account for, recorded rather
than smoothed.

So who=1's low byte is 19, bit 1 is set, the `cmovel` does not fire, and
**`think_attack` passes 0**: the hoplite captain takes the zero path.
Settled, by dump and listing together, with no capture and no inference.

## And it closed nothing

Both frames sit on neither of the zero path's two grids, so both take the
same narrow arm, `unit_respond_range * 0x180`. The measured bracket — the
search reaching its chosen target at 1340 units on 634 and not reaching
the bowmen at 1392 on the birth frame — admits **no integer** there: 3
gives 1152, 4 gives 1536. The only multiplier that fits is `* 0xc0`, at
`unit_respond_range` 7, and that is the arm **neither path applies to this
unit**.

Resolving the branch made the contradiction sharper instead of closing it.

## Why I stopped

The commander's brief said: if the flag says the captain takes the other
branch, the arithmetic is back to a contradiction, and that is the result —
say so and stop rather than fitting a third account. Two accounts have
already died here, each one plausible when written and each refuted by

itself, which is now very tight: **a melee searcher on the zero path
behaves as though its radius were `unit_respond_range * 0xc0` with
`unit_respond_range` 7.**

I also did not implement the branch. The commander's "then implement it
with `unit_respond_range` 7" was conditional on the flag saying the −1
path; it says the zero path, where 7 gives 14 tiles and moves 616 the wrong
way. Implementing it would have been fitting the third account with code.
