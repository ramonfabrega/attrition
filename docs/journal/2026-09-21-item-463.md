## The decompile, once the dump had named the question

`Unit::do_attack@005f1b80` tests the target object's `flags & 1` in
exactly two places, and **both sit under `*(int *)(ptype + 0x1e8) == 0`**
— `attack`, by the type record. A unit that can attack falls through both
to `LAB_005f2224`'s unconditional `fight(...)` and never asks. The
question is `Unit::fight@005fd4d0:196`'s, and `:102`'s recharging arm —
already written down in `docs/ORDERS.md` §7.11's four-row table, from
item 336 — returns before it.

`crates/sim` had asked it first. `Sim::do_attack` ran `valid_target` above
the reload gate, so six orders died two to eight frames early.

## The second half, which the first half exposed

With the reorder in, five of the six were right and `1/28` kept its order
for ever. `Sim::forget` — this crate's own sweep, not the original's —
cleared the attacker's `mandatory` along with its target. `mandatory` is
the **order's** field (`TargetOrder +0x1c`), and run100 prints it as 1 two
blocks after the building left the dump. With it cleared, a HOLD_FIRE
raider returned at `do_attack`'s stance arm and never reached the kill at
all.

## The field that was not in the comparison

`recharging` is dumped on every `UNITDATA` block of every capture and
`rondata::diff` compared none of it. That is why two items read this as
pathing: the one field that would have said "these units are reloading"
was invisible. It is in the widening now, and it **agrees on every AI unit
of all 909 blocks**, which is what turns the four-clock table above from
an observation into a measurement.

The working agreement's "diff the whole record" has a corollary this item
paid for: a widening that omits a dumped field cannot be read as saying
the record agrees. The `UNITDATA` block still has some thirty fields
nothing compares — `damage_frame`/`damage_o`/`damage_who`, `group`,
`waiting`, `num_queued`, `hold_frames`, `los_x`/`los_y` — and each is a
question the next item cannot see.
