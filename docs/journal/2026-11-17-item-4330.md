## The first act: the grab's match, by the profile's own matrices

The raw grab's `iCCP` profile `Mi monitor` has the primaries rXYZ (0.4964, 0.2287, 0.0067), gXYZ (0.3021, 0.6947,

γ 1.96100. Matching every pixel of the raw grab through these matrices into the system's `sRGB Profile.icc` (its
1,024-entry curve inverted) gives the following against `sips -m`'s output, by the per-pixel maximum channel difference.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pixels (of 2,135,040) | 1,718,847 | 396,544 | 12,121 | 7,255 | 264 | 9 |

On 200,000 deep-cyan pixels the mean difference is (+0.03, +0.07, −0.01). **ColorSync's match is the profile's.**

|---|---|---|---|

| ours, tile (200, 181) | (56, 134, 148) | (64.6, 120.5, 136.0) | (56.4, 134.5, 148.0) |

One display count at this colour moves sRGB by (+1.77, −0.03, 0) in red, (−0.57, +1.00, −0.04) in green and (+0.07,
+0.01, +1.01) in blue. Rounding adds at most about ±0.9 a pixel and averages to zero over 649 pixels. The tile is far from
clipping, since red is 56 to 71 and not near 0. The two sides lie 9 display counts apart in red and 8 in blue, so the
gap was already in the frame before the window server matched it. A different forward curve for the source does not
give the shape either. Pure γ 2.2 gives (−3.8, +1.5, +1.0) on ours, γ 2.4 gives (−10.8, −6.5, −5.8), and γ 1.8 gives
(+16.0, +18.1, +17.2). Each of these moves green with red, and the residue leaves green alone.
