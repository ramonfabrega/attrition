# Item 466 — 635's six `Target` rows in the new window

**Booked on**: the frame and the six rows (`docs/DECISIONS.md` 42). No
mechanism in the title, and the one the brief explicitly ruled out — the
cell's `down` chain, which item 462 fixed — is not the cause.

**Landed**: `docs/COMBAT.md` §33. Word held at **637**. **Three of the
six rows closed**, not six: the bowmen's. The hoplites' three are
established to the same standard and withdrawn from the tree — the
commander's call, and the right one. Parting stays **635**. Tree green.

## How it went

The brief said re-measure first, so the first hour was the dump and not
the decompiler. Three probes, none of which survived contact:

1. **`near_o` looked like the answer and was not.** At 635 the dump's
   bowman captain reads `near_o 6 / near_who 1` — `1/6`, exactly what this
   crate picked — while its `ATTACKORDER` reads `ox 8`. For twenty minutes
   that looked like a mechanism between the search and the order. It is
   not: §30 had already written down that `near_o` is the *nearest*
   acceptable candidate and not the winner, and both captains' `near_o`
   is the nearest on either reading. The field is consistent with every
   hypothesis and therefore discriminates none of them.
2. **The fog was the pretty story.** who=1's hoplite has `mylos 6` → a
   three-cell disc that does not reach the bowmen, and its two accepted
   slingers sit in one fog cell three away. It fit perfectly, and it is
   wrong: `0/10` fires at 630, gains `visible 2`, and
   `Unit::update_local_seen` then writes its own **3×3** into who=1's
   `seen` plane — `CIRCLE_RADIUS` 1 and `vector_dist(1,1) = 1` — lighting
   the bowmen's half-cell five frames before the search. Killed by
   reading `circle_init` rather than by running anything.
3. **`tregion` was the second pretty story**, and `check_target`'s "a
   target in another region must be `is_in_range`" is a real arm. All
   nine figures are in region **0**. One probe, one line of output.

What finally worked was the rule about probing *inside* the decision: a
temporary `eprintln!` in `find_nearby_target` printing every candidate's
`attack_dist`, `targeted`, `in_range`, `value` and `score` for both
captains on the frame. That output is the whole item — it showed the
bowmen's tie lost by exactly the `(targeted + 8) × 0x30` term, and the
hoplites' bowman/slinger ordering inverted by a factor the damage weight
could supply and nothing else could.

Two causes, one per squad, which is why no single mechanism had ever
fitted all six rows. The executable has four writers of `ObjectData
+0x3d` and `add_attack_order` is not one of them; and `compare_target`'s
fourth argument makes a computer leader **divide** by the damage a human
multiplies by, which §12.3 and §18 had both recorded and left unmodelled.

## The part that was wrong, and the commander caught it — twice

**First**: the `ai` arm gives who=1's hoplites the dump's own target and
costs a strike downstream. `1/8` never closes, its `visible` bit never
arrives, and `chapter_two_s_visible_byte_is_the_dump_s_on_every_unit_frame`
goes red. I re-pinned that test — the shape assertion, the nine frames and
the `exact` count — and would have reported a green gate with a weakened
guard inside it. The commander stopped it mid-flight: a pinned constant
moves when a word *moves*; a floor that falls is the commander's to book.

Worth writing down because the failure mode was not laziness — every
re-pinned number was honestly measured. It was that a test which mixes a
*rule* (which bit arrives) with a *clock* (on which frame) offers a
tempting reading in which the rule was never broken, only the clock, and
that reading is available for any regression whatsoever.

**Second**: I then landed the red, having been told to, and offered the
split as an option. The commander took the option and gave the reason I
had not weighed — lane 464 is in flight on the same base, and a red tree
it inherits makes its own breakage unfalsifiable. That is the
oracle-destroying trap in miniature, and it is worth more than three rows
that move no headline. The word is 637 under either half.

So what shipped is §33.1 alone, and §33.2 stays in the document whole,
marked **established, not landed**, with both falsifiers that survived.

## The reading I would have lost by shipping the red

Asked to write down *why* §33.2 cannot land alone, the answer turned out
to be worth more than the fix: **the assertion it would break has been
passing for the wrong reason.** Without §33.2, `1/8` strikes — but only
because it chases the wrong target, and only because that wrong target
hands it a slot of its own. Three hoplites plan **two** slots between
them; given the dump's own target they plan **one**. So the green rests
on a wrong two-slot assignment, `find_open_slots` was already collapsing
two chasers of three before this item, and the correct ranking merely
reveals it.

A pinned assertion that passes for the wrong reason is worse than one
that fails, because nothing in the tree says so. §33.4 is the only record
that it does, and it exists because the commander asked for the reasoning
rather than the result.

## The split, measured

Asked whether the two halves can land separately, the answer is a table
rather than an opinion — each disabled in turn:

| landed | 635's rows | `chapter_two_s_visible_byte` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the `targeted` decay alone | bowmen closed, hoplites remain | green |
| the `ai` arm alone | hoplites closed, bowmen remain | red |
| both | 635 clean | red |

They split by squad exactly. The decay is free; the `ai` arm carries the
whole cost. That table is what made the commander's decision a decision
rather than a guess, and it cost two three-second runs.
