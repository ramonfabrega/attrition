## The clock's reader, found by what the bar prints

The photographs' line reads `Normal: 00:03:38`, bottom left, over the button row. The calls to `say_timer` are
few, and one is `IFaceMainBase::draw_button_row_area@0080f7b0`. That function writes the speed's name and `: `, then
`say_timer`. The decompile hides `say_timer`'s argument, so the listing was read. At `0080f92d` it loads
`GameAccessConst::gamec` (`0xc061e8`) and passes `+0x560` in `edx`. By the type record that field is `Game::tick`.
`say_timer@007f1b00`'s listing divides by 3600 (`0x91a2b3c5`) and by 60 (`0x88888889`). It writes hours, minutes
and seconds through `say_timer_num@007f1a70`, which pushes a `0` first when the number is under 10.

`tick` has three writers: `Game::do_frame@00591ef0:297` (`if (frame % 15 == 0) tick++`, after the frame count's own
step), `Setup::build_game` and `Game::clear` (both 0). So `tick = frame / 15`.

## A zero and a negative rate

`IFaceResources::draw_resource@00818840`, lines 192–211: for a zero `income` it writes
`int_str_array +0x1f90`, which is `internal_strings.xml` entry 404 at stride 0x14 (`console.py`'s reader: 403
`BUILDTYPE`, **404 `0`**, 405 `down`). Any other `income` is written as `(v + (v >> 31 & 0xf)) >> 4` of
`v = income + 8`. The `+` is put in front only when the string differs from entry 404. So:
- a rate that rounds to zero reads `0`;
- a rising one reads `+40`;
- a falling one reads `+-2`.

run1570's photograph shows `20240/+10` and `20000/0`. The bar wrote `+0` before this item.

`a_rate_is_written_as_the_original_writes_it` checks `rate_text(shown_rate(i))` against that arithmetic, written
again for every `income` from −400 to 400. In the bar's diff, a rate token is now read back only in the
original's writing. The old writing parts on exactly the 5,534 zero rates in the records (49,276 of 54,810). No
record holds a negative `income`, so the `+-n` arm rests on the reading.

## The ring

`UnitOut::draw_underlays@00614100` marks a unit that `GroupData::member` finds in the console's selection. It calls
`draw_unit_circles@00606a10` with radius `+0x234 × unit_block_radius` in the owner's `TeamColor::get_iface`, and
that function draws one circle at each figure. A unit type's `+0x234` is `CIRCLE_RADIUS`
(`UnitType::init@0061ab50:655`; `Profile::circle_radius`). `client::hud::rings` builds those circles from the scene,
and `paint` projects them through `render::camera::world_to_screen` and draws two-pixel lines (`Painter::line`) in
`team_unit`'s colour. The Scout's radius is 48.

A building's marker is ours: the footprint's rectangle. The original's is `draw_funky_rectangle_circle` at
`BuildData::max_range` (vtable `+0x130`), a rounded outline, as run1570's photograph shows about Napata. It is not
ported. `the_selection_s_rings` checks every point's distance from its figure (±1) and the city's corners.
