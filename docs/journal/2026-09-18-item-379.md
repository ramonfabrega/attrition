# Item 379 — the golden record's draw is named, modelled, and still not spent

Rules track, Opus 5, its own worktree. The brief: `Guy::set_anim@005da300+0xf2f`,
the one draw standing at golden 617. **The word did not move.** What moved
instead is what anybody looking at 617 next will see, and it is worth the
item: the mechanism is modelled, the draw has a name, and the residue's real
cause is measured rather than guessed.

## The title named the draw and mis-named the caller

Twenty minutes in, the re-measure said what the standing warning said it
would. The draw at 617 is real, the address is right, and the caller is not
the swing:

```
f617  1  Guy::set_anim+0xf2f < Guy::move+0x166 < Guy::process+0x13
```

`Unit::fight` never spends this draw. Not once in the golden record's 901
frames, and not once in run53's 24,000: every one of the 13 golden and 214
run53 attack rolls comes from `Guy::move` or `Guy::inc_time`.

The listing says why in nine instructions. At `005da38a`, ahead of every
other arm, `Guy::set_anim` tests an attack request against the guy's own
`des != pos || des_angle != angle` and, if either holds, stores the request
in `GuyData +0x9e` and **returns without drawing**. Two statements before
it asks, `Unit::fight` has written the attack angle into every guy's
`des_angle` — so the swing frame always defers, by construction. `Guy::move`
pays the debt on the frame the angle lands, and *that* is the roll.

An attack asked of a guy already swinging goes to `+0xa0` instead, for
`Guy::inc_time` to pay when the animation running now runs out. Both
returns are silent, which is why modelling them is not optional: leaving
either out turns a no-draw into a draw.

`docs/ANIM.md` §6.2 has the whole of it — the four arms of `fight` (only one
carries the third argument, so only one can ever roll), the three payment
sites, and `Unit::set_anim`'s own first statement, which wipes the debt of
any guy whose unit asks for something else while guy 0 is not attacking.
That last one is the mechanism of the golden frame: on 616 all three of
who=1's hoplites swing and two of them walk, so the walk cancels their
debt; only `1/6`, which stands still, still owes the swing at 617.

## Four sites the trace could not name, and one it named wrongly

`Guy::set_anim+0xf2f` printed as a bare `5db22f` — a comparison that cannot
fail. Four rows in `rondata::trace::SITES` fix it (`Guy::move+0x166`,
`Guy::move+0xe3`, `Guy::inc_time+0x271`, `Guy::inc_time+0x357`).

A fifth row fixes something that was already wrong and invisible:
`Guy::inc_time`'s wrap has **two** call sites, not one. `+0x271` is the
shared tail that every looping restart and every queued attack goes through;
`+0x1ed` is the `set_anim(CHAR_DEFAULT, 0, 0)` an attack running out takes
on its way to the queue, and its third argument is zero, so the variant
bands do not apply. The crate marked both `SITE_WRAP`. Nothing had ever
reached the second, because until this item no unit in this simulation had
ever played an attack animation.

## The residue at 617 is the seating seam, and the seating is now exact

With the mechanism in, 617 was still one draw short — and the reason was not
animation at all. The debug said who=1's three hoplites all walked on 617
where the original's `1/6` stands still. They walked because this crate had
all three of them on **one point**: item 364's parked seating seam
(`docs/INPUT.md` §11.5).

`Objects::init_unit@0065e0c0` seats each member past the first with
`find_nearby_spot` over the ring `[size·0x30, size·0x60+0xc0]` around the
captain. The search takes no draw, which is why the stream never knew and
364 could leave it — but the *positions* knew, and so did the **second
`add`'s own spot**: with the first squad stacked the near ground stays free
and this crate put who=1's captain at `(1080, 7800)` where the original is
pushed out to `(1368, 7992)`. Eighteen tiles.

Twelve lines in `Sim::init_unit` reproduce all six of the golden dump's
coordinates exactly —

```
who 0   (888, 7800)   (1032, 7800)   (936, 7944)
who 1  (1368, 7992)   (1512, 7992)  (1416, 8136)
```

— and `chapter_one_s_two_squads_are_seated_where_the_dump_says` pins them.
Made to fail first: with the seating off it prints the stacked column and
the second captain 288 units short.

## What 617 is now, and why that is a better place to leave it

Seated, the sim's squads are where the original's are and the residue at 617
changes shape: **ours 6 draws, theirs 8**, missing `Unit::fight+0x9b0` as
well as the roll. The original's who=1 squad is in `do_attack` on 617 and
this crate's is not — it engages at 626. That is a combat item, not an
animation one, and it is the next rules brief.

It would have been possible to leave the squads stacked, keep the residue at
one draw, and hand the next session a smaller-looking number. Stacked units
engage early because they are standing on top of the enemy; the residue
would have been an artefact of a position this crate is known to get wrong.
Six coordinates that match the dump exactly are worth more than a
one-draw-instead-of-two residue built on a wrong one.

## The numbers

- **Golden chapter one: 617, value diff at 618.** Unmoved, on the same basis
  as 364 — the borrowed setup, `frame_seeds`/`frame_guys` refused.
- **Both long-capture words unmoved**: Great Lakes 9182, East Indies 9711.
  Every attack roll on Great Lakes is past its word — the first is at 9416,
  234 frames after it — so no roll this item adds can reach either.
- **Great Lakes' endpoint at 24001 moves, 50 → 66 off and 9 → 5 unlinked.**
  Every fighting figure's animation clock changes from its first engagement,
  and 14,800 frames past the word that re-deals the late game. East Indies'
  endpoint and both ladder rungs are unchanged. Re-pinned in `ENDPOINTS`
  naming this item; the queue's `Endpoint` line is the commander's to move.
  It is a reshuffle and not a regression in any sense a diff can test — but
  it is sixteen positions, and it is the honest price of a mechanism the
  listing owns.
