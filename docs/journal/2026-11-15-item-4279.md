**What was ruled out before the cause, each by a render scored on the two faces:**

**The cause.** A debug render (`dbg_spec`, the mapped shader writing the map's blue, its gloss and N·H) showed the
whole right face was not drawn by the mapped shader. Two more (`dbg_fsm`, `dbg_snow`) showed the snowcap underlay
covering it, nearly opaque (`dbg-which.png`). `t_mountainpeak`'s alpha is four soft radial splats, one per quadrant,
each fading to 0 at its edges (`peak-tex.png`). The patch samples quadrant 0, so its border should fade out, but ours
ended in a hard straight edge.

The client keys its alpha textures by a `usize` in a first-come cache (`Gpu::alpha_texture`). The snowcap's key was
930. The surf's is `SURF_ALPHA + mask` = 920 + the mask's index in `<SURF>`'s texture list, and those masks are
`[1, 3, 5, 6, 6, 10, 12]`, so mask 10 is also 930. The surf uploads first, so on run1559b the snowcap's stage 1
sampled the surf mask's level 0.

own 64 pieces), and `surf_mesh` lays no mask past it. `world::tests::the_alpha_keys_are_apart` checks the following:

- every one-key family (water, weather, road, snowcap) sits outside the surf's range, below the templates' 1,000, and
  apart from the others;
- the install's 14 transitions sit under the water's key;
- the install's `<SURF>` masks are `[1, 3, 5, 6, 6, 10, 12]`, all inside the range.

For the Loop: a renderer cache keyed by hand-picked integers is only as safe as its widest family's range. The surf's
range was implicit in a texture count that no constant named, and the collision showed only as a mountain's colour.
A key family with a range now names it, and a test holds the families apart.

A second lesson, from the probe scripts: restoring a file with `mv` from a `cp` gives it an mtime older than the last
build, so cargo does not rebuild. One "base" render was secretly the previous variant's binary. Touch a restored file.
