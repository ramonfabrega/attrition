## The brief's hypothesis did not survive twenty minutes

The item carried item 464's reading — `1/28`'s `GROUP_MOVE`'s `oxx` goes
40 → 28 between blocks 10233 and 10234, so it becomes its group's leader
on the frame it stands still, and `do_group_move` plans for the leader
alone — and told me to treat it as the fourth candidate rather than the
answer. It named its own falsifier, run100 blocks 10233–10235, and the
brief's instruction was the one that mattered: **every unit on the
frame, not the ones the brief names.**

I cut the window out of run100 and printed the six raiders' records side
by side. The first line of the table answers it:

| block | `1/27` | `1/28` | `1/29` | `1/40` | `1/41` | `1/42` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10233 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 |
| 10234 | 28 | 28 | 28 | 28 | 28 | 28 |

`oxx` is not `1/28`'s field, it is the **group order's**, and all six
change together. Nobody was promoted; the block was re-seated. And the
same print kills the second half of the reading for free: `1/27`'s own
order dies during frame 10238 and it plans **and steps** on 10239, as a
follower, with no dead frame at all. A follower plans.

Three hypotheses have now been written for this one frame — 456's
formation pathing, 463's order-death delay, 464's leadership promotion —
each read off one unit's value diff, each wrong, and the frame right
every time. DECISIONS 42 keeps earning itself.

## What the frame is, and where the defect actually was

`Unit::do_group_move@005e79a0`'s follower arm tests whether the leader is
still usable (alive, on the map, in my group, holding a move with my
group order's id). `1/28`'s leader `1/40` still holds its `ATTACKORDER`
on the dead building, so the arm falls to `LAB_005e7ee2`, calls
`Group::refresh_group_order` and **returns without moving**. That is the
frame. Next frame `1/28` is the leader and plans. `1/27` on 10239 finds
`1/28` usable, so it follows and steps at once. Two arms, and every plan
frame in the window falls out of them.

Then the thing worth more than the frame. None of that ran here, because
this crate's `1/28` held a plain `MOVE_TO` — and the reason is a line
**we invented**. `Group::action_move_near`'s gate at `705f00`–`705f61`
exempts modern infantry, a `role & 0x10` type that is not AI-driven, a
group of fewer than two, `unit_masks & 4`, a sea type and form 9. There
is no army test in it. This crate's read `g.army.is_some() && …`, with a
comment saying so in as many words: "that is this crate's own line rather
than the original's". An invented predicate is a different kind of bug
from a misread one, and it had been sitting in the gate for four months
while three items chased the symptom.

The probe (`docs/ARMY.md` §12) pushes six raiders **out of** army 1, so
at the moment their go-home move is issued the group has no army. The
dump's own `GroupMoveOrder` id settles the frame without a guess:
`8192502 = (65 + 8186 × 10) × 100 + 2` — group 65, frame 8186, order_num
2, which is this crate's frame for the same call to the frame.

## Two things had to be carried, and the second is why the first is safe

**A pushed group has to survive.** `Sim::pushed_group` was one slot with
no record; the scout's `go_to` overwrote it eleven times between 8186 and
the word. It is a `Vec` of slots now, each carrying the `GroupState` an
army's group already had, recycled the way `Groups::get_open_slot`
recycles. `group_of` asks the army first and the pool second.

**And dropping the army line alone collapses the word to 6994.** That is
the second squad's walk to the army, and it is a `Unit::go_to` — the
original gives *those* plain moves, but by `unit_masks & 4`, not by the
army. `go_to_unit@005f78c0` sets the bit over the whole squad one line
above the distance test; `Unit::work@0060d180` drops it again on the
unit's own 32-frame tick, inside the same `(frame + o) & 31 == 0` that
clears `visible`. With the latch carried, 6994 holds and 8186 groups —
and the dump agrees on both, since `1/28`'s `unit_masks` has bit 4 set at
10233 and its order at 8186 is a `GROUP_MOVE` all the same.

I found this the expensive way: the first build had the gate change
without the bit, and the word fell 10233 → 6994. Twenty minutes, and the
lesson is that the invented predicate was standing in for a real one — so
deleting it means finding what it was standing in for, not just deleting
it.

## What 10234 is, and the successor

The new word's record **agrees** and its draw stream does not: the
original spends 204 draws there, `PathFinder::calc_road_cost+0x46` over
and over, for `1/28`'s 43-node route, and this crate spends six for a

the next item on this frame should be: not the pathfinder's draw count,
which is a much larger and older hole, but the formation offset that puts
