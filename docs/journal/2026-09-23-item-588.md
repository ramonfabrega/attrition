### Why this crate halves the step

A scratch probe in `unit_step` printed `move_step`'s inputs on sim-frame
10324. The inputs were: speed 42, `owed` 0 after the turn, and
**`half_step` true on entry**. `move_step` spent it. A second probe in
`name_collider` named the frame and the call:

- on sim-frame 10324, `do_move`'s **waypoint probe**
  (`detect_unit_collision(u, mo.waypoint)`), before the path exists, so the
  waypoint is the order's own point (45528, 42360);
- the scan finds Trireme `1/32` soft on cell (944, 878). `1/32` still
  stands at its birth point, (45192, 41880), in both games (the navy's
  first order gave up, item 579). Its `block_radius` is 336, which reaches
  seven cells, and that covers the muster's neighbourhood;
- a soft hit with no hard one raises `unit_masks & 0x100000`, the one-shot
  half step (`docs/COLLISION.md` §12).

### What the original does with that call

`Unit::detect_unit_collision@00617060`, from the listing
(`617070`–`617113`):

- `domain` (`ptype +0x218`) 2, air, is its own arm.
- Otherwise, with `top_only` (`[ebp+0x20]`, the seventh argument) zero, a
  unit takes a **second arm** when any of these holds: `domain == 1`
  (sea), the type answers `+0x10c`, the unit answers `is_hero`
  (`+0xc4`, by default `ptype +0x2b8 & 0x20`), or the unit answers
  `is_supply` (`+0xcc`, by default `& 0x40`). The type record's
  `ObjectTypeData` field list names slot `+0x10c` **`is_siege`**
  (`LF_ONEMETHOD`, `vftable offset = 268`). `UnitTypeData::is_siege@00470460`
  is `unit_flags & 0x20000`.
- In that arm, with `nocoll` (`[ebp+0x1c]`, the sixth argument) zero:
  - `quick` (`[ebp+0x10]`) non-zero → `jne 61782b`;
  - `boats` (`[ebp+0x14]`) zero → `je 61782b`;
  - otherwise `detect_boat_collision(x, y, 1)`, and non-zero → `jne 61782b`.
- **`61782b` is a bare `xor eax, eax; ret 0x1c`.** It returns 0 past the
  exit bookkeeping at `617820`–`617827`, so it neither clears
  `collide_o`/`collide_who`, nor ages `collide`, nor clears the wait bit.

`do_move`'s waypoint probe is `5f86f9`–`5f8707` on the listing: five
`push $0x0` for `quick`, `boats`, `_`, `nocoll` and `top_only`. The
decompile's `(…, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)` is right. **So a ship's waypoint probe
never scans.** The original's Bark takes no soft hit on 10324 and steps
the whole 41.

`docs/COLLISION.md` §4.1's gate 3 already names the arm, and §7 lists
`detect_boat_collision` as not modelled ("no ships"). This crate runs every
unit through the land scan.

## The fix: the sea half of the second arm

`detect_unit_collision`'s second arm and `detect_boat_collision` are in
`docs/COLLISION.md` §13, read from the listing throughout. The listing
decided three things the decompile could not:

- `project`'s angle and length are in `ecx`/`edx`;
- the five zero pushes of the waypoint probe;
- the bare `xor eax, eax; ret` at `61782b` that skips the bookkeeping.

**The waypoint probe alone moved nothing.** With only that arm, the Bark
agreed to 10367. Then on 10368 run143 printed `half_step` 1 here and 0
there. `move_step`'s own probe asks for `boats`, so the original goes to
`detect_boat_collision`. That function skips a group-mate, and the Bark
and `1/32` are both in the navy. So the function went in whole for a sea
unit:

- the push profile, loaded from `unitrules.xml` and checked against
  run3's type dump for all 364 types (`81aaf2f`);
- the candidate search;
- the circle geometry;
- the refusals;
- the push.

**The land half is a seam, deliberately.** A siege engine, a hero or a
supply wagon takes the same arm in the original. It stops scanning on
quick and waypoint probes and shoves land units of every player aside.
That would reach Great Lakes' armies, and no diff has seen it. It is
§13.5's first bullet and a thing to park.
