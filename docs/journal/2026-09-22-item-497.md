# Item 497 — the scholar was never the clock

*2026-09-22, lane att-497, Opus 5. Great Lakes' long word **10303 →
10582**, 279 frames and the largest move of this chain; blocks 10274 and
10304 to none.*

## What was booked, and what it turned out to be

494 left the word at 10303: three draws here against the original's four,
the extra `Guy::set_anim+0x97a < Guy::inc_time+0x271`, and block 10304
holding two rows, both `1/51`'s animation clock — `g.cur_time` ours 30
theirs 0, `g.last_time` ours 29 theirs −1. 494 named the frame and the
delta and **no mechanism**, and the brief said so at length: six items in,
the briefed mechanism has been wrong while the frame was right, and the
last two were named from the draw site.

The brief was right a seventh time. `Guy::inc_time` is not the mechanism,
the clock is not wrong, and `1/51` is not a unit with an animation
problem. It is a **scholar** — `guy type 52`, `inside_up 44`, seated in a
university — and what was wrong was one clause in the roll that chose the
slot the clock was counting.

## The order the evidence came in

**The widening first, wider than the booking, before a line of decompile.**
This is now four for four. The booking named block 10304 and the widening
printed, thirty blocks *under* it on the same unit, a pair nobody had ever
named: **10274**, `g.cur_anim` ours **32** theirs **29**, `g.end_time`
ours **118** theirs **30**. Both sides had been on 32 together since 10250
and both wrapped on 10274; the original came back with a different slot.
From that row forward the item was about the roll and not the clock, and
10304 was arithmetic — 10274 + 30 is 10304, which is where a 30-frame slot
ends and a 118-frame slot does not.

**Then the record whole, for what `1/51` is.** `guy type 52` is the
scholar, and the same dump names two whole chains of them: `1/2019` over
`1/44` → `1/51` → `1/56`, and `1/2020` over six more. The heads play slot
25 and the students 29–32, which is §4.11's `+0x19` / `+0x1d` split
standing up on ten figures rather than the one run98 has.

**Then the decompile, and it was one loop.** §4.11 had already written the
arm down and written down what it could not settle: `param_2 == 0x20`
walks the host's chain for a scholar already on `0x20` and takes `0x1d`
instead, "and the decompiler's aliasing does not settle whether the walk
starts at the head or at the head's successor". Reading `set_anim:332` in
its own registers settles it — the pair entering the loop is the **host**,
so the first object examined is `host->inside_down`, and each round
examines the member below the last. Every member, head to tail. **The guy
asking is one of them**, necessarily: the arm is only reached when it is
not the head.

**And its `cur_anim` is still the slot it is leaving**, because `set_anim`
writes the new one a hundred lines down. So a student re-rolling variant 3
off `0x20` always bounces to `0x1d`, and the fourth student slot can only
be entered from one of the other three. This crate tested "some *other*
scholar in the chain" and so never fired on a chain with one student —
which is `1/51`'s, the only member of chain A on `0x20`.

One clause: `c != u`, deleted.

## Why the block is a proof and not a coincidence

The draw streams were in lockstep at 10274 — the word was 279 frames
further on — so both sides rolled the **same** number and this crate's
arithmetic turned it into `3 + 0x1d = 0x20`. The original had the same 3
and produced 29. Variant 0 would produce 29 too, but variant 0 is not what
the shared stream rolled. That is what makes one block enough.

§4.11 had named the falsifier itself: "a `GUYS` window over a university
holding two or more scholars: run98's holds one." run100 holds ten, and it
has printed them on every block since the day it was taken. No capture was
booked. This is the second reading-only claim in a day that a run already
on disk closed.

## The discriminator that earned its keep by failing

Mid-item the commander forwarded att-496's measured finding — `Guy::
inc_time` has a zero arm on `unit_masks2 & 0x10` — with a named
discriminator: **`end_time` at 10304**. Agrees, and it is the step; differs,
and it is the slot. It differed, 118 against 30, and that ruled out both
496's arm and a missing step-2 in one field. Neither cost an hour. A
falsification test that fails is worth more than one that confirms, and
this one was cheap because the field was already in the widening.

For the ledger, both free and both negative: `guy_flags & 4` is still
unobserved on this disk — all ten seated scholars carry **144** on all
10,304 of the window's records — and `unit_masks2` is **0** on `1/51`
throughout, which rules out 496's arm from run100's side independently of
the sign argument.
