### What the frame is

`report.py … when Unit::find_attack_pos 1` over run53 answers with **two**
frames in 24,000: 8186 (48 draws) and 17656 (14). Both are
`Army::find_target` frames. So the forty-six draws at 8186 are not "combat"
— they are one mechanic, and the mechanic is `find_target@006f69b0`'s
**two-unit probe**: on difficulty 0 against a human the AI takes the first
and last units off its army, pushes them as a group, finds a **farm**
(`FILTER_TYPE 0x1a1`, type 417) near the human's unit, sets stance 5 and
orders a mandatory attack on it. `Group::action_attack` asks
`Unit::find_attack_pos` once, for the leader; each member then asks again
from `Unit::fight+0xcb4`'s out-of-range chase.

`crates/sim/src/army.rs:1946` filed that whole branch as **"The two-unit
probe is a group order (seam); nothing changes."** It is the entire frame
the headline has been stuck on. And the hole is bigger than the frame: a
probe on run53 found that `Sim::group_action_attack` and `Sim::do_attack`
are entered **zero times in all 24,000 frames** — the chain has never
executed in this crate at all, on either map.

### The lesson

**A seam comment claiming "nothing changes" is a prediction, not a
measurement.** It is the fifth of exactly this kind found today —
`scout_danger` returning 0 behind a comment about a differently-keyed grid
(319), ARMY §18's `role` filed as carried where nothing carries it, GROUPS
§6.3 calling `MoveOrder +0x4` an origin against the type record, VISION §7
saying no dump carries a second fog plane when three do — and every one was
prose asserting something that no writer, no type record and no assertion
backed. A seam is worth writing down; a *claim about its consequences*
needs the same evidence as any other claim, and "nothing changes" is the
easiest such claim to make and the hardest to notice being wrong.

### What is pinned

`run53_s_8186_is_find_target_s_probe_and_its_ring_walks` (in
`rondata::diff::harness`) is fast — no simulation, two files — and it joins
the two oracles:

  candidate scores, two `Group::action_attack` ring-walk draws, one animal
  idle, forty-six chase draws, the crop clock. This is only possible
  because item 324 also names `00602129` in `trace::SITES`, with **two**
  chains (`Unit::fight+0xcb4` and `Group::action_attack+0x41a`) — until
  now the harness printed it as the bare `602129` on the original's side
  and nothing on ours, which is a comparison that cannot fail. Fourth of
  that family today.
- 17656's two families (4 and 10), so a reading of 8186 alone cannot be
  mistaken for the mechanic.
- **A bound on the cast taken from the draw stream alone.** The ring loop
  cuts its own iteration budget to `iter + 11` (or `iter`) the first time a
  candidate beats the best-so-far, and the first candidate that draws
  always does, so **one call draws at most 15 times** on the ranged arm and
  4 on the near one (`sim::fight::ATTACK_POS_CAP_RANGED`). Forty-six chase
  draws therefore need **at least four calls** — with no hypothesis about
  who is on the frame. run19's dump then names six units independently, and
  the test asserts `4 ≤ 6`. If either number moves the wrong way it says so.
- **The value diff**: run19's block 8187 against 8186 — the six units
  `1/27 1/28 1/29 1/40 1/41 1/42` taking a mandatory `ATTACKORDER` on
  `ox 2004 / whom 0 / uid 4` (the farm at (2112, 31296)), `stance 0 → 5`,
  `group 64 → 65`, the three `ATTACKTOORDER`s that go, and each unit's own
  new plain `MOVEORDER` destination.
- **Why two adds become six orders**, settled off the dump rather than
  guessed: group 64 holds **fifteen** units in five squads of three chained
  by `o_up` under the captains 27, 31, 34, 37, 40. `get_unit(0)` and
  `get_unit(n − 1)` are the first and last captains and `Group::add`'s
  subordinate recursion (GROUPS §4.1) brings each one's two followers.

Both new value pins were made to fail on purpose before landing (the cast
list shortened by one; one destination moved by a single unit) and both
failed with the message that names the quantity.

### What I would do next

`find_attack_pos` is worth implementing, and it is **not** a one-session
item done honestly: §17.2–§17.4 are single-reading arithmetic, and writing
code from them without a second reader is how a wrong formula gets pinned.
The cheap order is (a) a blind second reading of `00601280` briefed with
§17.2–§17.4 and nothing else, (b) the implementation, (c) the wiring —
`find_target`'s probe branch, `action_attack`'s single leader call,
`do_attack`'s chase — against this test's pins, which already say what the
answer has to be for six named units.

And the successor is dated: frame **8187** spends a draw at
`PathFinder::astar_path+0x1697`, also unmodelled and also rare (four frames
