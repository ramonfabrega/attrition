# Item 422 — the number was never a number (2026-09-19)

*Opus 5, on the AI lane. `docs/AI.md` §49.*

## The word did not move

**Great Lakes stands at 9510.** This item closed a derivation, not a score.
No capture: run111 was already on disk and covered the block, and one
scratch instrument — reverted — supplied the terms the record does not
print.

## What it was booked as, and what it turned out to be

Booked by its two numbers and its frame, deliberately with no factor named:
*5,755,741 against 45,568 on frame 9382*. §48.4 had recorded that the ratio
is not an integer and that nothing said the gap was one cause rather than
several.

**There is no factor. 45,568 is the remainder of a 32-bit overflow.**

`create_units`' tail is `out = wm(fac, wm(want, val) / divisor) / 256`.
This crate's terms on the re-offer frame, measured:

```
val 42,000,000   fac 256   want 20   divisor 25
20 × 42,000,000 / 25 = 33,600,000
256 × 33,600,000     = 8,601,600,000      i32 max 2,147,483,647
wrapped as i32       =    11,665,408      positive
/256                 =        45,568      exactly
```

The original's 5,755,741 × 256 is 1,473,469,696 — inside an `i32` with
room. It does not wrap.

## The sting is the sign

`docs/AI.md` §45 is titled "The ceiling is an overflow", and it is: the
original's `out < 0 → 9,999,999` catches a 32-bit `imul` that wrapped
negative, and this crate models that guard faithfully.

**This wrap lands positive.** So the guard never fires, and instead of an
obvious 9,999,999 the overflow leaves behind a small, plausible number
that ranks below a Citizen and loses a purchase. The same defect §45
documents, in the arm next door, wearing the one disguise that guard
cannot see.

That is why it survived being looked at: §45 taught this project to
recognise an overflow *by its ceiling*, and this one has no ceiling.

## The discipline is the reason the measurement arrived

§48.4 declined to name a factor, on the grounds that the ratio would not
divide. **Both that observation and the "126× gap" were artefacts of the
wrap** — a remainder bears no arithmetic relationship to the value it came
from, so the ratio was never going to factor and there was never a gap to
be 126×.

Worth recording plainly: the discipline of not naming a mechanism is
usually defended on the grounds that the named one is often wrong. This is
the sharper case. Here the *number itself* was meaningless, and any
mechanism named around it would have been an explanation of noise —
fitted, plausible, and unfalsifiable, because there was nothing there to
falsify. Third time today that not naming one is what let the measurement
arrive.

## The probe that corrected the write-up

Before the reading could be booked, the cheaper question: would the word
advance past 9510 if our Scholar merely outranked the Citizen? Two scratch
builds, both reverted.

| probe | what it did | word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| — | the tree as it stands | **9510** |
| 1 | every Scholar offer forced to the original's 5,755,741 | **8985** |
| 2 | the tail computed in 64 bits, so nothing wraps | **6783** |
| 3 | **only 9380's** offer forced to 5,755,741 | **9382** |

Both worse — and probe 2 corrected this item's own document. Removing the
wrap costs **2,727 frames**, which means the wrap is *load-bearing
fidelity*: the original is a 32-bit engine, its `imul` wraps, and this
crate matches it down to 6783 because of that. §49 had called the wrap
"the same defect §45 documents, in the arm next door" — **that was wrong**.
The wrap is correct. It is carrying a wrong input faithfully into a wrong
answer, and the defect is entirely upstream in `val`, `want` or `divisor`.

### A payoff probe must change only the frames under test

Probe 1 is a fact about my instrument, not about the simulation. Forcing
*every* Scholar offer to one constant broke 8985 — §41 and §42's own
frame, where the value is **already right** — so what it measured was its
own bluntness, and the 8985 it reported says nothing about 9382 at all.

The rule, in my words: **a payoff probe must change only the frames under
test.** A probe wide enough to touch a frame that already agrees will
report that frame's breakage as its result, and the number looks exactly
like a finding. I ran the blunt one first and it cost a full release run
to learn nothing. The scoped version — force only 9380's offer — is the
measurement the question actually asked for, and it is below.

### The payoff question is answered, and the answer is a second cause

Probe 3 — the scoped one, only 9380's offer — makes this crate buy the
Scholar, and the word falls to **9382**: the purchase frame itself, nine
draws against the original's eight, the extra at index 6 and it is
`Leader::make_stuff+0x63d`, the slot loop's expiry.

So the ranking is **not** the last thing holding 9510. A second difference
sits behind it on the very frame the purchase happens, in how many slots
the loop expires — and it is nearer than the want-and-divisor terms. The
reading gets *less* urgent, not more: it would tell you which term
differs, and 9382 would still part on the expiry count.

**And the structural fact is bigger than the draw count: 9510 is propped
up by the wrong purchase.** Making this crate buy the Scholar moves the
word *down*, 9510 → 9382. So the valuation and the expiry are not "a cause
and then the next cause" — they are a **pair that has to fall together**,
and fixing either alone costs 128 frames. Anyone who corrects the
valuation on its own will watch the score drop and reasonably conclude
they broke something. They will not have.

I cannot separate two readings of that extra draw, and say so rather than
picking: forcing the value changes the make list, and the expiry count is
a function of the list, so the third `+0x63d` may follow from the list's
shape rather than from the purchase. What is established is that **buying
the Scholar at 9380 does not advance the word**.

Probes 1 and 2 establish where a fix cannot be: not in the wrap, and not
in the offer's final value.

## The convention that produced an off-by-one, so the next reader inherits it

I wrote **9379** into §49's heading, this test's name and three comments in
`diff/leader.rs`. The re-offer is on sim-frame **9380**.

The reason, which matters more than the number: **`debug_leader` labels the
state *after* frame `f` has ticked.** The harness does `let f =
built.sim.frame; built.tick(); debug_leader(&built, f)`, so a line reading
`f9379 step 6 (Units)` means "after 9379 ran, the next step is 6" — step 6
runs on **9380**. My own instrument said so plainly, printing `f9380` from
`self.frame` inside `create_units`, and I wrote the other number anyway.

The dump has its own offset on top of that: block `n` is the state after
frame `n − 1`, so the offer appears in block **9381** and `REOFFER = 9381`
in the test was right all along. Two conventions, one step apart, in the
same paragraph of evidence.

Corrected in place everywhere. Recorded here as the convention rather than
as the correction, because the next person to read a leader debug line will
have exactly this chance to be off by one.

## What I did not do

`want · val / divisor = 5,755,741` with `divisor = want + 5` yields no
integer `want` for `val` of either 6,000,000 or 42,000,000, so at least one
more of the original's terms differs. **I do not know which, and §49 says
so rather than guessing.**

No capture can settle it — `want` and `divisor` are locals, not fields, and
no detail level prints a local. So I did not ask for the screen. If it is
to be settled it wants a **reading** of `create_units@006c40a0`'s
want-and-divisor block, which is exactly what `CLAUDE.md` keeps reading
for: the place no run reaches. The commander books it or does not.

And `val = 42,000,000` is `k × 6,000,000` with `k = 7` — **this crate's**
`k`, on this crate's census. I have left it labelled as ours, because
nothing on the disk says what the original's is.

## A postscript the branch earned

The gate rejected the first version of this test on clippy's
`assertions_on_constants`: I had written

```rust
assert!(45_568 < 234_782 && 234_782 < 5_755_741);
```

which is three literals agreeing with each other and cannot fail. On the
same branch as item 423 — a label that could only ever agree with itself,
unguarded for a hundred captures — I wrote an assertion that could only
ever agree with itself, one commit later.

Both are now real computations: the ranking claim runs through
`offer_value`, and "the original's does not wrap" runs through `wm`
(`wm(256, 5_755_741) == 1_473_469_696`, and equal to the unwrapped 64-bit
product) instead of comparing two constants. `cargo clippy --all-targets`
alone did not catch it; the gate's `-D warnings` did, which is a small
argument for the gate being the thing a landing names.

**And there is nothing to build here.** It reads like item 425's sibling
and it is not, for the reason that makes 425 worth building:
`assertions_on_constants` **already exists**, the gate already runs
`-D warnings`, and it killed this in ninety seconds. The class has a
working guard. 425 exists precisely because the label class has none — no
lint knows that `GOODS[2]` should be "wealth". So this strengthens the case
for 425 rather than spawning a number beside it, and booking work a lint
already does is how a backlog rots.
