## What it was booked as, and what it turned out to be

Booked by its two numbers and its frame, deliberately with no factor named:
*5,755,741 against 45,568 on frame 9382*. §48.4 had recorded that the ratio
is not an integer and that nothing said the gap was one cause rather than
several.

**There is no factor. 45,568 is the remainder of a 32-bit overflow.**

`create_units`' tail is `out = wm(fac, wm(want, val) / divisor) / 256`.
This crate's terms on the re-offer frame, measured:

```
val 42,000,000   fac 256   want 20   divisor 25
20 × 42,000,000 / 25 = 33,600,000
256 × 33,600,000     = 8,601,600,000      i32 max 2,147,483,647
wrapped as i32       =    11,665,408      positive
/256                 =        45,568      exactly
```

The original's 5,755,741 × 256 is 1,473,469,696 — inside an `i32` with
room. It does not wrap.

## The sting is the sign

`docs/AI.md` §45 is titled "The ceiling is an overflow", and it is: the
original's `out < 0 → 9,999,999` catches a 32-bit `imul` that wrapped
negative, and this crate models that guard faithfully.

**This wrap lands positive.** So the guard never fires, and instead of an
obvious 9,999,999 the overflow leaves behind a small, plausible number
that ranks below a Citizen and loses a purchase. The same defect §45
documents, in the arm next door, wearing the one disguise that guard
cannot see.

That is why it survived being looked at: §45 taught this project to
recognise an overflow *by its ceiling*, and this one has no ceiling.

## The discipline is the reason the measurement arrived

§48.4 declined to name a factor, on the grounds that the ratio would not
divide. **Both that observation and the "126× gap" were artefacts of the
wrap** — a remainder bears no arithmetic relationship to the value it came
from, so the ratio was never going to factor and there was never a gap to
be 126×.

Worth recording plainly: the discipline of not naming a mechanism is
usually defended on the grounds that the named one is often wrong. This is
the sharper case. Here the *number itself* was meaningless, and any
mechanism named around it would have been an explanation of noise —
fitted, plausible, and unfalsifiable, because there was nothing there to
falsify. Third time today that not naming one is what let the measurement
arrive.

## What I did not do

`want · val / divisor = 5,755,741` with `divisor = want + 5` yields no
integer `want` for `val` of either 6,000,000 or 42,000,000, so at least one
more of the original's terms differs. **I do not know which, and §49 says
so rather than guessing.**

No capture can settle it — `want` and `divisor` are locals, not fields, and
no detail level prints a local. So I did not ask for the screen. If it is
to be settled it wants a **reading** of `create_units@006c40a0`'s
want-and-divisor block, which is exactly what `CLAUDE.md` keeps reading
for: the place no run reaches. The commander books it or does not.

And `val = 42,000,000` is `k × 6,000,000` with `k = 7` — **this crate's**
`k`, on this crate's census. I have left it labelled as ours, because
nothing on the disk says what the original's is.

## A postscript the branch earned

The gate rejected the first version of this test on clippy's
`assertions_on_constants`: I had written

```rust
assert!(45_568 < 234_782 && 234_782 < 5_755_741);
```

which is three literals agreeing with each other and cannot fail. On the
same branch as item 423 — a label that could only ever agree with itself,
unguarded for a hundred captures — I wrote an assertion that could only
ever agree with itself, one commit later.

Both are now real computations: the ranking claim runs through
`offer_value`, and "the original's does not wrap" runs through `wm`
(`wm(256, 5_755_741) == 1_473_469_696`, and equal to the unwrapped 64-bit
product) instead of comparing two constants. `cargo clippy --all-targets`
alone did not catch it; the gate's `-D warnings` did, which is a small
argument for the gate being the thing a landing names.
