**The listing was read first.** The decompile prints all three of
`action_unqueue`'s `unqueue` calls with no arguments. The listing gives
them:
- `6203ef` passes `(queued − 1, 1)`, looped until the queue is empty;
- `62043f` passes the same, `min(queued, 5)` times;
- `620470` passes `(p, 1)`, with a negative `p` replaced by `queued − 1`
  at `62045d`.

The bit's early return is `cmp edi, −1; jge` at `6203c1`.

**What the emulator showed**:
- `issue_unqueue` writes 15 bytes, `30 [who][o][p][uid]`, with no group.
- `unqueue(0, 1)` on `[132*, 132, 170]` removes slot 1 and keeps the
  head. On `[132*, 170]` it removes the head. The refund is the entry's
  recorded pairs.
- With `0x40` set, `action_unqueue(0)` and `(−1)` clear the bit and
  write nothing else. `(−2)` clears the bit **and** removes the last.
  `(−5)` and `(−10)` clear it and remove.
- On an empty queue nothing is written, so a stale bit stays.

The premise's three claims (the walk, the recorded refund, and the
single cancel that turns the bit off and removes nothing) are the
listing's. The emulator added what the selector does and the −2 edge.
