# Item 402 — the `AMMO` record, whole: fifteen of twenty-seven, and 183 blocks

**The word did not move, and that is the result.** Item 396 owed this
widening, `CLAUDE.md` says why it comes before the next residue row, and the
answer it gives is that the fourteen fields this crate holds were already
the original's on every one of run109's 183 blocks. Great Lakes stays at
**9510 of 24,000**. No floor moved in either direction.

## The record is bigger than the last item thought

**The record has twenty-seven fields, not twenty-five**, and that sentence
is the most reusable thing this item produced. §22.4 and the queue both
said twenty-five, and both got it the same way: from item 396's own reading
of the record it was *using*, which is a count of what one item looked at
rather than a count of what the function prints. The measured number comes
from two independent places — `log_data`'s own unwind indices run
`0x00`..`0x1a`, twenty-seven of them, and every one of the 183 blocks on
the disk carries twenty-seven `key value` lines.

Nothing turned on the difference. What turns on it is the shape: **a
widening exists precisely because the item that wrote the record's
description counted its own usage**, and the only way to find that out is
to walk the whole thing. It is also the third booked number in two days to
be wrong while the frame around it was right, which is what the standing
warning is for. Re-measuring cost one probe.

## What the widening actually compares

Before: five numbers (`sx`, `sy`, `ex`, `ey`, `total_time`) on nine arrows,
at each one's first block. After:

- **fourteen** columns on **all 183 blocks** — `who`, `o`, `whom`, `ox`,
  `sx`, `sy`, `ex`, `ey`, `angle`, `cur_time`, `total_time`, `splash_area`,
  `num_guys`, `accuracy` — against this crate's own state, frame by frame;
- **`sz`** against run100's guy record plus §22's own table, which is the
  fifteenth field and the first thing that has ever read `launch::Node::dz`;
- **`v1z`** and **`dx`** derived from this crate's endpoints and flight
  time, in integers, to within a float32 ulp;
- **ten** engine fields pinned as the original's own values, so a residue
  row is visible rather than silent.

The extra columns are the smaller half. The bigger one is that nine first
blocks became 183 blocks, so the arrow's **lifetime** is now compared: the
original's `cur_time` runs 1 to `total_time − 1` and the record stops,
because `Ammo::inc_time` lands and frees the shot on the frame its clock
would reach `total_time`. That is the same predicate `process_projectiles`
has, and the counts match 183 against 183 — asserted *before* any value, so
a crate that kept an arrow a frame too long says "the arrows' lifetimes
differ" instead of failing on an aim.

## Three fields the reading answered better than the fit

Grepping the decompile for the three I could not name from the dump paid
three times over:

- **`index` and `graph_index` are different animals.**
  `Objects::add_ammo@00658b10` scans its pool from slot 0 and breaks at the
  first whose `flags & 3` is clear — the lowest free slot — then hands
  `Ammo::init` that slot *and* `objects+0x1f8`, incrementing `+0x1f8`
  after. So one is a reused slot and the other a monotone count of every
  ammo the game has made. Replaying the allocator over the nine launches
  reproduces 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, **0, 1, 2, 3** — four reuses — and
  `graph_index` running 0..8 says these are the game's first nine arrows.
- **`GameLog::dump_ammo@0092fe40` walks the pool**, skipping dead slots, so
  a frame's blocks ascend in `index` *with gaps*. Measured: no frame
  descends, and 9463 carries five at once.
- **`GRAV_Z = -10.4875`**, a literal in `GraphicPieces::init@008ffcc0` —
  the same loader §22's release-node table comes out of. Fitting it from
  the nine arrows alone gives −10.487500 ± 1e-6 across three flight times,
  so the two halves confirm each other. And `Ammo::init`'s
  `field_0x58 = fVar22 / local_28` with `fVar22` still a *sum of squares*
  is the FPU-stack artefact the Ghidra README warns about: `sqrtf`'s result
  goes into a discarded temporary. `dx` is a plan distance over the flight
  time, and `dx × T` measures 2714 where the 3-D length would be 2722.

## What it found that needs a mechanism

**The ammo pool's order is not this crate's.** `Sim::projectiles` is a `Vec`
that `process_projectiles` `swap_remove`s from, so from the first landing
its order is neither launch order nor slot order: at 9453 it holds a4, a3,
a2 where the original's pool reads a2, a3, a4. Nothing in run109's window
turns on it — no two arrows land on the same frame after 9451, and landing
is what draws — but a frame where two do would spend the original's
`Random::get` draws in the other order. That is a successor, not this item:
a slot pool changes ordering and could move the word in either direction.

## The parser, and why it is one parser

Item 396's warning was the whole risk of this item: its first `ammo_blocks`
reset the pending record on `BEGIN AMMO` instead of flushing it, six of nine
arrows vanished silently, and only a count assertion caught it. So the walk
is written once, in `rondata::diff::ammo`, and item 396's test now calls it
rather than keeping a second copy.

Two things the old walk could not do. It skipped nesting by *name*, which
would splice a `SplineData` block's fields into the ammo's or truncate the
seven that follow it; the new one skips by **indentation**, because the log
writes no `END`. And it counted a field as present only if it arrived in the
type `log_data` prints it in, so the `== 27` assertion catches a field that
changed type, a field that vanished, and a field nobody here knows about.

Every one of those was made to fail on purpose before landing. Reverting the
flush-before-open fails all six run109 tests; reverting the nesting fails the
synthetic one; perturbing `angle` by one prints the parting row and its
neighbours; filtering one block per frame trips the count assertion.

## What is next

- **The pool, as a mechanism.** §24.4 names the rule and the consequence.
- **The nested `SplineData` branch has no capture behind it.** All 183
  blocks print `traj 1`. A catapult or anything whose `ammo_path` is set
  would make the parser's nesting evidence rather than a hand-built
  fixture.
- **One piece, one trajectory, one target.** Everything here is a
  Longbowman shooting a farm. `flags` bit 1, `rolling` and
  `start_roll_angle` have no behaviour behind them yet, and nine
  allocations from a cold pool cannot say whether `graph_index` wraps.
