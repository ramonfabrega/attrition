# Item 483 — the word was a label, and the guard found a dead row

*2026-09-22, worker lane, Opus 5. `docs/COMBAT.md` §39, `docs/ORDERS.md`
§19.3 struck.*

The brief was the narrowest one this lane has had: Great Lakes' sequence
word stood at 10237 against a count word already at 10244, and item 478
had established that the parting there was not a disagreement but a
**missing name** — the original's trace printed `678cb9` where this crate
printed the tick's `projectiles` phase mark. Add a `SITES` row, add a
`SITE_*` const, and the sequence should rise to meet the count.

It did, exactly, and the interesting part of the item is everything
around that.

## The expected shape was the actual shape

Forty minutes of measurement before a line changed, and every number
predicted the outcome:

- `0x678060` is `Ammo::do_damage` per the export's index; `0x678cb9` is
  `+0xc59`; `llvm-objdump` shows the `Random::get` call at `678cb4`
  returning there, `% 0x29 - 0x14` applied to the ammo's x, then the same
  pair for y at `+0xc7e`, then `WorldData::restrict` and
  `puncture_ground`. The function has exactly two draws and these are
  both.
- `report.py … when Ammo::do_damage` over run53: **three frames in
  24,000** — 10237, 10242, 10249, two draws each.
- The same over every trace on disk: the four Great Lakes captures agree
  to the frame; run16 has sixteen and run24 one; the other ninety-odd
  have none, and neither run16 nor run24 is read for labels.
- This crate's side, from the 24,000-frame label dump: the bare
  `projectiles` label occurs on **the same three frames and nowhere
  else**.

So the change was two consts, two table rows, and four marks' worth of
edit. Sequence word **10237 → 10244**; count word unchanged at 10244;
frames draw-for-draw 10,506 → **10,509**, which is the three frames and
nothing else. The whole label dump either side of the change diffs to 52
lines, all of them on those three frames or the summary. That is the
answer to the brief's sharpest question — *did any other frame's
attribution move* — measured rather than reasoned.

## The item's real product is the guard

The brief's warning was the right one: a label change moves the score
without changing behaviour, which is exactly the shape a **wrong** label
would also have. `SITES` is the only place in `rondata` where a number
carries a name, and it is the one error class the differential check
cannot report — a wrong pairing makes a frame read as agreeing, or as
parting somewhere else, and nothing in the suite had ever looked at the
pairing.

So the row is checked now.
`every_site_s_address_is_the_function_its_label_names` resolves every
one of the table's ~75 addresses to its containing function in the
decompile export's `INDEX.tsv` and requires the label's own
`Name+0xoff` to be that function and that offset, with a
chain-qualified row checked at both ends. A millisecond, every commit,
loud skip without the export.

Made to fail four ways on purpose. **And it failed for real on its first
run**, on a row nobody had reason to doubt: `SITE_FIRST_WOUND_FLOCK`
carried `0x0065218b` where its own label says `+0x18b`, which is
`0x006521ab`. Two digits transposed — onto an address that is not an
instruction boundary at all (`65218a` is a three-byte `mov`), so the row
could never have matched a draw. A scan of all 102 traces finds
`0x6521ab` in none of them: the siege flock has never been drawn in a
captured game, the row was dead, and nothing moved when it was fixed.
Which is the point. The cheapest kind of correction, and the kind only a
guard finds — the row had stood since item 394 and read as pinned.

## What was deliberately not done

`Ammo::do_damage`'s draw arm has a third clause this crate does not have:
the original punctures the ground when the object hit **is the shooter**,
where `Sim::land` returns without drawing. It is very likely dead —
`check_hit`'s search is filtered by the shooter's own owner on both sides
— and no capture on disk reaches it. It went to §39.4 with its falsifier
named rather than into the code, because this item's whole value is that
it changed no behaviour: mixing a behavioural change into a label-only
measurement would have destroyed the one thing the measurement proves.
