# Item 447 — the predicate was `is_seen`, and half the fog was never lit

*2026-09-21, Opus 5, `worktree-att-447`. No capture; run113 still unspent.
Chapter two's word **616 → 624** of 901, sequence 624, first value
disagreement 625. Great Lakes' endpoint 55 → 53 off. `docs/COMBAT.md` §31
with strikes in §30, a line in `docs/VISION.md` §6, two changes in

The brief was the narrowest yet, and it was right to be. §30 had spent
five items retiring the radius programme by measurement and left exactly
two survivors, each with what would kill it written down before anything
ran. That framing was the asset: the item took an hour, and most of it was
`grep`.

## Before anything ran

The brief asks for the falsifiers in writing, so they went into the job
directory first. Survivor 1 — `Object::valid_target` — would be
*confirmed* by a branch whose deciding input is printed per-object and
differs between the bowmen at 616 and the slinger at 635; survivor 2 — the
`UnitTypeData +0x9a` arm of `poor_target` — by that byte differing
between a Bowman and a Slinger, which is static data and needs no capture
at all. A third outcome was allowed explicitly: neither.

## Twenty minutes of reading, and one `grep`

`Object::valid_target@00648ba0` turns out to be mostly the *capture*
ladder — `check_capture_eligible`, `Build::check_capture` — which a unit
target falls straight through. Its one interesting line is its first:

```
iVar2 = (**(code **)(*(int *)this + 0x138))(param_1,param_2);
if (iVar2 == 0) return 0;
```

`vtables.txt` says `+0x138` is `ObjectData::valid_target_const@006472c0`,
and that function's fifth test — above every domain and mask arm §26–§30
argued about — is `target->vtable[0x48](this->who, 0)`, which is
`UnitData::is_seen@00607a60`. A fog test. `Sim::valid_target`'s own doc
comment said "no fog".

`is_seen` falls back, when the live plane says no, to
`(visible >> who) & 1` — `ObjectData +0x40`. The brief's method section
says to grep the dump before assuming a field is unprintable, and that is
where the item was actually won: `visible` is printed on every unit record
at `UNITS=3`, so run112 has had the answer on disk since 2026-09-19.

```
frame 631 | o=10 who=0 | visible=2      <- the slinger, before 635
frame 636 | o=6..8 who=0 | visible=2    <- the bowmen, after 635
```

The bowmen carry 0 at 616 *and* at 635. Whatever the fog says, the
fallback is 0 on the item's frame, and the hoplite's six tiles of sight
against a gap of 7.25 do the rest.

The pattern of those frames is not vision, which is the part worth
keeping: `docs/VISION.md` §6 already named `visible`'s writers —
`Unit::set_attacking`, cleared by `Unit::work` — and the listing confirms
`this->visible |= 1 << victim_who`. **A unit that attacks you becomes
visible to you.** Each of those frames is the frame that unit opened fire,
not the frame anybody saw it. Without that the table reads like a vision
latch and the whole story comes out wrong.

## The gate, and the thing it found

`Sim::target_is_seen`, called from `Sim::valid_target` at the original's
own position. The word went 616 → **621** and stopped.

621 is the slinger captain's birth frame, and ours now refused *its*
search. Player 0's fog did not cover the hoplites' cell — because nothing
had ever lit it. `Sim::add_unit` did `Object::add_to_world`'s two
collision indices and not its third job, `update_seen(0)`.
`Sim::come_out_place` has made that call since garrisoning landed; a unit
**born** on the map made none, so its owner's line of sight did not exist
until it first crossed a half-cell or the hundredth-frame resync came
round. Nothing had ever read the plane closely enough to notice.

Both halves in: **624**.

## The widening, which is the part I did not expect

The commander's ruling was to write 624's widening in the same landing
rather than leave the row owing one — 616's pin had gone stale by
succeeding, the second time in a day (parked 449 caught the same shape on
Great Lakes that morning). So: every record run112 carries over
`[620, 628)`, both directions.

It failed on its first run, and correctly. The draw stream holds to 624;
the **positions** part at **622**, two frames in front of the word, on the
slinger squad alone. `0/9`'s position is *not* in the map — the captain
stands where the dump stands it and only its order parts — so it is a
destination and not a step: this crate plans all three slingers' move to
`(2424, 7800)`, which is `1/6`'s own seat, against the original's three
spread points and six path slots to our ten. Parked 400's shape, one squad
over. Booked by its frame and its value delta, mechanism named as the
hypothesis it is.

That is the whole argument for the rule. Two frames of agreement in the
draw stream on top of a squad walking at the wrong point is exactly the
"agree on a wrong destination for a long time" `CLAUDE.md` warns about,
and only the value comparison saw it.

## The row that moved the other way

`chapter_two_s_first_attack_orders_are_the_dump_s` had this crate's
hoplites ordering an attack at 616 where the dump has 635. They now order
none at all. The original's `1/6` accepts `0/10` at 635 on the `visible`
fallback — the slinger set its own bit at 631 by attacking a hoplite —
and player 1's sight never reaches that cell.

I stopped there rather than model `visible`. It is the same function's
other half, so the temptation was real, but it is a second mechanism with
its own writers, its own clear-on-`work` rule, and an unidentified gate on
the attacker's type vtable `+0x10c` that `vtables.txt` does not name for
any `*Type` vtable in the export. Four items on this frame have named a
mechanism one fetch too early. §31.7 books it instead.

## Survivor 2

Not killed. `find_nearby_target` calls `check_target` with `param_6 = 1`,
so `poor_target` is reached and its type-record arm is live as far as this
reading goes. What changed is that it can no longer move chapter two's
word: the frame it would explain now agrees. The capture §30.5 named
stays unbooked; run113 is still unspent, and the lane was free the whole
time.

## What it cost, and what it says about the method

An hour, no capture, no fan-out. Three things did the work and none of
them was a reading in the expensive sense: the two-survivor framing §30
left, one `grep` of a field the dump had been printing for two days, and a
widening that failed on its first run. The 45 MB dump answered a question
five items had reached for a capture over — which is the "grep the dump
before booking a reading" clause, and this is the second day running it
has paid.
