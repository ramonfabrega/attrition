# 479 — a captain retargets to its cached incumbent (645 → 680)

**Booked by** the frame and `0/9`'s three rows on block 645. The brief
named no mechanism; item 472's §36.6 named one as *its* hypothesis and
said so.

## The fingerprint, first, because it is cheap

§36.6 supplied an identifier before it supplied an argument: `in_range`
1 with `ever_in_range` 0, one writer each. Twenty minutes of `grep`
over run112's own dump — a slice of `[638, 652)`, one flattener, one
delta table — and it held on the first look:

```
644 0/9  ATTACK ox=8 uid=14 in_range=0 ever=0   near=6/1 o_up=-1 o_down=10
645 0/9  ATTACK ox=6 uid=12 in_range=1 ever=0   MOVE gone, stack 2 → 1
646 0/9  ATTACK ox=6 uid=12 in_range=1 ever=1   visible 0 → 2
```

That is the tenth candidate on this map surviving the check nine others
did not. But the check only says *the arm was entered*; what made the
item safe to build was the **whole-cast** delta over `[642, 648)`, every
field of every dumped record, which the brief asked for and which said
four things at once:

- exactly three units retarget on 645 — `0/9`, `0/10`, `0/11` — and
  they are exactly `0/9`'s `o_down` chain, `9 → 10 → 11`;
- the bowmen `0/6`-`0/8`, on the same map with the same target and the
  same `near_o 6/1`, do **not**;
- `0/10` and `0/11` keep `in_range 1 ever 1 new_ord 0` across the
  switch — an in-place field rewrite, not a new order, which kills
  every `add_attack_order` path including the captain mirror;
- the old target `1/8` is alive, undamaged and walking, so nothing
  about the target forced it.

## The detour worth writing down

The dump prints **two** `up`/`down` pairs and they are different
things. `ObjectData +0x2a/+0x2c`, in the `OBJECT` block, is the world
cell's occupancy chain — `Object::add_to_world` threads it and its own
loop check is the error string `UNIT LINKED LIST LOOPS <ADD>` — and on
644 it reads `9 → 11 → 10 → 8 → 7 → 6`, all six of who=0's soldiers,
because all six stand in cell `(1, 10)`. `UnitData +0x8e/+0x90`,
`o_up`/`o_down`, is the squad, and it is printed further down the same
record, **after `play`**, out of the walker's own field order.

Reading the first for the second cost half an hour and produced a
confident wrong conclusion — "the walk would reach the bowmen and it
does not, so the propagation story is wrong" — which is exactly the
shape of the nine hypotheses that died here before. The repair was to
print the whole record rather than the fields a helper had been asked
for. `CLAUDE.md`'s "a row is a field, never a unit" has a sibling: a
field name is not a field either.

## What the reading added that the dump could not

- `near_o` is the **nearest** candidate a search saw, not the one it
  chose; written above the `max_dist` gate; cleared past `0xf00`. This
  crate had no term for it at all, which is why five items' worth of
  hypotheses about *who chooses* could never have reached it.
- The two range tests in `do_move` are one function with a different
  sixth argument. The kill uses the reach less `0x90`; the retarget
  uses the plain reach. That is the whole of why one frame can refuse
  to end a chase and switch its target.
- `poor_target`'s deciding conjunct is the **speed**: `0/9` prints
  `myspeed 28` against `1/6`'s `25`. Had it been the other way the
  distance floor — one tile against 1296 — would have refused.

## Two mechanisms, and the dump chose between them

`Unit::think` has its own `near_o` arm — thirty-one frames in
thirty-two, a captain takes `add_attack_order(near_o)` and skips the
search entirely. It would have produced the same *shape* for `0/9`.
Two things rule it out and both were already on disk: `think` is
reached from `do_idle` and nowhere else (one caller, grepped), and a
created order prints `in_range 0`. It is parked as a successor and it
will move more than this did.

## The row that was said to be impossible

`crate::diff::golden`'s own dump reader carried a note saying `near_o`
and `near_who` were left unparsed *on purpose*, "because this crate's
`Unit` models neither, so parsing them would buy a comparison against
nothing". Giving the crate the field made that note false, so the
landing includes the comparison it ruled out: every unit of every block
of `[606, 684)`, **4668 of 4668 agreeing**, 161 of them carrying a live
pair.

Then the rule about guards: make it fail once. Deleting the write parts
exactly the three searching captains — `0/9` from 621, `0/6` and `1/6`
from 635 — so it has teeth. But two other mutations left it **green**:
removing the `0xf00` clear, and taking the last qualifying candidate
rather than the nearest. run112 runs three searches inside the window
and all three are before 636, so §37.1's *write rule* is untouched by
this row and stays a reading. That limit is in the test's own doc
comment rather than discovered later, which is the §33.4 lesson: a green
is worth what the failure that was tried against it is worth.
