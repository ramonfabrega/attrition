## 2026-09-17 — item 329: the chase runs in the frame it is ordered (Opus 5)

**The score.** Great Lakes' long word **8187 → 8201**; the sequence with
it, 8187 → 8201. Fourteen frames, and it **crosses East Indies' 8193** —
Great Lakes is no longer the lower map, so the queue's "lower map first"
order changes hands.

**What the brief said, and where it was half right.** The item was framed
as "make the unit-grid search refuse where the original's refuses": the
original spends one `PathFinder::astar_path+0x1697` on 8187, this crate
spent two `Guy::set_anim+0x97a < Guy::move+0x19f` instead, and §17.6 had
the address and the gates. That framing named a real mechanism and
**still missed the larger half**, which is the eleventh time today a
named mechanism was not the whole answer. The widening is what said so.

**The widening.** `track.py UNITDATA … --changes` over 8185–8190 took
twenty minutes and answered the question without a hypothesis: on
sim-frame 8187 the original moves **five** of the probe's six and freezes
the sixth; this crate moved **none of them**. Both extra arrival stands
were units standing still, not units stepping. And the dump carries the
`STACK<TYPE>` block — the unit's whole path stack, length and every
`PATHDATA` entry — which no test had ever read: at the end of sim-frame
**8186** the original's six already hold stacks of 43, 43, 43, 94, 47 and
46 entries, and this crate's held none. The chase was planned a frame
late.

**The cause, and it is not the pathfinder.** `Unit::fight@005fd4d0`'s
chase tail does not return after `add_move_order`. At `005fe3e7` it calls
the unit's own vtable `+0x188` — **`Unit::work`** — behind a latch on the
action order's flag `0x10`, so the move it has just pushed is dispatched,
planned, and walked on the same tick. `docs/ORDERS.md` §7.2 had the row
and had it twice wrong: it named `do_idle`, which is `+0x184`, one slot
out; and it read the latch as "what makes the next `fight` re-search",
where `005fde93` shows the bit *suppressing* the one-in-five search. Both
struck in place, with §7.10 as the successor.

Two gates, and the listing was worth the minute: `005fe395`-`005fe3b1`
is two `je`s, one per coordinate, so the re-entry needs the chosen spot
to differ from the unit's own in **both** x and y — matching either one
returns.

**A comparison that could not fail, removed.** `flag::RETARGET` (0x10)
was read in `do_attack` as "re-target requested" → kill the order and
find a new target. `Unit::do_attack@005f1b80` reads **no order flag at
all**, and nothing in this crate ever wrote the bit, so the arm was dead
code with a wrong name attached. It is now `flag::FIGHT_REENTRY`, written
by `fight` and read by `fight`, and the one-in-five suppression it
actually controls is modelled.

**The second half, and a seam whose premise had expired.** With the
re-entry in, 8187 went to seven draws against eight — the `684e07` still
missing. `crates/sim/src/path.rs`'s unit-grid failure tail carried

> SEAM: the pause roll happens only when the order's target is a unit;
> move orders here never target one, so no draw.

That premise is **not in the function**. `astar_path@00683770` has two
unit-grid failure doors and neither mentions a target: the work-cap one
(`006848c9`) gates on the current order being a transit, and the
open-list-exhausted one (`00684e07`) gates on that **and** the move
data's `attempts < 0xd`. Reading the two as one gate is the error the
listing prevented. And `find_upath@00682f30:187`-`195` is what makes it
matter: the failed search kills the order *unless* the roll left a
non-zero `retry`. Without the roll this crate destroyed `1/28`'s chase
outright — its 42-entry stack thrown away, back in `fight` on 8188.

`MoveOrder +0x1c` is **`retry`** and `+0x20` is `attempts`; §17.6 called
the first one a "retry delay at `+0x1c`" without naming it, and the type
record settles both. `do_move@005f7b30:348` is the consumer: while
`retry` stands the move does nothing at all, and the frame it expires
`attempts` gains three.

**What this changes beyond the frame.** The re-entry fires for **every**

is the release gate's whole diff suite, and nothing outside the 8187–8201
