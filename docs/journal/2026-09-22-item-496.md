# 496 — the frozen frame, and the target the order keeps

**Booked by** 491 as a frame with no mechanism and, deliberately, no value
rows: chapter two's word at **695**, twenty-one draws against twenty, every
value on the frame agreeing but item 495's three. The brief said the shape
was different from the four items before it and that I would have nothing to
work from. That turned out to be the finding rather than the difficulty: the
values agreed because the comparison could not see them.

The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` §43. **The word did not move.**

## The re-measure said what it was told to say, and then one more thing

Step 1 of the brief was to confirm 21-against-20 and the emptiness before
reading anything. Both held on the tip: the widening walks 695 and reports
exactly `damage 1/7`, `damage_frac 1/7` and `damage_frame 1/7`, unchanged
since 686.

Step 2 was to name the unit without a hypothesis, and the walk already
knew how: `attributed_sites` has hung off the Great Lakes harness since item
204 and keeps each draw's `unit who/o`. Twenty minutes of scratch test later
I had the three bowmen `0/6`, `0/7`, `0/8` ending an attack animation
together on 695 where the original ends two — and then found the window I
had just rebuilt sitting in `harness.rs`. Item 432 did the same thing in
August. It is in `walk_chapter` now, as `RON_GOLDEN_SITES`.

## The dump could answer it and had never been asked

`GuyData::log_data` announces four detail levels and the animation clock is
past the third. run112 is `GUYS=2`, so a `GUY` block stops after `ox`, and
the one number the item needed — did `0/6`'s clock step on 695 — was not in
any file on the disk. That is what run118 is: run112's line with one field
changed, half an hour end to end, and it answers in a grep.

```
  0/6  cur_anim 12  cur_time 29  end_time 30  last_time 29    ← last == cur
```

`last_time` is `cur_time` before the step. Every other block has
`last = cur − 1`. The original's `0/6` did not step its clock on 695, so it
did not wrap, so it did not draw.

And the *cause* of the zero step was already on the disk twice over.
`Guy::inc_time`'s step is zero under `unit_masks2 & 0x10`; `docs/ANIM.md` §5
has said so since the mechanic was written; `the_frozen_frame_s_figures_do_
not_step_their_clocks` has asserted it against the corpus since 2026-09-05,
ending with "the day `guy_inc_time` learns the bit, this is the check that
says what it should do"; and run112's `OBJECT` block prints `unit_masks2` at
every detail level and carries **16** on `0/6` at exactly that block. Six
unit-frames in the whole capture carry the bit and the first of them is this
item's frame.

So the mechanism was documented, asserted and dumped, and unimplemented.
The capture was still worth its half hour: without `last_time` I would have
had a plausible story rather than the step itself.

## Why the word did not move

The bit's writer is `Unit::fight`'s `LAB_005fe502`: *still ordered to
attack, reload open, not firing this frame*. Getting our `0/6` there needs
the frame the original actually has, and this crate does not have it.

run112's `ATTACKORDER` keeps the **dead** `1/8` on all three bowmen from 683
to 695 — twelve frames — because the reload gate returns before `fight` is
entered. On 695 the reload opens, the target is found invalid, all three
retarget to `1/6`, `0/7` and `0/8` strike, and `0/6` freezes. This crate's
`Sim::forget` drops the dead object from every attacker's target slot on the
frame it dies, so its bowmen carry nothing at all from 684 and go idle on
695 instead.

I tried the obvious half. Removing the clear alone leaves the word at 695 —
no writer for the freeze without the whole arm — and turns two green pins
red: `near_o 0/7` and `0/8` part at 696, because this crate's invalid-target
arm kills the order and searches on every squad member where the original
searches only on the captain. Three changes have to land together and the
middle one is a mechanic, not a residue. It is parked with its frame.

## What did land, and it is the reason the item was not a dry hole

The twelve frames above were **invisible**. `compare_orders` reported a
target only when both sides named one:

```rust
if let (Some(a), Some(b)) = (mine, logged) && a != b { … }
```

`target_ids` answers `None` for two unrelated reasons — "this crate does not
model a target for this order kind", which must stay quiet, and "this crate
says the unit is attacking nothing", which must not. For an attack order it
is always the second. Thirty-six unit-frames of real disagreement, directly
under the word, reading as agreement for as long as chapter two has existed.

The map's first parting moves **686 → 684**, and the three `order` rows with
it, 696 → 684. Item 462 fixed the sibling hole in `unit_ids` — a target not
on the board at the start read back `None` — and this is the same shape one
level up. That is three items in this chain whose finding was the
instrument.

## What it cost

About two and a half hours. The capture was 30 minutes of it and the
scratch test I need not have written was 20.

The thing I would tell the next session: **"every value agrees" is a claim
about the comparison, not about the frame.** The brief warned that a frame
with no rows is where an unearned green hides, and it was right, and the
green was three fields wide and twelve frames long.
