### The draw, and what it actually is

7679 is four draws against three, this crate spending a
`Guy::set_anim+0x97a < Unit::move_step+0x823` at index 1 that the original
does not. `RON_DEBUG_SITES=7674-7684` on run53's own test attributes it in
one run: it is **`1/39`**, and nothing else on the frame parts.

The site had been read as the walk. It is not. `docs/ANIM.md` §4's caller
table puts `move_step:281` (`+0x823`) on **a unit whose step is blocked,
before the three give-up tests** — a `(DEFAULT, 0, 1)` idle request,
[`sim::anim::SITE_BLOCKED`] — while the walk is `:304`, which never draws.
So 7679 is not an animation question at all. It is a **position** question,
and the position is four frames upstream.

### Where it lands, and why it was not fixed

`crates/sim/src/path.rs`'s `find_wpath_from`/`astar_path`, reached through
`crates/sim/src/grouppath.rs`'s `group_plan_path` — the pathfinder and the
group-order modules, `docs/PATHFINDER.md` §3 and `docs/GROUPS.md` §6.7.
Item 304 was live in exactly those files and this item's brief fenced them,
so the diagnosis stops at the fence rather than editing across a live
worker.

**The one candidate ruled in, and it is a declared seam.** `group_loc`
returns the leader's position flat, and its own doc comment says the
`(ox, oy)` substitutions of `GroupData::get_loc` are unmodelled
(`docs/GROUPS.md` §12). The decompile bears out that they exist —
`GroupData::get_loc@0070e030` returns `(ox, oy)` outright when a
`vector_dist` comes in under `0x181`, and otherwise
`leader_pos − order.orig + (ox, oy)` under a second gate — and run73's
`GROUPDATA` says what `ox/oy` holds: a live group's is a real point
(`ox 36576, oy 26592` with `o_angle -1925840896`), which is the move's
recorded destination, the same thing this crate keeps as `group.o`. The
decompiler loses both `vector_dist` calls' operands (`unaff_EDI`,
`unaff_ESI`), so **which** gate fires here cannot be settled from the
decompile and wants the listing.

**The capture that would settle it is not on disk.** No Great Lakes dump
carries `GROUPDATA` past **5591** (run73); the whole corpus's `GROUPDATA`
captures are listed by frame range in the same grep. A `GROUPS` window over
Great Lakes `[7670, 7686)` would print `ox/oy` on the frame the plan is
made and decide between the two arms in one read — and `tools/gamelog/
README.md` carries the trap that goes with it: `GROUPS` needs `DEATHS`
**off** or it comes out empty.
