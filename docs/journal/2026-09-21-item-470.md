## What the twenty-minute probe said, and what it took an hour to notice

The probe printed each asker's slot, its group, and its squadmates'
`orders_pos` at the moment it asked. The answer was there on the second
line and I read past it:

```
f=635 melee 1/7 group=3 -> (1512, 8040)  open=10
         mates orders_pos=[(6, 1512, 8040), (7, 2568, 7800), (8, 2472, 7944)]
```

`1/6`'s `orders_pos` is **already** the slot `1/7` then takes. The group
pass had the answer in its hand and returned false anyway — because it
reads the last slot `push_group` filled, not the asker's own group. 465's
own doc comment on `Sim::pushed_last` says so in as many words, and it
landed in my base an hour before I read it.

§33.4's reading — the three are invisible to each other because the 3×3
chain walk is round the *slot* — is true of the chain and beside the
point, because the second pass exists for exactly that case. I spent that
hour on a different hypothesis, and what finally pointed here was adding
one field to a probe that was already running.

`65b4d4` takes the group off `unit +0x80`. With 465's pool and
`group_of` to resolve it, that is answerable for the first time; the open
count then falls 10 → 9 → 8 across the three askers, `1/8` strikes, and
`chapter_two_s_visible_byte_…` is green **for the right reason** rather
than on §33.4's two-slot accident. `pushed_last` has no reader left and
went with the fix.

## The three frames upstream that this does not fix

`0/11` is standing still when the hoplites ask because this crate drops
its chase at 635 (`attack_dist` 1130, reach 1158) where the original keeps
it to 638. §19's arm only calls `find_melee_pos` for a target that is
**not moving**, so the original never enters the function at all here; its
three destinations sit at three different Chebyshev radii from the target,
which no square ring can produce. The dump's own coordinates said that
before any probe ran and I did not look until after.

## The conjunct that fixes *that*, and why it is not in the tree

`do_move@005f7b30:216` is the only `is_in_range` call in the executable
passing a non-zero sixth argument — `attack->mandatory == 0` — which adds
`+0x90` before the range test. `combat::in_range` has carried that
parameter since it was written, named `melee_bonus`, and nothing had ever
passed it `true`: the same shape as the `0xf6` reach item 384 found.

Wired, it is right about `0/11` (639, the dump's own frame) and one frame
*late* about `0/10`, and because `0/10` then stands where the original
does not, who=1's ranking at 635 changes and the three rows §33.2 had just
closed come back. Net loss. Measured both ways into §35.3 so nobody runs
it twice.

Landing one conjunct of a three-conjunct predicate and watching the score
move backwards is the cheapest possible refutation of §25.5's "each of
them only makes the kill rarer, so none can be costing a frame in the same
direction". Rarer is a direction. Chapter two's kill fires too early.
