## What actually happened

The brief's `find_open_slots` story is exactly right about the function:
ten slots are open around `0/11`, every hoplite takes the nearest, and
`find_ordered_collision`'s 3×3 walk round the *slot* cannot see a
squadmate standing 1,200 units away. A probe inside `find_attack_pos`
printed the ten slots and the single answer in twenty minutes.

The same probe printed the line that killed the story:

```
f=635 asker=1/6 target=(0,11) moving=false head=ATTACK
```

`moving=false`. §19's arm only calls `find_melee_pos` for a unit target
that is **not moving**, and run112's `0/11` carries a `MOVEORDER` on
every block from 622 to 638. The original never enters this function on
this frame. Its three destinations sit at three different Chebyshev
radii from the target, which no square ring can produce — the dump said
so in its own coordinates before any probe ran, and I did not look until
after.

Our `0/11` is standing still because it drops its chase at sim frame 635
where the original keeps it until 638:

```
f=635 do_move/attack u=0/11 t=(1,8) max_range=6 attack_dist=1130 reach=1158
```

That is the whole item, four links long, and `find_ordered_collision` is
the fourth.

## The conjunct that fixes it, and why it is not in the tree

`do_move@005f7b30:216` is the only `is_in_range` call in the executable
that passes a non-zero sixth argument — `attack->mandatory == 0` — and
it adds `+0x90` to the distance before the range test. `combat::in_range`
has carried that parameter since it was written, named `melee_bonus`, and
**nothing had ever passed it `true`**. The same shape as the `0xf6` reach
item 384 found: the reading captured it, the implementation never used
it.

Wired, it is right about `0/11` (drops at 639 against the dump's 639) and
one frame *late* about `0/10` (631 against 631, ours keeping what theirs
drops), and because `0/10` then stands where the original does not,
who=1's ranking at 635 changes and the three rows §33.2 had just closed
come back. Net loss on this map. Measured both ways and written into
§35.3 so nobody runs it a second time.

Landing one conjunct of a three-conjunct predicate and watching the
score move backwards is the cheapest possible refutation of "each of
them only makes the kill rarer, so none can be costing a frame in the
same direction" (§25.5). Rarer is a direction. Chapter two's kill fires
too early.
