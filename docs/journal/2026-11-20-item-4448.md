(High halves, as the bytes read.) Both are the computer's units under a `GUARD`. On the tick before, each
finished its `AttackTo` leg onto the guard spot. On the parting tick, `Unit::do_guard@005e5c70`'s turn arm
(`set_angle(angle, …, 0)`) turns it to the leg's angle. Both sides turn the heading on the same tick; only
`des_angle` parts. The whole 111-byte record parts in those two bytes alone, and on the next state ours catches
up through `Unit::work`'s own `update_action` (`:283`). So **theirs acted**: something rewrote theirs'
`des_angle` after the turn, inside the same tick.

**The writer.** `+0x58`'s writers in the export are `Unit::init` (`0x55555555`) and `Unit::update_action@0060a870`
(heading, or the last transit leg's angle). `Unit::set_angle@00605400` writes `+0x50` and the guy, not `+0x58`.
`work` calls `update_action` before `do_job` and not after it. Of `update_action`'s callers, the one a standing
guard meets after its own step is **`Unit::detect_unit_collision@00617060:236-240`**. When the blocker's action type
(`local_28`, `get_action`'s vslot `0x10`) is `0xc`, it calls `update_action(blocker)` and then reads the guard's
target off it (vslot `0x74`). It reaches that arm for every candidate past `is_here` whose action is `GUARD`; the
caravan test before it wants `0xf`. Our steps (`detect_unit_collision_steps`, `candidate_is_soft`) carried the
arm's question, `guard_targets_me`, and not its side effect.

**The fix.** `SimDetect::guard_targets_me` calls `update_action` on the blocker first, as the original does. With
it, both ranges agree on the parting state and on the states either side.
