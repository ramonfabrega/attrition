# Item 394 — 9451 is two arrows on one farm, and the seventh draw is a bone

The brief was the headline frame: Great Lakes 9451, five draws against seven,
the original spending two at `Object::take_damage@00652020+0xe1` that this
crate did not. `docs/COMBAT.md` §9.5 said that draw belonged to the first
wound of a fort, a temple or a town, and `0/2004` is a Farm — so the document
did not cover the frame, and the item said so.

**The word did not move. Two other things did.**

## The same address twice is not a puzzle, it is two objects — or one, twice

The trace prints the frame's game stream as seven consecutive draws, and the
two in question carry the *identical* three-deep chain:

```text
f9451 18  seed 3ab7596d = 60391  Object::take_damage+0xe1 < Object::do_damage+0x159e < Ammo::do_damage+0xc11
f9451 19  seed 0104ebe8 = 24102  Object::take_damage+0xe1 < Object::do_damage+0x159e < Ammo::do_damage+0xc11
```

`lcg(0x3ab7596d) == 0x0104ebe8`, so nothing sits between them. The listing
says `+0xe1` is the return of the single `call Random::get` at `0x6520fc`,
reached only from `damage == 0`, and no jump in the function comes back to it.
`do_damage` has exactly one `call *0x16c(%eax)` and `Ammo::do_damage`'s
no-splash arm exactly one `call Object::do_damage`. So: two entries, and the
single `SoundGlobal::play` on the frame was the one thing that looked like it
ruled that out — it does not, the sound is not one-per-call.

Nearly an hour went into reasoning about which *object* the second one was,
and the whole-record diff answered it in one command:

```text
block 9340   damage 0   damage_frac 0
block 9452   damage 1   damage_frac 10      +26 sixteenths
block 9465   damage 2   damage_frac 7       +13
block 9470   damage 3   damage_frac 4       +13
```

**Every step is thirteen. The first is twenty-six.** Two arrows, one frame,
one farm — and thirteen is one Longbowman figure's share of a damage of five
(`5 × 0x100 / AMMO_PER_ATT 2 / UBER_SIZE 3 = 213`, `213 >> 4 = 13`,
`whole 0 frac 13`). `whole 0` is the mechanism: §7.2 step 4 carries only at
sixteen, so the first arrow leaves `damage` at **zero**, and step 3's gate
reads `damage`, not `damage_frac`. The second arrow finds the gate open.

I should have run that command first. `CLAUDE.md` says to grep the dump before
booking a reading; the corollary this item earned is **grep the dump before
booking a hypothesis**, and the reason the record could be grepped at all is
that `BuildDump` had never carried `damage` or `damage_frac` — nine tenths of
`BUILDDATA`'s `ObjectData` half was unparsed. It is parsed now.

## What §9.5 got wrong, and the shape of the error

Not the arithmetic — the **order of the tests**, which is where every reading
error in this repo has been. The `% 100 < 5` roll is taken on *any* building's
first wound; the fort/`TEMPLE`/`TOWN` test sits below it and gates only the
flock's second draw at `+0x18b`. 9451 proves it twice over: the first roll
returns `60391 % 100 = 91` and never reaches the type test, the second returns
`24102 % 100 = 2` and does — and stops there, because a Farm is not a fort.

"A building" is the vtable's, slot `+0x1c`: a folded `return 1` on `Build` and
`Wall`, a folded `return 0` on `Object`, `Unit` and `Animal`. COMDAT folding
hands both thunks other classes' names in the map (`Buffer::is_pending_load`,
`Window::get_button`); the six bytes at each address settle it in a minute,
which is the lesson `docs/audit/README.md` already carries.

§9.5 is struck in place and pointed at the new §19. §7.2 step 3 keeps the
substance and gains the two addresses, the vtable predicate and the
`damage`-not-`damage_frac` note.

**The negative was run, and it is a trap worth writing down.** Adding
`damage_frac == 0` to the gate halves the first wounds from two to one — and
makes 9452 agree with the original *eight draws for eight*, where the correct
gate spends nine. The unpinned past-the-word counts go up with it, 9,722 →
9,764 frames draw for draw. That is item 89(c) in miniature: past the word
both streams are nobody's, so a frame that starts agreeing there is not
evidence of anything. The evidence is the original's own 9451, which spends
the site twice, and only a gate that ignores the fraction can.

## The seventh draw, and why it is not a bug in the arithmetic

With the first-wound draw in, 9451 is **six** of the original's seven. The
seventh is the second arrow: `1/28`'s shot, launched 9426, lands on 9452 here
and 9451 there.

Everything that feeds `total_time` was checked against the capture rather than
argued about, and all of it agrees: both archers stand on the dump's own
coordinates with one figure each at the unit's position; the four scatter
draws reproduce the original's values exactly; the aim rule
(`x_size × 0x30` back along `find_angle(T − launch)`) is what makes `1/29`'s
two flights come out on the original's frames. The formula itself is the
listing's at `0067d0a3` — integer difference, `cvtdq2ps`, `sqrtf`, `divss`,
truncate — with no rounding freedom.

What is left is where the arrow *starts*.
`GraphicEvents::execute_game_events+0x40d` adds `GraphicPieces::get_position`'s
vector to the guy's `x/y/z` before `Objects::add_ammo`, and the vector is per
(piece, node, anim, starttime). `1/28` fires on `CHAR_ATTACK2` and `1/29` on
`CHAR_ATTACK3` — which is exactly the shape of a defect that is right for one
archer twice and wrong for the other twice.

Two candidate explanations were killed rather than assumed. A uniform distance
offset is arithmetically impossible: `1/29`'s shots allow at most 75.7 and
`1/28`'s need at least 76.6. And `Ammo::init`'s own `CHAR_ATTACK2` arm, which
shifts the *landing* point, is gated on `unit_flags & 0x400000` — Longbowmen
are `<FLAGS>lmjiy</FLAGS>`, no `w`, and the two scatter draws confirm it
(that flag also zeroes `s`).

## It does not license a float, and there is a capture that closes it

`GraphicPieces::positions` is an `Array<AttachPos>` — `{time, anim, node,
Vector<float> pos, vel}` — and `GraphicPieces::init_position@00901820` fills it
**at load** by running the model's animation at `starttime × 0x43`. So the
original is already `docs/DECISIONS.md` entry 16's second shape, a table built
before the first frame, and the port's answer is the same table pinned. The
blocker is a `.bh3` skeleton reader, not the no-float rule.

The cheap route around all of it is one line of `gamelog.ini`: **`AMMO=1`
under `[End Frame]` over `[9420, 9460)`**. `AmmoData::log_data@00679c00` writes
`sx, sy, sz, ex, ey, ez, cur_time, total_time, …` — the PDB's own names — so
the offset is `(sx − guy.x, sy − guy.y)`, measured, and five rows pin the whole
Longbowman. No dump on this disk has `AMMO` enabled; run53, run97 and run100
all write zero `AMMODATA` blocks, so it is a booking rather than a grep.

## The scoreboard

**The word did not move: Great Lakes stays at 9451.** The frame is now six
draws of seven and the seventh has a name, a bound and a capture. The East
Indies pair and Great Lakes' ticks and orders are untouched.

**The endpoint moved, and the wrong way on its main column**: `off 43 → 58,
unlinked 13 → 12, build_diverged 9 → 10`, re-pinned with the item named. All
of that is one draw's stream running 14,550 frames past a word that did not
move, and 389 had just taken the same column 54 → 43. DECISIONS 36 exists for
exactly this: the draw is the original's — the listing spends it at `0x6520fc`
and the original's own 9451 spends it twice — so a row that moves the wrong
way on a change the oracle requires is a number to record, not an argument
against the change. `great_lakes_scholars_sit_on_their_universities` moved its
carrier `1/58 → 1/55`, the fifth index that line has held, re-measured rather
than diagnosed per item 342.

The gate's one failure is the queue's own `Endpoint 24001:` line against the
re-pinned counts — the commander's to book, the same shape 385 and 389 landed
with. Everything else is green: 322 of 323 rondata, 848 sim, clippy and fmt
clean, `cargo run -p rondata` zero, peak 12.8 GiB of a 20 GiB ceiling.

**The record widening was narrowed on purpose.** `BuildDump` gained `damage`
and `damage_frac` and not the other nine `ObjectData` fields the record
carries, because `ledger.rs`'s rule is that a parsed field is a compared field
and this crate's `Building` models none of them. `gamelog.rs` says so at the
field, and the ledger's counts fall rather than rise: 18 → 16 uncompared,
46 → 43 single-capture.
