**What the original does there.** `modify_ocean_floor@00873960` overwrites a land corner's RGB with the floor's
colour (`water_height_modify`'s, times 0.6), plus a byte read at `+0x55dc` (listing `873d5e`, `movb`), saturating.
It keeps the alpha, and it does so only where that alpha byte is under `0xdc`. `land_alpha`'s alpha 220 is
height −82.07, the threshold §3.22 recorded. On the packet: 9,185 such corners, 9,184 of them a neutral grey,
`light_vert`'s colour on two. The 1,139 on the still's screen are `floor_grey` at our ramp **plus 92 to 103**. So
the strip's light is a draw the packet shows: the land's own pass, with a vertex colour that ours takes from N·L
and theirs from the floor. It is not the surf's pass. It is not a blend order.

**A probe, not landed**: those corners flat and white, the rest lit. The ground row's `SANDY` read **654** (past 653)
and `RUN1564_3279_SURF` read **700** of 772 (short of 702). It is a fit with no mechanism, so it was not drawn. The
log is `probe1.log`.

**Why it stopped here.** The build needs a second per-vertex colour on the land's pass, laid again whenever the
screen's ramp moves. It also needs the byte read and every row the frame's white moves re-pinned. `tools/ctx.py`
read 144 k after the first act, and the build would not finish under the brief's line.

**The next step, by frame:** run1564's still at 3279. Draw the land's corners under alpha `0xdc` with the vertex
