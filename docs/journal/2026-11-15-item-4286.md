**The first act: the row, minted before its count** (`bf29ba48f`). `Colours::warmth` measures the distance between
two chromaticities: that of our mean colour over an object's held pixels, and that of the photograph's mean over
the same pixels. The bound is `WARMTH`, 0.01.

*Why that reader.* The item names a cast: every pixel shifted a little toward blue. The colour reader asks whether
each pixel lies within 0.05, and the buildings' pixels mostly do (23,196 of 36,670). A shift shared by thousands of
pixels survives in their mean. Per-pixel noise does not. So the mean's chromaticity is the quantity that sees a
cast and nothing else.

*Why that bound.* It is a fifth of the per-pixel 0.05, because a mean over thousands of pixels averages away the
noise that tolerance absorbs. It is also 2.4 times the plain grass's own mean agreement on the same still: 0.0042,
from (175.1, 171.7, 71.8) against (166.3, 164.1, 71.0) in 4284a's table. That is the land that 4212 brought to the
photograph's colour. Both reasons were fixed before any building was measured.
