# 510 — the word is the swing's facing, and the record it is spent in was never compared

**Booked by** item 502 as chapter two's word at 725: seven draws against nine,
the delta two `Guy::set_anim+0xf2f < Guy::inc_time+0x271`, one per follower.
The brief named §42.5's rolled arrow as the leading hypothesis, said so
explicitly as a hypothesis, and told me to re-derive it rather than inherit it
— the named mechanism having been wrong eight times in this chain.

The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` §45. **The word did not move.** What moved is
the instrument, and the hypothesis became a measurement.

## Widening past the booking found the hole in one step

`CLAUDE.md` says to widen before reading a line of decompile, and I did, and it
was the whole item. `chapter_two_s_word_frame_is_widened_whole` walks every
record `compare` produces — positions, orders, angles, hit points, the firing
record, the death list, four more vectors item 466 added for exactly this
reason — and it had **never opened a `GUY` block**. Neither has `compare`. The
one walk in this crate that compares a figure's animation clock is Great Lakes'
`run100_s_word_block_is_every_record_the_dump_carries`.

Chapter two's word has been an *animation* draw since 695. Eleven items ran on
a window that could not see `cur_anim`.

I added the rows, mirroring Great Lakes', and got three: `g.angle` on the three
bowmen, which is the unit-level `angle` row one level down. Everything else
agreed — including, apparently, the clock on the word's own frame while the
draw stream parted by two. That is not a result, it is a symptom, so I forced a
print of the values rather than the divergences:

```
GDUMP 726 0/7#0 g.cur_anim:      ours 0    theirs None
GDUMP 726 0/7#0 g.hold_attack:   ours 1    theirs None
```

**`None`.** run112 was taken at `GUYS=2`, and `GuyData::log_data@005de6c0`
stops a `GUY` block after `ox` at that level. The comparator capture does not
contain the animation clock at all, so every clock row compared `Some(ours)`
against nothing and was skipped. `CLAUDE.md`'s "a quiet field is checked against
the reader before it is called agreeing" with the roles swapped: the reader was
willing and the *dump* was silent, and the result is the same green.

run118 is the same lobby, seed and `.cmd` at `GUYS=4`, truncated at block 846,
which contains the whole of `[606, 729)`. The walk now opens it beside run112
and asserts that every key **both** files print — the figure's position and its
angle — is equal on every guy of every unit of every block before reading a key
only run118 has, and pins the count of records actually taken (2694). Eleven
rows became thirty-nine.

## The word's delta, as values, for the first time

```
726 g.cur_anim[0]    0/7: ours 0  theirs 11
726 g.end_time[0]    0/7: ours 31 theirs 30
726 g.stopped[0]     0/7: ours 0  theirs 1
726 g.hold_attack[0] 0/7: ours 1  theirs 0
```

`Guy::set_anim@005da300`'s attack block is two tests in a fixed order — the
listing, not the decompiler's control flow: defer to `hold_attack` while
`des_angle != angle`, **then** queue to `queued_attack` when an attack is
already playing. Only the queued one is paid inside `Guy::inc_time`'s wrap loop,
and only that payment rolls. One draw against two, and the branch that decides
is the facing.

This crate implements both arms in that order and is right. So is the in-loop
arm the brief flagged as suspect: §44.3 could not square `0/7` ending on slot
11 with a branch that names `CHAR_ATTACK2`, and the answer is that `slot < 2`
rewrites the slot **and** sets the roll, so 11 and 12 are two outcomes of one
branch rather than evidence of two. I have struck that paragraph in §44.3.

## The mechanism, and it is the arrow after all

The original's bowmen target `1/6`, at (1608, 8040) from before 690 to past
726 — stationary. This crate's target `1/7`, which walks from (1800, 8472) to
(1656, 8472) between 690 and 700. The recharge is thirty frames, so the swing
on 725 is the first since 695: the original recomputes the same angle its
facing already holds and queues; this crate recomputes a different one from a
thirty-frame-stale facing and holds.

I would not have trusted that from the dump alone, so I measured it from the
other end. Forcing the facing settled on 725–727 and nothing else — no target
moved, no damage touched — takes the word to **743**, where the next parting is
a death draw the original spends and this crate does not. Both ends are §42.5:
the arrow that wounds `1/7` on 685 and cannot be landed without a float
parabola.

So the brief's hypothesis was right. It was still worth re-deriving, because
what came out of the re-derivation is not the hypothesis — it is a blind
instrument, a second capture wired into the walk, the word's first value diff,
and ten rows at the window's floor that nothing could see.

## What it cost, and the one thing I would tell the next session

About three hours, and the widening was the first forty minutes of it. The
decompile reading was twenty minutes and only confirmed what the widening had
already framed.

The thing to carry forward: **the widening rule has a second half nobody had
written down.** "Diff the whole record" assumes the dump *has* the record.
run112 prints a `GUY` block on every unit of every frame — the block is there,
the indentation is there, `grep GUY` finds it — and it contains eight fields out
of thirty-nine. A capture's *detail level* is part of the instrument, and a
window whose comparator was taken at the wrong level is as blind as one whose
parser never asked. The tell is cheap: print the `theirs` side rather than the
divergence, once, on the word's own block. I would not have found this by
widening harder.
