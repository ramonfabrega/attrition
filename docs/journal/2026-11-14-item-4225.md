## 2. The surf is its own class, and the packet holds it

The decompile names it: `Surf`, `SurfWave` and `SurfWaveSection`.
- **Placement.** `Surf::init@008a0cf0` lays waves and breakers along splines of the shore. It takes each wave's
  start time from `internal_random` (`Random::get(-stalltime, wavetime)`), and `add_wave@0089d5a0` its shape.
- **The draw.** `Surf::draw@0089d6f0` keeps the waves whose cell the terrain's visibility byte marks
  (`terrain +0x47e8`, bit 0). Each section's `.mot` shape is lerped between two keyframes of its timing, placed at
  z 0 by the wave's 2 × 2 axes, and appended to `Surf::final_verts`, `final_tex0`, `final_tex1`, `final_color`
  and `final_faces`. There is one list for each `<SURF>` texture in `Data/effects_graphics.xml`, and each list
  is one draw.
- **The texture names.** The `Texture` objects' `filename` strings are empty on the packet (the `String` at
  `+0x34` has a null pointer). So `surf_packet.py` names each list from the XML's order, which
  `Surf::init_class@0089f050` appends one by one.

## Parked

- **4225a. The surf, drawn by the client.** On the still it is worth `SANDY` +41 (`RUN1564_3279_SURF`). It is a
  port of `Surf::init`, not a draw:
  - the shore's splines and the waves' spacing along them;
  - `internal_random`'s state at map load, which sets each wave's shape and start time;
  - the `.mot` section models, a format no reader here decodes (31 files under `art/`);
  - `SURFTIMING*` against the frame's clock.

  A packet-fed draw would be a fixture, not a renderer. The client needs `fs_patch`'s two textures plus a vertex
  alpha (one fragment entry), at source alpha over.
- **4225b. `OCEAN`'s interior.** The 179 parted tiles are mid-lake, where the photograph's water is lighter and
  redder than where it agrees. It carries light streaks and a mottle. No layer drawn over the deep water carries
  them: not the surf, and not the fish herd (one blot). The candidates are not read. They include the floor's
  texture through the water at 26 %, the screen reflection, and the frame between the card's last draw and the
  photograph.
- **4225c. The beach reads whiter than the masked surf.** With the masks dropped, `SANDY` reads 630 against 484.
  So the photograph's shore band is brighter than the packet's surf at its masks gives on our bytes. The HDR
  blend and the tone map are not modelled in the composite. Not read.
