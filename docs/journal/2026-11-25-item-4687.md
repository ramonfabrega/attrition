## The mechanism

The four line passes (`mark_white_lines@00892c10`, `mark_yellow_lines@00892550` through `set_neighbor@00890c30`,
`fixup_lines@00891210`, `delete_straglers@008910b0`) each set a line bit on a neighbour. When the bit is fresh,
they push that neighbour onto the ping-pong's other list. `leech_codes@00890da0` copies each cardinal
neighbour's line bits on the side that runs into the tile. After the redo's `flags = 0`, the copy is what rebuilds
a joined corner's ring, and the fresh ring bits are what push (15, 155) after (14, 155)'s split zeroed its `S`.

The pushes in the original go onto the lists the simulation redoes. Those redos rewrite connections (4687a), so
here they go to `RoadMesh::tex`. A tile only there is redone by `Sim::texture_redo` on the connections as they
stand. The cursor and the caches are saved and restored, so nothing the simulation reads is written.

**Both halves are needed, shown on the walk.** With the pushes on and no `leech_lines`, (15, 155)'s mesh
lines read `c900` and its intersections `00`: no ring, no `S`. With `leech_lines` on and `tex_push` returning at once, it read `4500cf00`, and
`RUN1814B_PLATE_CANDIDATES` was 53 and the ring row 20 (red). With both, `4500cf04`.

The sim test lays run1814b's element-1 block moved up by 145. It pins the ring: with `leech_lines` returning at
once, (15, 10)'s lines read `004800` (red). The laying orders I tried do not reach the split that zeroes `S`.
