# Item 384 — the engagement frame is 615, and the constant is `0xf6`

Rules track, Opus 5, its own worktree. The brief: "our squads engage at 626
where the original's swing at 616". **The word did not move.** What moved is
the frame's anatomy, which was wrong in the brief and is now measured end to
end, and one constant that had been in the document since the second reading
and in the code's own signature since it was written, with nothing passing it.

## The re-measure said the mechanism was wrong, again

Twenty minutes in, `track.py` over the golden dump's `UNITDATA` for frames
610–640 said the brief's premise was not true. **Both sides auto-engage on
the frame the squad is born.** who=1's three hoplites carry `type 10`
ATTACKORDER in the dump under frame 616 — the state at the end of 615, the
frame they are added — and so do ours. Nobody engages at 626 and nobody
waits ten frames.

What is different is two things, and neither is the engagement:

- the original's `1/6` **strikes from its seat** and ours walks;
- the original's `1/6` targets `0/7` and ours targets `0/6`.

That is 377's warning for the sixth time in two days, and the first time the
wrong mechanism came from a *previous worker's successor claim* rather than
an item title. The frame was right; the sentence about it was not.

## One constant, six verdicts

Seated, the six cross-squad `attack_dist`s are 198, 288, 316, 339, 339 and
198. The original's answer, read off its own dump: `1/6` and `0/7` strike
each other without moving — `in_range 1`, `recharging 32`, and their
coordinates never change — and the other four walk. `0x66` (102) puts none of
the six in reach. `0xf6` (246) puts exactly those two and no others.

`ObjectData::is_in_range@006486b0`'s melee arm asks the **attacker**
`is(0x84, 0)` and takes `0xf6` for the line it names. `docs/COMBAT.md` §13.2
has had that since the 2026-08-20 second reading, and `combat::in_range` has
had a `hoplites` argument since it was written — **and the one call site
passed `false`**. So every melee unit in this simulation has fought at `0x66`
for a month. `0x84` is 132, which is the tech-tree id the golden record's
`add hoplite` resolves to; the crate names it `sim::fight::HOPLITES`.

`chapter_one_s_captains_reach_each_other_and_nobody_else` pins all six rows,
distance and verdict together — the distance column is half the point, since
a reach that was right for the wrong extent would pass the verdicts and fail
the numbers. Made to fail first: with `hoplites: false` the two `true` rows
come back `false` and the other four are unchanged, which is exactly why only
the whole set is an oracle.

Two more errors fell out of reading `Unit::find_melee_target@005ff9c0` for
the radius: the AGGRESSIVE `+0x180` sits **inside** the `max_range != 0` arm,
so a melee type gets a flat `0x120` where this crate added the bonus to it;
and a unit carrying `unit_masks & 0x40000` floors its radius at
`unit_respond_range * 0x180` rather than `* 0xc0`. `docs/COMBAT.md` §12.4 had
both right and the code had neither.

## Who searches, and who only gets told

`ObjectData::near_o`/`near_who` are written by `Object::find_nearby_target`
and by nothing else. That makes them a per-unit witness of *having searched*,
and the golden dump prints them: over 901 frames **exactly one unit ever
carries one** — who=1's captain `1/6`, `near_o 7 near_who 0`, from 616 on.
Its two squadmates hold the same target with `near_o -1`, and all three of
who=0 read `-1` for the whole record.

So a squad engages by its captain searching and
`Unit::think_attack@005f5a80`'s tail calling
`Group::target_opportunity(group, o, who, …)`, which hands that one answer to
every member. All three of who=1's hoplites carry `group 64`; who=0's carry
`-1`. This crate has all three searching independently, which is why our
`1/7` and `1/8` picked a different unit from our `1/6`.

And who=0 never searches at all. `Unit::think` reaches `think_attack` only on
a unit's first idle frame or when `(o + frame) & 0x1f == 0`; who=0's three are
born at 610 and their next cadence frames are 632, 633 and 634. Their
ATTACKORDERs at 617 and 618 are all on `1/6` — the unit that hit `0/7` — and
that is the "Hit" path of §12.4, not a search. The dump's discriminator is
`unit_masks`: `262144` on who=1's and `0` on who=0's.

Both of those are in `docs/COMBAT.md` §18 and `docs/GROUPS.md` §13, not only
here.

## The tie I could not settle, stated precisely

`1/6`'s three candidates score **exactly equal**. `dist + (0 + 8) × 0x30` is
723, 582 and 672; each `/ 0xc0` is 3; each scores `value / 4`; and
`compare_target` returns the same `value` for three identical undamaged
hoplites, because its `get_damage` is called at `find_angle(0, 0)` and
bearing cannot separate them. A tie goes to whichever comes first in the
scan — and **neither candidate order gives the original's `0/7`**. The world
cell's own `down` chain, which `find_nearby_target` walks and which the dump
confirms link by link (`1/8, 1/7, 1/6, 0/8, 0/7, 0/6`), yields `0/8`. This
crate's unit-index order yields `0/6`.

I can derive what would fix it — a per-side extent of about 84 instead of 72
puts `0/7` alone in the bucket below the other two and keeps all six range
verdicts — and it is a **fit**, so it went in the document as a falsifier and
not in the code. Only one unit in the whole chapter ever searches, so there
is exactly one observation and any number of constants satisfy it. §13.1's
formula was read off the disassembly rather than the decompile, which makes
the extent the best-evidenced of the three steps and points at §12.2's two
ranking constants instead — `/ 0xc0` and `(targeted + 8) × 0x30`, doubly
confirmed by a reading and never once exercised by a diff. They are now open
question 8.

One hypothesis died with evidence rather than with a shrug: `check_target`'s
"a target in another region must be `is_in_range`" cannot be the rejector,
because `get_tregion` resolves all six tiles to region 0 — cell `(1, 10)`
carries `region 0`, `region2 1`, and no tile of it reads ocean.

## What I did not do

The scan order. `find_nearby_target` should walk the world cell's object
chain, which this crate already maintains (`Sim::chain_heads`, push-at-head,
confirmed against the dump's `up`/`down` on all six units). It walks the unit
array instead. I left it: our chain holds **units only**, so switching the
search to it would drop every building from target selection, and the change
that makes it safe is a bigger one than this item. It would also not have
moved this frame — the chain's answer here is `0/8`, and the right answer is
`0/7`.
