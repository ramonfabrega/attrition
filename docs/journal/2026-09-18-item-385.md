itself is not modelled; only its flag, which is the gate") and nothing
downstream had ever asked for it.

So the change is nine lines of simulation: a `treaties` matrix beside

risk of the item was that this crate's scout would arrive at the human's

## The part that was worth the day

Moving a word brings frames under it that were never compared, and three
of the six gate failures were that rather than a regression. Two are
honest floors — the walk-slot band's two rows on `1/35` at 9338–9339, and
the point-and-goal floor 6 → 8 on the same unit — and I pinned the
walk-slot rows **by name** rather than raising the count, so the next
reader can see they are above 9182 and item 352's own band is still empty.

The third was not a floor at all. `run97_s_window_orders` looped to the
word instead of to run97's own window, and run97's file ends with a
**truncated `BEGIN FRAME 9361`** — the click-free lane gives up by
stopping the process, so the last block is whatever had been written. The
moment the word rose past 9349 that partial block arrived as 47 `Length`
rows on **39 units**, player 0's among them. That reads exactly like a
simulation that has come apart, and I spent twenty minutes believing it.

What saved it was item 368's choice to assert the **set** of units rather
than the row count: the count would have been a floor to re-pin and the
mechanism would have gone unnoticed. `ORDER_RESIDUE_RUN97` did have to
rise, 48,698 → 53,622 — but per block it *fell*, 42.3 → 40.7, and the
constant now carries both numbers, because a row count over a window the
word controls cannot be read as a rate on its own.

This is parked 373's guard arriving as a bug rather than as a guard. The
sibling test had the right bound all along.
