# Item 434 — the reading that killed its own best story twice

*2026-09-19, Opus 5, `worktree-att-365`. A reading, no capture. The product
is `docs/COMBAT.md` §26 and it lands as a hypothesis, which is the honest
shape: what the reading settles is what 616 is **not**.*

The brief set three conditions — the hypothesis stays a hypothesis, it must
also explain the slingers, and it lands reading-only. All three held, and
the second one did real work: it killed two accounts that fitted the
hoplites perfectly.

## Two stories, both mine, both dead

**"It is fog."** The best story of the day and it fitted every number.
who=1 has three Hoplites at LOS 6 and no other unit within 198 tiles; the
bowmen are 7.25 tiles away; so who=1 cannot see them and cannot target
them. who=0 sees everything through the bowmen's LOS 11, which is why the
slingers engage a target 8.6 tiles away that their own LOS 8 cannot
reach — player vision is the union. And the clincher: at 635 the hoplites
target `0/11`, a slinger that closed, **not** the nearer bowmen a
nearest-enemy search would have picked.

It is wrong. There is no visibility test anywhere on the search path —
`find_melee_target@005ff9c0`, `Object::find_nearby_target@00648da0`,
`Object::valid_target@00648ba0`, none of them. I would have shipped it if
I had stopped at the data.

**"It is one constant."** `UNIT_RESPOND_RANGE` turns out not to exist in
`rules.xml` at all; this crate's 12 is a default and happens to equal the
neighbouring `UNIT_BUILD_RESPOND_RANGE`. The measured bracket — the search
reaches 6.85 tiles and not 7.25 — has exactly one integer solution, 7. I
set it to 7 and ran the oracle.

**Nothing moved.** The AI-driven arm re-raises the radius to
`unit_respond_range * 0x180`, 14 tiles at 7, and 7.25 is still inside. I
had "ruled that arm out" in item 426 by disabling it and seeing no change —
a correct experiment I described wrongly: disabling it leaves the plain
floor at 12 tiles, which also covers 7.25. **Neither arm alone is the
cause; the radius overshoots by both routes.** §26.3 states it properly and
this journal is where the earlier over-claim is corrected.

## What is left is a contradiction, and that is the product

Without the AI-driven arm, `unit_respond_range` must be 7. With it, 3 or
less — and then the plain floor cannot reach the 6.85 tiles the original
*does* engage at. Both cannot hold.

Two facts I went and got rather than assumed, because each could have
dissolved it: `UnitData +0x68` **is** `unit_masks` by the type record, so
the arm's own word is not misread; and the hoplites take **no damage
through 650**, so 635 is a search and not retaliation — which is what makes
6.85 a lower bound at all.

The way out I believe, and it is written as a belief: the 6.85 belongs to
`think`'s *cheap* remembered-target arm, whose range test is
`ObjectData::is_in_range@006486b0` and not the search. That vacates the
lower bound and lets one constant explain everything. §26.5 names the read
that decides it and it needs no run.

## The slingers, which is why any of this is trustworthy

The pass condition earned its place. Both dead stories explained the
hoplites; the surviving hypothesis has to explain why the slingers are
**invariant** — their own term, `(6 + 1) * 0xc0 + 0x180` = nine tiles,
dominates the respond floor at every value in play, and who=0's units carry
`unit_masks` 0 so the AI-driven arm never touches them. So the slingers
engage at 8.6 under every value the hypothesis ranges over, which is
exactly why only the melee squad is wrong.

And that is now an assertion rather than an argument: item 426's oracle
requires six of its nine rows to keep agreeing, so a fix that makes the
hoplites right by making the slingers wrong **fails**. Most readings here
cannot be checked the day they land; this one can, and the next item will
find out.
