# Item 392 — the ring was the wrong function, and the word moved to 619

Rules track, Opus 5, its own worktree. The brief named `docs/COMBAT.md` §17's
ring as what stands at the golden record's frame 618. **It is not the ring.** A
melee asker with a seated unit target never reaches the ring at all: §17.3's own
second arm hands the whole call to `Unit::find_melee_pos@006010b0` and returns.
That function is now built, it reproduces the original's own destination from
the original's own state, and the word moved: **618 → 619**, value diff 619 →
620.

## The measurement first, and it said the direction of the difference

The brief said "ours answers nothing and loses the move". The widening said
something sharper — frame 618 was **ours 7, theirs 6**, an *extra* draw:

```
f617  ours 8 theirs 8   Unit::fight+0x9b0 · Guy::set_anim+0xf2f < Guy::move+0x166
f618  ours 7 theirs 6   ours has one more Unit::fight+0x9b0
```

`0/6`'s chase order at 617 was dropped inside the frame, so 618 found it idle
and it rolled §12.4's re-search again. The original's `0/6` carries the move
from 618 and does not reach `do_attack` until 650.

## Reading, and the thing the section already said

`docs/COMBAT.md` §17.3 already had the answer and nobody had followed it:

> a type without `+0x2c8 & 0x400` — `max_range == 0` — takes `local_18 = 0x30`;
> and **only then**, if the target `is_unit` **and** is not moving (`+0xd8`),
> the **melee** path `Unit::find_melee_pos@006010b0` and a return.

`0/6` is a hoplite (melee), `1/6` is a seated unit. So the ring was never in
this frame. The implementation's own module comment said `find_melee_pos` was
"not modelled" and answered `None`, which is exactly the lost move.

`find_melee_pos` is two steps — `Unit::find_open_slots@00600e30`, then the
slot nearest the asker by `vector_dist` — and `find_open_slots` is an
axis-aligned square ring of quarter-tile centres around the target, **with no
draw at all**. That is why the melee chase is invisible in the trace and only
the dump can measure it. `docs/COMBAT.md` §19 is the specification.

The decompile lost three things and the listing gave all three back in twenty
minutes (`llvm-objdump 0x600e30..0x6010b0`, `0x6010b0..0x601280`):

- the radius, `asker + target + 4` where Ghidra showed `+4` unconditionally —
  it is `+1` when the asker's `ObjectData::is(0x84, 0)` is false, and `0x84` is
  `HOPLITES`;
- `vector_dist(unaff_EDI, unaff_ESI)`, whose arguments are the candidate minus
  `param_6`/`param_7`, and the call site fills those with the **asker's own**
  position;
- the walk itself, which is `orthog_x@00add250` / `orthog_y@00add210` indices
  1..4 read straight out of the PE: `(0,−1)`, `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(−1,0)`.

## What nearly sent this down a hole, and what stopped it

With the ring built, the harness answered `(1224, 7704)` and the original's
dump says `(1080, 8280)` — which is the **farthest** of the ring's four corners
from the asker. Six nearer candidates beat it on distance. For twenty minutes
that looked like the reading being wrong.

It was not. `(1080, 8280)` is the ring's *first* candidate, and the original
rejects the six that would have beaten it on `find_ordered_collision` — against
three `orders_x`/`orders_y` the same dump block prints, two unit cells each way:

| ordered destination | quarter-tile | rejects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0/7` seated, `(1032, 7800)` | `(21, 162)` | `(22, 160)`, `(22, 163)` |
| `1/7` walking to `(1320, 7800)` | `(27, 162)` | `(25, 160)`, `(28, 160)` |
| `1/8` walking to `(1176, 8088)` | `(24, 168)` | `(22, 166)`, `(22, 169)` |

Six cells, six rejections, nothing left over. Grepping the dump for the other
five units' `orders_x`/`orders_y` cost a minute and settled what a capture
would have cost an hour to ask.

## What is pinned

- `chapter_one_s_melee_chase_stands_where_the_golden_dump_puts_it` — the six
  units at block 617's own positions and ordered points, asserting
  `(1080, 8280)`. Made to fail on purpose by seating `1/7` and `1/8` on
  themselves: it then answers `(1080, 7992)`.
- `the_open_slot_ring_walks_four_sides_and_closes` — the seventeen candidates
  in order, the starting corner tested twice. Made to fail by reversing the
  direction table: thirteen slots and never the west side.
- `GOLDEN_WORD_CHAPTER_ONE = 619`.

And a second, independent value diff from the live harness: this crate's `1/8`
now walks to the original's own `(1176, 8088)`, where before it walked to the
target's point.

## What stands at 619, and it is one destination

`Guy::set_anim+0xf2f < Guy::move+0x166`, the deferred swing, which the original
pays on 619 and this crate on 621. The chase is planned on the right frame now;
it goes to `(1224, 7704)` instead of `(1080, 8280)`, so the turn takes a
different number of frames.

The *mechanism* is right — §19's test gets the original's point from the
original's state. What is wrong is the state: two of the six rejections hang on
`1/7`'s ordered destination, and this crate's `1/7` is ordered to
`(1176, 7944)` because it searched and found `0/6` where the original's squad
is handed the captain's `0/7`. That is `docs/GROUPS.md` §13's open
`Group::target_opportunity`, and §13 now carries the listing for it —
including two things the entry had not said: `think_attack`'s tail contains
**no `add_attack_order` at all** (an ungrouped unit that finds a unit target
takes no order there, and this crate calls it directly), and
`Group::target_opportunity` hands the target only to a group's **captains**,
behind a fifteen-frame cooldown on the group.

## The lesson

The eighth wrong mechanism in three days, and this one was wrong in the
briefing paper rather than in a session's guess: "§17's ring" was written into
the item's title and into `docs/COMBAT.md` §18.2, and the function that
actually runs is named four paragraphs earlier in the same document as the arm
that *replaces* the ring. Re-measuring the frame is what the standing warning
asks for; re-reading the section the brief cites is cheaper still.
