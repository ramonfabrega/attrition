# 502 — §43.2's arm, landed whole

**Booked by** item 496 as four pieces that only score together, with the
unusual warning that the mechanism was already measured and the risk was the
opposite of the usual one: landing three of the four would look like a
regression. The brief said to sequence it so I could tell. I did, and the
sequencing earned its keep twice — once when the first piece alone moved
nothing (as predicted), and once when it turned out the first piece *still*
moved nothing after I landed it, for a reason nobody had considered.

The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` §44. **The word moved 695 → 725.**

## The first piece landed and the diff did not notice

Piece 1 is one deletion: `Sim::forget` stops clearing a unit's attack target.
Item 496 had measured what that alone does — nothing to the word — so I
expected the `order 0/6`, `0/7`, `0/8` rows at 684 to close and the word to
stay. The rows did not close. They did not even change.

`target_ids` asks `unit_ids` to name our target in the dump's `(who, o)`, and
`unit_ids`' fallback is gated on `alive()`. Our bowmen were now carrying
exactly what the dump carried — the dead `1/8` — and the comparison answered
`None` for it, which is what it had answered when they carried nothing at all.
Item 496 landed the rule for this ("a comparison written as *compare when both
sides carry it* reads an empty side as agreement") and I hit the same hole one
level down, in the same function, on the first thing I ran. Third time in
`compare_orders`: item 462's was a target not on the board at the start,
496's an attack order with no target, mine an attack order whose target is
dead.

`unit_ids_dead_or_alive` is the fix, and it is the reason anything below is
visible. The rows moved 684 → 696 on it alone, twelve blocks of real
agreement recovered, before a line of simulation code changed.

## The brief's mechanism was right and its evidence was not

§43.2 said the followers escape the frozen frame because the captain "searched
and spent its frame doing it", citing `near_o 6 near_who 1` on `0/6` at block
696 against `-1` on the other two. I went to check what the captain's search
costs before implementing it, and the field says something weaker than the
document does: `0/6` has carried that pair since block **635**. Sixty-one
blocks. `near_o` is a search *footprint*, only written when a nearer candidate
is seen, so "searched this frame and won" and "has not searched since 635" are
the same reading of it.

And the real mechanism is not the search at all. `Unit::fight` has a block
between the cell-centre snap and `:196`'s `valid_target` that runs for a
non-captain only: it reads the captain's **action**, and if the captain's
target is valid for this unit it writes `(ox, whom, uid)` into this unit's
*existing* order and falls through. `0/6` is processed first, retargets and
freezes; `0/7` and `0/8` read their captain's already-updated action, adopt
`1/6`, and strike. The followers never enter the branch that freezes.

The falsifier the brief wanted a capture for was already on disk. An
`add_attack_order` leaves `new_ord 1 ever_in_range 0`; an in-place retarget
touches neither. Block 696: `0/6` has `new_ord 1 in_range 0 ever_in_range 0`,
`0/7` and `0/8` have `new_ord 0 in_range 1 ever_in_range 1`, all three on
`ox 6 whom 1 uid 12`. Two fields, one grep, and the two mechanisms are told
apart.

The same reading corrected §43.2's other claim. `LAB_005fe502` is not the bit's
only writer and is not the site 695 takes: `Unit::fight` writes `0x10` at three
places and two of them are inside the invalid-target branch. The one that
fires is `LAB_005fdb9e`'s tail, which adds a condition §43.2 could not state —
the mark is set **only if the search put an attack order back in front**,
which is why the bit is as rare as §43.1 found it. The `+0xa2` that §43.2 read
as `near_o` is `cavarch_o`; the dump's `near_o` is `ObjectData +0x34`, a
different field with a different writer. A name is settled by the type record.

## Land all four, measure at each step

With the four in — the kept target, the follower inherit, the mark, and
`guy_inc_time`'s zero step — the word went to **725** and the widening's map
fell from thirteen rows to eleven: three `recharging` rows closed, three
`order` rows recovered twelve blocks, and three more rows the raised ceiling
revealed (`damage_frame 1/6` 728, `order 1/0` 722, `pos 1/0` 723) closed with
them. `chapter_two_s_hit_points` now names the *same* wounded set on both
sides, because the frames the bowmen stop losing are the ones that reach
`0/10`. And `NEAR_TALLY` grew from 5568/206 to 7368/296 with the agreement
still total — which refutes directly the thing item 496 feared, that this arm
would part `near_o` on the two followers. It cannot: with the inherit landed
they never run a search to write a footprint with.

`damage_frac 1/6` at 712 is new and it is the ceiling's, not the fix's. I
measured it rather than arguing it: with the arm switched off and the window
already at 729 it stands identically. That same run is the anti-vacuity for
the `unit_masks2` row I added to `compare` — with the arm off, `unit_masks2
0/6` at 696 is reported and this crate's frozen-frame list is empty. Make the
guard fail on purpose once.

## What stands at 725, and the honest attribution

Ours spends 7 draws against 9. The delta is two
`Guy::set_anim+0xf2f < Guy::inc_time+0x271` — `docs/ANIM.md` §6.2's **queued
attack** — one per follower. Both sides strike on 725; the original's swing
lands in `queued_attack` and plays `CHAR_ATTACK2` out of the wrap, ours lands
in `hold_attack` and does not.

But the *cause* is not §6.2. From 696 our three bowmen are on `1/7` and the
dump's are on `1/6`, because our `1/7` was never wounded by item 491's rolling
arrow on 685 and §33's damage weight ranks the two hoplites the other way. A
different target is a different `des_angle`, which is what sends the swing
down the other branch of §6.2's split. So 725 is the parked arrow's frame
wearing a new mechanism, and I have written it that way rather than as a new
item's: `docs/COMBAT.md` §42.5 says what it needs (a float parabola and a
float terrain surface), and that is not a residue.

## What it cost, and the one thing I would tell the next session

About four hours. The capture was the smallest part of it and the decompile
the largest: reading `Unit::fight` whole, rather than the arm the brief named,
is what produced both corrections and the mechanism.

The thing to carry forward: **the brief's mechanism was measured, and its
evidence still did not survive a grep.** Item 496 did the right thing — it
read the field, it named the frame, it did not guess — and the field it read
was sixty-one blocks stale. `CLAUDE.md` says to grep the writers of every
field you call frozen. The sibling rule this earns is to grep the *history* of
every field you call fresh: a value that has not changed looks exactly like a
value that was just rewritten to the same thing, and `--changes` on
`tools/gamelog/track.py` answers it in one line.
