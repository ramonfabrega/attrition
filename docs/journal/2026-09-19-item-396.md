# Item 396 — `AMMO` over `[9420, 9480)`: the launch offset, measured

**Great Lakes 9451 → 9510.** The seventh draw on 9451 was `1/28`'s second
arrow arriving a frame late, and it was a frame late because this crate
launched every shot from the middle of the unit's square. run109 measured
where the arrow actually starts.

## The grep that changed the shape of the item

The brief said "no dump on disk has AMMO enabled" and asked for `AMMO=1`.
Both were wrong, and both cost a minute to find rather than a run.

`AmmoData::log_data@00679c00` hands `Log::begin` the string at
`int_str_array + 0x9ec`. The table is `String`s at a 20-byte stride, so the
index is `0x9ec / 20 = 127`, and `Data/internal_strings.xml` — the file the
runtime `StringTable` is loaded from — puts `AMMO` at exactly 127. The
identification is seven for seven on records whose names the dumps already
show: `UNITDATA` 7086, `OBJECT` 142, `BUILDDATA` 287, `GUY` 3940,
`LEADERDATA` 4627, `WALLDATA` 7128, `ANIMALDATA` 131. Under the right name
**run17 carries 173 ammo records** and run29 one; the earlier sweep had
looked for `AMMODATA`.

And the level: `log_data` opens detail 1 for `cur_time/total_time/who/o/whom/ox`
and **2 for `sx, sy, sz, ex, ey, ez, angle`**. `AMMO=1` would have dumped 183
blocks with no launch point in any of them — `docs/ORACLE.md`'s "the detail
level is the knob", and the failure would have read as the capture working.

## run17 answered the model before the capture cost anything

24 Slinger shots, and run17 nests each `GUY` inside its `UNITDATA`, so the
shooter's position is recoverable even at `GUYS=1`. Rotating
`(sx − guy.x, sy − guy.y)` back by the guy's own angle collapses all 24 onto
**three** vectors — `(+39.4, −118.0)` at `dz 151`, `(−42.9, −112.3)` at 177,
`(+68.2, −68.2)` at 178 — each held to ±2, which is the integer quantisation
and nothing else. §20.3's reading of `GraphicPieces::get_position` was right,
and that was known before the screen was touched.

What run17 could not give is which animation: its `GUY` detail is 1, so no
`cur_anim`. That is the whole reason the capture was still owed, and it is a
better reason than the one booked.

## The stanza carried predictions, and the one that could fail held

Written before the run, from run100's own guy records (`GUYS=4` over
9340..10899, so the shooters' positions, angles, anims and clocks were
already on the disk):

- the original's `total_time` on the four launches would be **27, 26, 26,
  26** where this crate has 27, **27**, 26, **27**;
- the `AMMO` block counts would step 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 on named boundaries over
  9420..9451;
- `|offset|` would sit in [90, 130] if the Longbowman inherited the
  Slinger's node geometry.

The first two held. **The third did not** — the Longbowman's radii are
64..82, below the Slinger's 96..125 — so the transfer was right about the
shape and wrong about the size, which is the useful way for a stated
hypothesis to fail. §20.3's own bands, derived from the four flight times,
held exactly: `CHAR_ATTACK3` at 70.2 and 65.1 inside [0, 75.7],
`CHAR_ATTACK2` at 80.7 and 81.4 inside (76.6, 114.1].

## Six entries, and they are every one the type has

`unit_graphics.xml` gives `LONGBOWMEN` six `<RELEASEEVENT>`s, and through
389's measured `ms × 3 / 200` truncation they are frames 6, 22, 4, 22, 10,
24 — exactly the six the window caught. So the table is complete for this
piece rather than sampled, which is not something the window was sized for;
extending the brief's `[9420, 9460)` to 9480 to take `1/29`'s whole next
animation cycle is what got the two `CHAR_ATTACK1` rows.

**`1/27` is the proof it is a rotation.** It fires `CHAR_ATTACK2` at `t 4`
from a facing 8.45° off `1/28`'s, and its world offset is `(−61, +56)` where
`1/28`'s is `(−68, +47)`: same node, same length, different answer. Two
samples, two unknowns, and the fit is exact.

## The port

`crates/sim/src/launch.rs`: a bearing relative to the guy's facing and a
radius, so the world offset is `movement::sin_component` and nothing else.
Six integer rows reproduce **all nine** measured launch points to the unit,
across three units and two facings. No float, and not by avoidance — this is
`docs/DECISIONS.md` entry 16's second shape, the original's own load-time
table with the building step replaced by a measurement.

The wiring is two lines: `guy_release_events` takes the launch point from
the table and aims from *there* (`find_angle(T − launch)`), and `fire_ammo`
takes it as an argument instead of reading `pos_of(shooter)`.

## What it cost, and the one that bit

The lookup key. `PieceReleases` is keyed by `PieceName::piece(ty)`, and the
table went in keyed on the unit **type** 177 because that is what the loader's
`ty = 0x32 + i` looked like on the way past. `guy.gpiece` is **127** — the
piece, which is also what the original's own `GUY` record prints. Everything
built, every unit test passed, and the word did not move: the table was
simply never hit. A `node {:?}` in the release loop said `None` on the first
run and the whole thing was ten minutes.

## What is next

**Nothing in `rondata::diff` parses the `AMMO` record.** The nine rows are
pinned as constants in `launch.rs`'s test rather than re-read from run109
each run, so the assertion cannot notice the archive changing. The record has
twenty-five fields and this used seven. That widening is the successor, and
it is the rule this item otherwise followed.

**One piece is in the table.** The Slinger's three families are measured in
run17 and cannot be entered, because no capture on this disk ties them to an
animation. A `GUYS=4` re-run of any Slinger fight closes it, and the same
shape closes every missile type — which is what the renderer will eventually
want anyway.

**Run numbering has no guard.** 108 was taken by item 386's capture, which
runs through `tools/explore/golden_capture.sh` and leaves no stanza in
`captures.txt`, so `DRY=1 runqueue.sh` could not see it and neither could I
until `docs/RUNS.md` refused the append. This capture is **run109**; the
archive was renamed before anything referred to it. A `runqueue.sh` that
read the highest `## run<N>` in `docs/RUNS.md` as well as the stanzas would
have caught it.
