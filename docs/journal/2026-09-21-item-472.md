## How it went

**The brief's one prohibition was the right one.** "Do not re-run the
geometry" — and the first twenty minutes went into re-running it anyway,
because the dump makes it free. That was not wasted: it turned 470's
*reading* into a **proof**. `attack_dist` snaps to the quarter-tile and
run112's `1/8` stands perfectly still from block 621 to 635, so `0/10`'s
distance is **1108 on block 629 and 1108 on block 630** — the original
declines on one and kills on the other with bit-identical inputs. `0/11`
closes it from the other side: it is killed at 979 and declined at 1082,
so one radius would have to be at once under 1082 and over 1108. After
that the only question left was *which clock*.

**The dump named the clock and then named the mechanism.** Mining run112
for every `[ATTACK, MOVE] → [ATTACK]` transition gives seven events. Three
of the seven — 640, 672, 704 — are `0 (mod 16)` in `(o + frame)`, and
those same three are exactly the three where `AttackOrder::in_range` and
`ever_in_range` flip on the **same block** as the kill rather than the
next one. That pairing is what made the finding falsifiable before a line
of Rust was written, and it came out of a `grep` over a file already on
disk.

`ever_in_range` did the identifying work. A scan of the whole `.text` for
byte writes to `+0x1e`/`+0x1f`/`+0x20` finds **three** writers in the unit
code and no more: `do_move+0x6da`, `fight+0xba2`, `fight+0xba9`. So
"`ever_in_range` is 1 on the block the move died" means `Unit::fight` ran
that frame, which means `do_attack` ran, which means the head order was
already the ATTACK when `Unit::work` dispatched — which means the move died
**above** the dispatch. That is a two-line argument that rules `do_move`
out entirely, and it only needed the dump plus one `grep`.

**Five hypotheses had died on these frames and the sixth was in `work`,
not in `do_move`.** Every earlier item read `do_move` because that is where
the chase-ending kill lives. The kill that matters for `0/10` is a hundred
lines earlier, in the caller, behind a phase — and `Unit::work` is a
function nobody had had a reason to read to the bottom.

**The margin was right all along and its measurement was right too.** 470
recorded that `is_in_range`'s sixth argument is right about `0/11` and one
frame late about `0/10`, and parked it as a net loss. Both halves were
correct: `0/11`'s kill really is `do_move`'s in-range kill with the margin
(979 + 144 ≤ 1158, and 1082 + 144 > 1158 the frame before), and `0/10` was
one frame late only because the thing that should have stopped it a frame
earlier did not exist yet. Landing the pair costs nothing and the parked
row lands for free. **The table 470 wrote so nobody would run it twice is
the reason this took one measurement instead of three.**

**The three hoplites came over without being touched.** §35.1 wrote the
chain out in four links — the chase dies early → the target is not moving →
the melee arm is entered → the slots are shared — and fixed link four. This
fixed link one, and links two and three went with it: `0/11` is still
walking when who=1 chooses at 635, `find_attack_pos` takes its sweep rather
than the melee ring, and 669/678/675 become **672/698/665**, the dump's own
frames. `visible`'s exact count goes 5 → 8. Nobody measured
`find_attack_pos`'s flanking branch, which §35 called the next thing to ask.

## What the new word is

645, and it is the **third** of the three mechanisms the seven transitions
sort into: `do_move`'s captain arm, which runs only for `o_up < 0` — `0/9`
has `o_up -1` where `0/10` and `0/11` have 9 and 10 — takes an incumbent
from `ObjectData::near_o`/`near_who` (`0/9` carries `near_o 6, near_who 1`
from block 643), and ends in `Unit::change_target`, which rewrites the
target in place, kills head orders until one is an action, and then walks
`o_down` to the rest of the file. That is why all three slingers' targets
move to `(1, 6)` on one block without two of them deciding anything.
`ever_in_range` staying **0** at 645 while `in_range` goes to 1 is the
fingerprint: `do_move+0x6da` writes one and only `fight` writes the other.
`docs/COMBAT.md` §36.6 has it whole; it is the next item on this frame run.
