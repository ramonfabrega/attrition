# Item 457 — `ObjectData::visible`, the other half of `is_seen`

**Opus 5.** The score: chapter two's golden word, **624 before and 624
after**. What moved is the sub-score item 447 named on its way past —
`chapter_two_s_first_attack_orders_are_the_dump_s`, **six of nine rows to
nine of nine, the dump's own timeline**. `docs/VISION.md` §9 is the
specification.

## What the brief predicted, and what it got wrong

The brief was right about the mechanism and right about the payoff it
named. It was wrong about one thing and it said so itself: "a gate on the
attacker's type vtable `+0x10c`" is not a gate on the write. It is
`ObjectTypeData::is_siege`, and it gates one of `Unit::fight`'s **two**
call sites — the siege one. The main strike path at `LAB_005feec6` is
ungated. Chapter two has no siege, so it never mattered here; what it cost
was ten minutes of reading the wrong function.

The trap the brief flagged was real but not where it pointed. `+0x10c` is
absent from `vtables.txt` because **no `*Type` vtable is in the export at
all**, and the listing was not needed to settle it either. Three cheaper
sources agreed: the PDB's own type record for `ObjectTypeData` puts
`is_siege` at `vftable offset = 268` between `is_dock` (264) and `is_tank`
(272); `ObjectData::is_dock@004711e0` is a one-line thunk whose body is
`ptype->vtable[0x108]()`, which pins the origin without any arithmetic;
and `ObjectData::is_siege@0046ef90` reads `+0x10c` itself. Memory's note
about reading a vtable slot out of the PDB's `LF_ONEMETHOD` records is what
made the first of those a two-minute lookup.

## The grep found three writers nobody had counted

`CLAUDE.md` says to grep the writers of every field you call frozen, and
this is the third time in a week that has paid. §31.7 named two;
searching the decompile for writes to `+0x40` on an `Object` lineage found
**five**. The extra three are `Unit::work`'s clear — which is the whole of
the second thing the brief owed — and two **building** writers:
`Build::do_attack` and `Build::do_missile_launch`.

That last pair withdraws a sentence that has stood in `docs/VISION.md` §6
since 2026-08-31 and was repeated in `docs/COMBAT.md` §31.3: "no building
path reaches it". A tower that shoots you becomes visible to you on
exactly the unit's rule, and a missile silo that fires writes `0xff` — it
becomes visible to everybody. Neither is modelled; both are parked (308).

The reason a name search finds nothing is worth writing down: the
decompiler prints this field as `field_0x40` on every one of its writers,
never as `visible`, because Ghidra applies `ObjectData` to none of them. A
grep for `visible` across `funcs/` returns 279 files and not one of them is
this field.

## The clear rule, and the frame offset that was not a fudge

`Unit::work@0060d180:63`, above every gate in the function:

```
if ((frame + o) & 0x8000001f) == 0 and (flags & 0x80) == 0:  visible = 0
if order != ATTACK:                                          flags &= 0x7f
```

The latch is read before it is dropped, so the frame a unit stops attacking
still counts as attacking. run112 carries five clears and every one lands
on the first slot frame after the latch drops — `0/6` 819 → 827, `0/7`
848 → 858, `0/8` 848 → 857, `0/9` 846 → 856, `0/10` 830 → 855.

They land at `(f + o) ≡ 1 (mod 32)` in the dump's own numbering, which
looked for ten minutes like an off-by-one in the reading. It is not: the
dump's frame label is the sim frame **plus one** throughout this chapter, a
fact `chapter_two_s_word_frame_is_widened_whole` has carried in a comment
since item 447. With it the decompile's `% 32 == 0` is exact with nothing
added. Reading the neighbouring test before inventing an offset is what
saved that.

## What the widening said, and why the pin is shaped the way it is

`ObjectData::visible` is printed inside the `OBJECT` block at **every**
detail level, so it went into `crate::diff::harness::compare` rather than
into one test — the rule that when the original dumps a record you diff the
whole record. It is now checked on every capture the crate walks: 37,138
further unit-frames on run79, run87 and run89, none of them disagreeing.
Those windows carry no non-zero byte on either side, so what they check
there is that this crate does not invent one; the non-vacuous oracle is
chapter two's.

The first honest attempt at that oracle was "every unit-frame agrees", and
it failed on 297 frames. Reading them is what shaped the pin. Every
disagreement is a **frame**, never a bit and never a unit: the same nine
units gain a bit, each the right player's, and four of the nine land on the
dump's own frame. `set_attacking` fires from `Unit::fight`'s tail, so
`visible`'s arrival frame *is* the frame of the first strike — a bit two
frames early is two frames early because the arrow was. Asserting the shape
exactly and the frames loosely is what keeps this mechanic separable from
the engagement residue that owns them, and a regression in the rule still
fails whatever the engagement does.

The clears past 683 diverge the same way and for a reason worth naming:
the dump drops `0/6`'s byte at 827 and this crate does not, because this
crate's bowman is still carrying an `ATTACK` order there. The slot
arithmetic is exact on both sides — this crate's `1/7` clears at **794**,
its own `f ≡ 26 (mod 32)` — and the latch's *input* is what differs.

## Why the headline did not move, and the candidate it left

The word is a draw stream and 624's extra draw is on the original's side:
`Guy::set_anim+0x97a < Unit::move_step+0x823`, a unit stepping where this
crate's does not. That is the chase **destination** residue §31.6 measured
at 622 — this crate plans all three slingers to `1/6`'s own seat where the
original plans three spread points — and no visibility term reaches it.
The order that gets *issued* was this item's to fix and it is fixed; where
the unit then walks is parked 400's.

The item did leave 400 a candidate, and it came out of the implementation
rather than the reading. A unit type's `ObjectTypeData::x_size` is the
XML's `CIRCLE_RADIUS` (`UnitType::init@0061ab50:655`-`662`), settled by the
string table's uniform 0x14 stride putting the read between `Y_SPACING` and
`BLOCK_RADIUS`. This crate's `Profile::x_size` is **0** for every unit
type — and `find_attack_pos` takes the target's extent as
`(x_size * 0x60, y_size * 0x60)`. So the ring this crate offers a chaser is
a point where the original's is a tile and a half. It was not set here:
moving it would have moved several unit paths at once and confounded the
measurement this item was booked on. It is parked (309) with its falsifier
written as a frame and three coordinates.
