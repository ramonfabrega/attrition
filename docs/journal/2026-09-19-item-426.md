# Item 426 — 616, and two mechanisms ruled out

*2026-09-19, Opus 5, `worktree-att-365`. The rules headline's own frame.
**No capture taken** — run112 was already on the disk and covered it, and
every question this item asked was answerable from it or from an experiment
in the harness. The screen was never needed.*

The brief booked the frame and named the 140-unit seating error as the first
thing to rule in or out. It is ruled **out**, and so is the second suspect I
found on the way. What the item produces is a much sharper statement of the
divergence than it started with, and two dead ends closed by measurement
rather than by argument.

## What the disk already said

`report.py draws 615 616 617` prints the original's frame 616 whole: **25
game draws**, and they are eighteen `Animal::think_bird` (six birds, three
each) and seven `Farms::inc_time`. Not one `Unit::fight`. Ours is the same
twenty-five with a `Unit::fight+0x9b0` inserted after the birds.

`report.py when Unit::fight 1 600` dates every fight draw in the run: the
first is **621**, not 616, and its chain is `Unit::fight+0x9b0 <
Unit::do_attack+0x6ba` — a unit already carrying an attack order.

So the question stopped being "why an extra draw" and became "who has an
attack order". The dump answers that outright:

| squad | born | dump's first `ATTACKORDER` | ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| bowmen `0/6`–`0/8` | 611 | 635 | 635 |
| hoplites `1/6`–`1/8` | 616 | **635** | **616** |
| slingers `0/9`–`0/11` | 621 | 621 | 621 |

**The original issues no attack order at 616 at all.** Two of the three
squads this crate already gets exactly right; the hoplites engage on their
birth frame and the original's do not. That single spurious order is the
whole of the word — it puts the captain into `do_attack`, and the
one-in-five re-search spends the twenty-sixth draw.

## The two suspects, and how each died

**The 140 west.** The brief's first suspect, and mine. I seated who=1's
squad on the dump's own cells at the frame the `add` runs — `(2424, 7800)`,
`(2568, 7800)`, `(2472, 7944)` — and re-walked. The order still appears at
616. Ruled out in two minutes, and it would have been a plausible story
forever: ours stand 6.5 tiles from the bowmen where the original's stand
7.25, which is exactly the shape of a range predicate tipping over.

**`find_melee_target`'s `0x40000` arm.** The better suspect, because the
dump confirms its premise: who=1's hoplites carry `unit_masks 262144` and
who=0's carry 0, so the arm that widens an AI-driven searcher's radius to
`unit_respond_range * 0x180` does fire for them and not for the others. It
is also not the cause — disabling it leaves 616 unchanged — and the reason
is arithmetic I should have done first: the plain `unit_respond_range *
0xc0` floor below both arms is already twelve tiles, and the bowmen are
1392 units away, 7.25 tiles. The wide arm never mattered.

**That arithmetic exonerates the radius as such**, which is the useful part.
`find_melee_target@005ff9c0` floors a melee searcher the same way — the
`unit_respond_range * 0xc0` line sits below both the `r == 0` and `r != 0`
arms, outside them — so had the original's hoplite captain searched on its
birth frame it would have found the bowmen too. **It did not search.**

## The control that makes it a measurement

The slingers. They engage on **their** birth frame in both, at **8.6**
tiles — *further* than the 7.25 at which the hoplites do not engage. No
distance threshold can order those two. Whatever separates them is not
range, and the two squads that agree are what say the third is a finding
rather than noise.

## What is left, written as a hypothesis

`think`'s auto-attack gate — `attack != 0 && (idle == 1 || (o + frame) &
0x1f == 0)` — firing on a cheat-spawned captain's first frame where the
original's does not. The slingers fire the same arm on their birth frame
and the original agrees, so the hypothesis has to explain the asymmetry
too; I did not find what does, and grinding further would have been
guessing rather than reading. It is a reading of `Unit::think@005f6e40`,
not a capture, and `docs/DECISIONS.md` 42 is why it is in the successor's
title as a hypothesis rather than in this one's as a finding.

## What landed

`chapter_two_s_first_attack_orders_are_the_dump_s`: the nine units' first
attack-order frames, ours pinned in no direction beside the dump's own, with
two assertions that keep the pin honest — **six of the nine must still
agree** (so a change that made the hoplites right by making the slingers
wrong fails here), and the dump's timeline must still have nothing at 616.
That is the oracle the fix will need, and it is failing-capable in three
directions.

`docs/GOLDEN.md` §6 carries the finding. Nothing in `crates/sim` changed —
both experiments were reverts, and `git diff crates/sim/` is empty, which
the journal has to say because a measurement taken against a tree that no
longer exists is unreproducible otherwise.

**No score moved.** The word is 616 before and after; what moved is that
two of the three things it could have been are now known not to be.
