## Where the water is, before the capture

On run1559b's frame, 4143's census found no water, shore or cliff tile. So the camera had to move. I wrote a scratch
survey (deleted before the commit) that walks our world to 3279 and maps the 60 × 60 cells by kind and by who 0's fog.
It then projects every tile centre through the pinned camera with `loc` swept over the map. It found:

- **The map holds no `CLIFF` cell.** No cell's flags carry 0x40, the bit `Cliffs::add_cliff@008a6690` marks. So no
  photograph of Great Lakes can show a cliff, and the census counts `CLIFF` at zero. 4143 counted `ROCK` as the cliff
  candidate; here it is its own kind.
- **Who 0 has explored none of the water by 3279.** The explored area is the start corner, cells (0–10, 34–45), and it
  is all land. This did not matter in the end. The lane profile's `StartReveal=1` already sets `semaphore` bit 1, the
  display reveal, which is `run_cmd` case 0x57 (`reveal`, item 3481's `herd3481-reveal.cmd`). `TerrainOut::render`
  skips the unseen black and the fog's grey under `semaphore & 0x42`. run1559b's photograph was revealed too.
- **The best centre is tile (192, 200), the south-east lake.** By centre projection it holds 424 `OCEAN` tiles, 720
  shore tiles, 32 `ROCK` and 584 plain.

## The shore in register: `RUN1564_3279_SHORE`

The census does not show that our tiles sit where the photograph's do, so one more count can fail on a misplaced
camera. For each scored tile, it asks whether the photograph's ground pixels have a blue mean (b > r). It then
counts the wrong ones: plain tiles shown blue, and `OCEAN` tiles shown otherwise.

- At the packet's camera there are **0 and 0**. 563 of the 772 `SANDY` tiles are blue, so the waterline falls inside
  the beach.
- With `loc` moved two tiles, the four directions give (0,0), (3,0), (0,0) and (0,0).
- At four tiles they are (16,0), (40,0), (0,0) and (3,0).
- At eight tiles they are (127,0), (142,0), (0,8) and (39,24).

So the shore holds the camera to about two tiles, the width of the beach, and no closer. Pixel register on this frame
rests on the packet's camera being the camera, as it does on run1559b's.

## What is not established

- **A water pixel's tile is the seabed's projection.** Our heightfield under the lake is the seabed, and the original
  draws the water at its surface. So a water pixel is assigned by where the seabed projects. That puts the census's
  `OCEAN` and `SANDY` tiles near the shore off by the depth's parallax (4160a).
- **The photograph's frame is taken as 3279, by analogy with run1559b's (4073a).** No mover is in view to clock it.
  The terrain is static, so only the 2,156 + 6,649 object pixels could move (4160b).
- **Whether `camera` disturbs the simulation.** It is a camera write. The take's `--log-window 3292 3296` dump was not
  diffed against our walk on this branch.
