# Item 423 — a name with nothing to disagree with (2026-09-19)

*Opus 5, on the AI lane. No document changed; see below for why.*

## What it was

`GOODS` in `crates/rondata/src/diff/leader.rs` read

```
["food", "timber", "metal", "wealth", "knowledge", "oil"]
```

and index 2 is **wealth**. Found sideways while landing item 414: the
assertion I wanted was "the wealth either side of the purchase", and the
key the harness generates for it is `bucket[2:metal]`.

## The authority is the type record, and it is not close

`rise.pdb`'s own `TypeIndex` enum, from the decompile export's
`enums/TypeIndex.txt`:

```
0 FOOD   1 TIMBER   2 WEALTH   3 KNOWLEDGE   4 METAL   5 OIL
```

That is `sim::economy::Resource` exactly. Two independent confirmations
from the dumps sit beside it: run100's scholar queue slot is `good[0] 2,
cost[0] 40` and a Scholar is paid in wealth; and the original's `bucket`
index 2 moves 53 → 82 across the purchase on 9382 that spends 40 on one.

A name is settled by the type record and never by the surrounding code.
Here the surrounding code was the only thing that had ever said otherwise.

## Constants renamed, every value byte-identical

The whole diff is **seven lines and not one of them is a number**:
`GOODS` itself, one live assertion key in run19's test (`leader.rs:984`,
the `bucket` read at block 8183), and five rows in `PARTS_ON_RUN111`.

**And that is the whole churn** — smaller again than the estimate I sent
before starting. `PARTS_ON_RUN19`, `PARTS_ON_RUN84`, `PARTS_ON_RUN91` and
`PARTS_ON_RUN107` carry **no index-2 row at all**, so the only pinned
constant touched is the one item 414 landed yesterday. The parked claim
said "three pinned constants across three items"; the measurement is one
constant and one assertion key.

Every index-2 name, and only index 2 — there was never a `[3:wealth]` or
`[4:knowledge]` row anywhere, because who=1's knowledge and metal happened
not to part on any of those windows, and `0:food`, `1:timber` and `5:oil`
are correct in both orderings.

The five leader tests pass unchanged after the rename, which is the claim:
no pinned number moved, because no pinned number could.

## The experiment, and it answered a third question

The commander framed two outcomes for leaving one pin on the old name —
the label is load-bearing, or the label is decorative. **It is neither, and
the run says which.** With `GOODS` corrected and `PARTS_ON_RUN111`'s
`income` row alone left reading `metal`:

- every value comparison **passed** — 33,536 field-frames, and the four
  other leader tests green;
- the `parting` equality **failed**, and the two 104-row lists it printed
  differ in **exactly one position**: `income[2:wealth]` generated against
  `income[2:metal]` pinned.

So the label is **self-consistent**. Both sides of every comparison build
their key from the same array — `rows` for this crate, `theirs` for the
dump, which prints six bare values under one heading — so the index is
positional on both sides and the name never participates in matching. The
pin guards the literal against the generated name. Nothing guards the
generated name against the truth.

## Why it survived, and what that says

**The specifications had it right and the decompile export had it right.**
`docs/AI.md` §2 reads "`gather_slots[2]` (wealth)"; `docs/ECONOMY.md`'s
wealth-slot section is that index; `TypeIndex` is unambiguous. Neither a
specification nor a decompile carries this string, so no second reading
could have caught it — and that is the *smaller* half.

The larger half is that **the suite is blind to it by construction**. 858
tests, five of them comparing this very record field by field over four
captures, and a self-consistent label has nothing to disagree with. It
could have stayed wrong forever without a single test caring, and it very
nearly did: it has been wrong since run19.

This is the day's argument again from a new direction. The tree is checked
by running it, not by reading about it — and a thing that is only ever
compared to itself is not checked at all, however many times it is
compared.

The guard that would catch the class — every index-to-name mapping the
diff layer pins, checked against the type record — is **item 425** and is
the commander's to book. Named here; deliberately not built.

## No document changed, and that is the finding

Nothing under `docs/` said anything wrong, so nothing under `docs/` is
amended. Recorded explicitly so the next reader does not go looking for the
specification that was misled: there wasn't one. `docs/AI.md` §48.4, which
recorded this in item 414 as a thing found and not fixed, is now answered
by this item rather than struck — and the reasoning it gives for not
fixing it in place, "three pinned constants across three items", was
simply wrong: it is one constant and one assertion key. I wrote that
estimate without counting, in a document, and it stood for a day.
