## Step 1: the scan, as an instrument

935 had scanned by hand, and §4 had scanned on 2026-09-01 with a script
that is not in the repo (`emu-dead-scan` is named in §7 and exists
nowhere). That made this the third time, so it is now a verb:
`tools/trace/report.py <exe> refs ADDR|NAME … [-]`. For each target it
looks for:

- every `call`, `jmp` and `jcc rel32` in `.text` whose target is the
  entry, found at every byte offset rather than by an instruction walk;
- every `rel8` jump from the 128 bytes either side, from outside the
  function;
- every four-byte copy of the address, and of its RVA, at any offset of
  any section, with a `.rdata` hit named as its vtable slot from the
  export's `vtables.txt`.

Because the opcodes are found at any byte offset, a hit can be bytes
inside another instruction. So each jump site is then checked against
llvm-objdump's listing of the function that holds it. An RVA hit whose
word is itself another function's address is printed as an alias and
not counted. The byte before the entry is printed too, because a
padding byte (`0xcc`) rules out a fall-through.

It found both of 935's referenced controls exactly:
`LeaderData::get_handicap`'s four callers and `MapGrass::make_continents`'s
vtable slot. It also turned up two lookalikes the listing settles:

- a `js` byte six before `PathFinderData::get_estimate` is the middle of
  a `cmpb` in `find_node_open`;
- `cos_table`'s RVA occurs six times in `.rdata`, and each time it is
  `ObjectData::attack`'s own address in a vtable slot.
