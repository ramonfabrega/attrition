## What was booked, and what it turned out to be

The brief was eight rows of `1/28` on block 10818 — `collide` 1 against
0, `collide_who` 8 against −1, and the stand and animation clock under
them — with the draw stream agreeing on the same frame: ours three draws
against two, the extra `Guy::set_anim+0x97a < Unit::move_step+0x823`. It
named no mechanism and said so; the mechanism named in a brief had been
wrong eight times in this chain.

Ninth, and the *booking's own framing* was the thing that was wrong. The
collision is real and it is a **consequence**. On 10818 this crate's
`1/28` stands at `(17208, 26952)` where the original's stands at
`(16838, 27194)` — 370 units east, 242 north, about fifteen frames of its
own walk — and the thing it collides with is the gaia animal `8/0`, which
sits at `(17278, 26821)` on **both** sides and does not move once in six
hundred blocks. A unit 370 units from where it belongs will find
something to stand on.

The block could not say that, and the reason is worth keeping: `1/28 pos`
first parted on **10235**, 583 blocks earlier, and a `firsts` map records
only a key's first parting. The word's own block shows the rows that
*open* on it, and a position that has been wrong since before the window
is not one of them. **Parked 477 and the word were one cause all along** —
which is exactly the thing the brief warned not to assume on proximity, so
it was established by measuring the chain rather than by arguing it.

## The order the evidence came in

**The animal first, because it is the cheapest falsification.** Track
`8/0` over `[10790, 10830]`: one position, `myspeed 11`, `collide 0`,
every block. Track this crate's: the same point to the unit. So the
collider is not what moved, and §8.2's "gaia is not filtered out of a
step" stays true. That left the walker.

**Then the walk, from its first frame rather than from the word.** Both
sides leave `(4776, 30168)` on sim-frame 10234 with the same facing
`1252851712`, the same `last_speed` 25, and the same `avg_speed` ramp
`6, 10, 13, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 22, 22` — identical to the number.
And the step parts on that first frame: ours `(25, 7)`, theirs `(24, 7)`.

**Then the sine table, backwards, which took five minutes and killed
half the search space.** A python port of `movement::sin_component` fed
that angle answers `(25, 7)` for a distance of **26** and `(24, 7)` for
**25**, both exactly. So the trig agrees to the bit, the angle agrees to
the bit, and the entire question is one scalar. `1/28`'s `myspeed` is 26.

**Then the scalar, solved frame by frame for all six raiders** over
`[10230, 10830]`, from the dump's own `guy.angle` and position deltas:
`1/27` (26) walks **25** on 491 blocks, `1/28` (26) walks 25 on 487 and
26 on exactly five, `1/29` (26) walks **29** on 456, and `1/40`, `1/41`,
`1/42` (25) walk 25. Group 65 is two squads, 26 and 25.

`UnitData::get_speed@00608720` has exactly one arm that can turn 26 into
25 — the group cap; everything else is a halving, a doubling or an
eighth. And **`1/29` is the control that makes it an argument rather than
an inference**: it holds an `ATTACK` in its order stack, so `get_action`
answers non-null, the cap's `local_8 == 0` fails, and the *same*
`local_8` sends it down the `× 9/8`. `26 × 9 / 8 = 29`. One predicate
explains both why one member of a group ignores the cap and why that
member walks faster than its own quoted speed.

## The half that grep called dead code

`Group::compute_speed` — the leader's `UnitData::speed`, set at
`add`/`kill`/`normalize`/`clear` — is *not* where a marching group's
number comes from, and believing it was would have been wrong in both
directions by about a frame and a half.

`Group::report_speed@00713bb0` and
`Group::leader_report_speed@007137f0` have **zero callers in the
48k-function export**, which reads exactly like dead code left over from
an earlier design. They are not: both are **inlined at their one call
site**, in `Unit::do_group_move@005e79a0` — the leader's at `:95`–`:97`
after its own `do_move` succeeds, the follower's at `:336`–`:338` in the
in-formation arm. Together they make a two-phase accumulator: `new_speed`
collects this pass's minimum, the leader's next step promotes it to
`speed` and restarts it at the leader's own **uncapped** speed.

That lag is not a detail — it is `1/28`'s five frames at 26, which are
the frames its slow squad is standing still and reporting nothing. With
both reporters in, this crate reproduces sim-frames 10234–10240 exactly:
position, heading and guy clock. `CLAUDE.md`'s "grep the callers of every
function you call once-only" earned its place again.

Two more shapes that read as bugs and are the original's: a group whose
`speed` is 0 can never be *lowered* (`report_speed`'s test is `<`), so
priming matters and `Group::normalize`'s tail is what does it; and
`copy_group@006fa690` copies `speed` without `new_speed`.
