## 1. The rule, before its count (`render::silhouette::Luma`, RENDERER §3.14)

- The pixels are the colour reader's own. An object's are those of its own key inside the traced footprint
  (`read_colours`' held pixels); a tile's are the ground row's. Dark pixels are **kept**, since dark is a
  brightness.
- Y = 0.3 r + 0.59 g + 0.11 b, the install's luminance weights (`LUMA`, already pinned by 4114). Each side's mean
  is floored at 8.
- A unit agrees when the larger mean is at most 1.2 times the smaller. An object is scored on 40 held pixels; a
  tile is scored as the ground row scores it, and a tile we draw no pixel of parts.
- The figures are counted twice: as drawn (`Colouring::Silhouette`), and under 4114's `lit_pipeline` at the clamp
  (`Colouring::Lit`). The lit count applies the team blend first, then the face's N·L against −`cur_dir`
  (4106's L), on every textured mesh pixel not behind. The pixels behind and the sprites stay as painted.
- Whichever count more objects agree with is drawn, in the painter and the client alike, if its colour row is not
  under 18. A tie keeps the drawn.

Why an object's mean, and not a share of pixels: a light changes the level of a whole object. A pixel-by-pixel
brightness test on a texture that is a pixel off reads the texture's registration, and the traced and colour rows
already read that.

## 3. The mountain's body on the client (`client::gpu`, `fs_mountain`)

The painter's body agrees and the still's does not, so the miss is in the client's own arithmetic. `upload_mips`
makes every texture `Rgba8UnormSrgb`, so a sample arrives linear. `fs_mountain`'s `t0 + t1 − ½` then ran in linear
space, where the card's stage and `MountainArt::over` run it on the stored bytes. Now the shader encodes both
samples, sums them, and decodes the result for the sRGB target to store. The still's boxes, before → after
against the photograph:
