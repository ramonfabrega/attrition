## The grep before the capture, and what it bought

Two things, both of which changed what run112 was for rather than being
discovered afterwards.

**The `AMMO` record is already parsed whole.** `crate::diff::ammo` reads all
twenty-seven of `AmmoData::log_data`'s fields, landed by item 402's widening
of run109 — so chapter two needed no new parser, and `AMMO=5` is exactly the
level that reader expects. Had I not looked I would have written one.

**Three captures on this disk already carry `AMMO` blocks**: run17 (173),
run109 (183), run29 (1). None of them can give a golden word — they are AI
games on a different script — but knowing they exist is what makes "373
blocks over 186 frames" a result rather than a novelty.

## The four predictions, and they held

Written into `chapter2.cmd`'s header before the run, because a golden chapter
takes no `captures.txt` stanza (run101–run105 have none; this lane is
`viadriver.sh golden_capture.sh`, not `runqueue.sh`) and the stanza's
`check:` lines have to live somewhere.

Eight `INFO cmd` records each returning 1. 296 window blocks, 605..899
contiguous, 901 the quit block. Nine units, three per `add`, at 611, 616 and
621. And the chapter's own falsifier, the one it exists for: **373 `AMMO`
blocks over 186 frames**, first at 645 — no arrow at all would have said the
ranged arm was never entered.

The other two falsifiers did not fire either. No `ATTACKORDER` on the Bowmen
at 611. The first arrows are `0/6` and `0/7` on `1/8` at 4.95 and 4.04 tiles,
inside a ten-tile reach rather than beyond it.

## The first take reported a word of 0, and it was the setup's

I gave `--detail end:` and `--detail misc:` and no `--detail start:`,
copying run101/run103's line rather than run105's. The capture **succeeded** —
901 frames, every command run, the same 373 `AMMO` blocks — and its
`[Start Game]` block held 0 `LEADERDATA` and 0 `UNITDATA` where run105 holds
4 and 52.

`borrow_from_siblings` lends a capture the map it could not print for itself,
so the harness stood *something* up, walked it, and said the word parts at
frame 0: 80 draws against the trace's 120. That is a number, it is in the
right format, and it is the setup's rather than the simulation's. It is the
third time this repo has produced one — `crate::diff::endpoint`'s run28 read
12 units of 71 in a region-less world, and item 364's first golden word of 7
was the borrowed frame stream correcting itself — and the common shape is
that **a wrong setup does not fail, it scores**.

So the fix is a refusal rather than a note. `walk_chapter` now asserts the
start block carries leaders and units, and prints run105's `start:` line as
the remedy. It refused the bad capture by name before I re-ran anything,
which is how I know it works. Every chapter file's recipe carries the line
now, and chapter one's says it is not optional.

The re-take reproduced every check exactly — same eight commands, same 296
blocks, same 373 `AMMO` records over the same 186 frames beginning at 645,
same unit counts on the same frames. Two launches of one script are one
game, measured again on a second script.

**A second defect on the way in**: a relative `--cmd-file` cannot work,
because `golden_capture.sh` cds into `tools/explore` before exec'ing the
runner, so `docs/RUNS.md`'s own recipe resolved to
`tools/explore/tools/gamelog/...` and the runner refused before the game
launched. Fixed in the script — a relative path is now resolved against the
repo root — rather than in the recipe, so the recipe stays the one a reader
would type. No game was launched, so nothing needed restoring.

## The word is 616, and the value diff found what the draw stream did not

The draw stream parts at **616**: ours 26 draws, theirs 25, and the extra one
is `Unit::fight+0x9b0`, the one-in-five re-search, spent on the frame the
hoplite squad appears. Sequence parts on the same frame, values at 617.

I wrote down "no position disagrees at all — the word is a pure draw-count
divergence" and turned it into an assertion, and **the assertion failed**.
That is the whole argument for the rule it was obeying.

The dump does hold all six combatants stock still at 615–618. But
`add hoplite who=1 12,40` asks for tile 12, internal 2400; the original seats
the squad at 2424, 2568, 2472 — a clean `+24` on the captain — and this crate
seats it at 2284, 2428, 2332, **uniformly 140 units west on all three
figures**. The Bowmen, the chapter's *first* `add`, are exact, which is what
makes the second one a finding rather than noise: same ring, same frame, one
squad right and one 140 out.

That is `find_nearby_spot` on **clear ground**, and it is precisely what
chapter one cannot test. Chapter one's second `add` asks for a point one tile
from the first squad, so item 379 fitted the ring where the near ground was
already taken. Here the nearest other unit is seven tiles away and the
asked-for spot is empty, and the original simply takes it.

Pinned, not fixed. Item 379 already said a change to that function belongs in
an item that re-runs the pins it is on, and it is on every production path.
What the pin buys is a cause for 616 to test instead of a mechanism to guess:
ours stand 1252 units from the bowmen where the original's stand 1392, 6.5
tiles against 7.25, so a re-search predicate keyed on range is the first
thing the successor should look at. The frame is 616 either way.

## What this chapter cost and what it returned

One item, two launches, five minutes of game time. It returned a second
pinned word, a new record family in the golden lane, a guard against a whole
class of false numbers, two tool defects fixed, and a clear-ground
counter-example to the seating model that six captures of chapter one could
not produce. The second chapter earning more than the first is not the
expected shape and is worth saying out loud.
