## The order of work

1. **The cities line, red first.** I decoded the stream's 6 ranges by `CityData`'s type record. The stream corrects
   §8 on one point: each city's `vans` header follows its own two ranges. It is not one array after the last city.
   The image was written from the city record, the owner's `CityAi`, the economy's enhancer bytes and the world's
   region numbering. The test was run first on an empty image, and all 6 of 6 ranges parted. The first real image
   agreed on all 228 bytes.
2. **The goods line.** It is the items record over the goods array, with `ever_seen` from `territory.good_seen_bits`.
   179 of 180 ranges agree. Good 15 parts: ours 0, theirs 2.
3. **The byte's writers.** I grepped the export for every store at +0x20 of a goods-list pointer and every
   `|= 1 << who`. There are five writers (`docs/CHECKSUM.md` §13): `Good::init`, `reveal_fog`'s rare arm,
   `compute_reg_territory`'s goods scan, the census's step 9, and `Animal::work`'s herd arm. This crate carries the
   two arms beside `new_rare`.
4. **Why ours lacks it: probed.** On our frame 91 leader 1's `seen2` holds good 15's half-cell and leader 1 owns its
   cell. But `new_rares[1]` is empty and `known_rares` is 0, where the original's `LEADERDATA` prints
   `known_rares 1`. The starting city's `update_seen_ally` writes `seen2` without `reveal_fog`. The original's setup
   `compute_all_territory` then meets the rare. Our wholesale setup pass runs only the oil half. Stepping the walk on,
   ours sets the bit on **frame 387**, at its first in-play border pass.
5. **The line is held out.** Porting setup's goods scan would change the AI's `new_rares` on the minted walk, which
   is a mechanic in its own right. The goods test pins the held-out byte both ways.
