## The mechanism

`Roads::add_roads@0088f4b0` runs the texture marks twice. Item 4677 built the second run, inside
`redo_changed_roads`. The first is `add_roads`' own second pass over the tiles it lays (`add_roads:334`–`338`):
`mark_splits(1)`, `mark_white_lines(1)`, `mark_yellow_lines(1)`, `mark_intersections`, `fixup_lines(1)`. That pass
was empty in `SimRoads`. A tile laid into a gap, joined both ways on the call that lays it, takes its bits there.
It is never redone after, because the redo lists hold its neighbours. The sim test lays (10..12, 10), then (14, 10),
then (13, 10). It asserts 13's `inter` is `0x10`. Made to fail: with the hook empty again it read 0 (red), then 16
restored.

A temporary trace (an env-gated `eprintln!` in `RoadMesh::mark_intersections`, the redo's `clear_flags`, `seed` and
`road_changed`'s fresh element, with an `add_roads` call counter) printed each tile's history. It is not kept; the

- **run1559b, 28 apart: 27 are seeded.** Every one of (12, 157), (13–15, 156), (14, 163), (19, 158–163),

- **run1814b, (15, 155)**: `add_roads` call 96 lays it and marks it `S`. In the same call's redo, (14, 155)
  (`E|S|W`, road east and west, none north) runs `mark_splits`. Its `S|W` arm joins east and zeroes (15, 155)'s
  low byte (was `04`). Nothing redoes (15, 155) after. In the original `mark_splits` pushes a neighbour onto the
  lists only on a fresh join, and (15, 155) already holds `W`. So the redo that restores its `S` must come from a
  line pass's push: `mark_white_lines`, `mark_yellow_lines`, `fixup_lines` and `delete_straglers` all
  `add_unique` a neighbour whose line bits they change. That is 4677a's build.
