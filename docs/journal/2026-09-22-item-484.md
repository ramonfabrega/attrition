# 484 — the hit-point record, and five rows nobody was looking at

**Booked by** item 481's own report: `crate::diff::compare` carries no
hit-point row at all, so chapter two's widening could see that the
original's hoplite `1/8` was *gone* on block 684 and not that it had
been wounded on the way there. It gates 485, the death at 684.

The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` **§40** — 483 is live and holds §39,
so I took the next one, as the brief said to. It is a combat claim
rather than a format one: the format half (the indent, the block) is
one guard test and is cited from §40.1.

## The field was not merely uncompared — one of three was never parsed

`myhits` and `damage` came off the `OBJECT` block correctly and had
since item 394. **`damage_frac` did not exist on `UnitDump` at all.**
It was parsed for a building from the same item and never for a
figure, so on every capture ever taken the sixteenths that decide
*which frame* a unit's next whole hit point lands on were invisible.

That is item 478's shape one record over, and it is now twice in one
chain: when a field is quiet everywhere, suspect the parser.

The guard came first and was made to fail both ways before landing —
`the_unit_s_hit_points_are_the_object_block_s`, with a decoy
`damage`/`myhits`/`damage_frac` at `UNITDATA`'s indent (`7/700/70`)
*and* inside `SUBOBJECT` (`9/900/90`). A reader that walks out one
level reddens on `Some(700)`, one that walks in on `Some(900)`; both
on the value, never on `None`, which a capture without decoys cannot
do.

## `hits_left` is not a fourth row, and that is deliberate

The brief named three fields — `myhits`, `damage`, `hits_left`. What
landed is `myhits`, `damage`, `damage_frac`: the three the record
actually writes.

`hits_left` is `myhits − damage` on both sides. This crate keeps the
complement of the dump's accumulator — `health` is the squad-sized
`myhits` with this figure's damage already taken off — so a
`hits_left` row parts exactly when `myhits` or `damage` does and
prints every disagreement twice. `run100_s_word_block_is_every_record_
the_dump_carries` reads it that way because it has no `damage` row of
its own; `compare` has one, so it does not need the derived figure.
`damage` is also the better row to read: it is the dump's own number,
and it is the one §38.4 quotes.

## Three ways for one window to agree because it is not looking

`chapter_two_s_word_frame_is_widened_whole` has now been wrong in all
three:

- its **floor** sat just under the word and hid `order 0/10` at 630
  (item 470);
- its **ceiling** sat four frames over it and hid `extra 1/8` at 684
  (item 481);
- and a **field** was missing from the comparator, which hid five rows
  from 656 (this item).

The first two are about the window and the third is not. No widening
of the window could have found it, which is the argument for
`crate::ledger` and for "diff the whole record": the window can be
perfect and the comparison still blind.

not one: 16,362 comparisons **and 580 of them on a unit-frame the dump
wounds**. Corrupting `myhits` by one reddens the assertion on all 21
of the chapter's units at their birth blocks; silencing the comparison
reddens it on `0 hit-point comparisons`. Both were run before landing.

## What it cost

No capture, no reading, no probe. One `track.py` pass over the golden
dump to read the record's shape, one parser line, one guard, one
comparison block, and four runs to attribute what moved. The expensive
part was the full release suite, which was green.
