# 485 — the arrow left the wrong square

**Booked by** item 484's §40.6: `1/8`'s wound ladder runs one arrival
behind the dump's from block **656**, every value this crate holds is
the dump's own previous one, and 484's hypothesis for it was §7.3's
`uber_size` divide. The brief said to re-measure before accepting it —
"the eleventh named mechanism on this map's residues; nine of the first
ten were wrong while their frames held".

It was the eleventh too. The frame was right and the mechanism was
half-right in the wrong place: the divide is real and it is **684**'s
fact, and 656's is that our arrows launched from the shooter's own
square. The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` **§41**.

## The first twenty minutes went on my own reader

I wrote a Python parser for the dump's `UNITDATA` blocks — they have no
`END` lines, so records are delimited by indentation — and flushed each
record when the *next* `BEGIN UNITDATA` appeared. Which means every
block's **last** record landed in the next block, and chapter two's
last record is `1/8`: the one unit the whole item is about. My table
disagreed with §40.4 by exactly one block and for a while I thought
§40.4 was wrong.

The brief's own warning is what saved it — "if something about these
rows looks impossible, suspect the new reader before the simulation" —
and the thing to suspect turned out to be *mine*, not `compare`'s. Cost
twenty minutes. The parser now flushes on `BEGIN FRAME` too, and that
fix is commented in the scratch script because the shape will recur.

## `recharging` was the whole first half of the answer

Before naming a mechanism I widened what 484 had not: `UnitData` prints
four fields at its own indent that nothing compared — `recharging`, and
the overkill window `damage_frame`/`damage_o`/`damage_who`. One landing,
two questions answered:

- **`recharging` parts nowhere** on 21 units over 81 blocks. So the
  swing frames are the original's and the cadence was never the fault.
  That killed four hypotheses at once (a late `fight`, a wrong
  `RECHARGE`, the `(o + frame) % 16` phase the brief suggested, the
  one-frame `pending_attack` deferral §6.2 models).
- **`damage_frame` parts by exactly one** on both wounded units. The
  dump dates its own hits; 484 had to read the frame off the
  accumulator.

`damage_who` had no field in `combat::State` at all. `do_damage` step 2
writes the pair and this crate kept the captain alone — the same shape
as 484's unparsed `damage_frac`, one level over again.

## And then the capture had the answer printed in it

`tools/gamelog/golden/chapter2.cmd`'s own command line asks for
`--detail end:…,AMMO=5,…`. run112 has carried **373 `BEGIN AMMO`
records** since the day it was taken, with each arrow's `sx, sy, sz`,
`cur_time` and `total_time`, and nothing had ever read one. `grep -o
"BEGIN [A-Z_]*" | sort | uniq -c` on the dump is what found them, which
is `CLAUDE.md`'s "grep the dump before booking a reading" paying for
itself in about ninety seconds.

The nine arrows of the window say three things at once: the launch
**frames** are the original's arrow for arrow (so §9.0's animation model
is confirmed on a second piece pair), the launch **points** are not, and
`total_time` is one too big on four of the nine and right on two.

**The two that were right are why this looked like arithmetic.** An
extra eighty units of distance crosses a whole frame at `proj_speed`
100 most of the time and not always, so two impacts landed on the
dump's own frame while the ladder was wrong. I spent half an hour
trying to fit a single constant offset to the *impact* frames and got a
contradiction, because I was measuring against the dump's post-lead
`ex, ey` and our pre-lead landing. An impact frame that agrees is not an
arrow that flew right.

## Four wrong turns worth writing down

1. **The parser's off-by-one** above.
2. **"The launch frame is one late."** It fits six of eight impacts and
   I nearly built on it. The `AMMO` record's `cur_time 1` block killed
   it outright.
3. **"`total_time` is one too big because `proj_speed` is wrong."** I
   solved for a single `d` per type and got an empty intersection for
   the slingers — a real falsification, and it was only empty because
   the distances I was using included the lead.
4. **"§22's bow-hand offset points backwards."** It does not; the
   offsets are all forward, eighty to a hundred and twenty-five units,
   and the sign was measurable directly off `sx - guy.x` once I had the
   record.

All four were cheap because each was checked against a printed field
rather than a reading. The expensive one was the one I checked against
my own arithmetic.

## What the death cost, and what it exposed

§7.3's divide is a one-line fix and `combat::share` was **already
written, documented and never called**. Our `1/8` reached `damage` 51
with `killed: false`; the original's died at its fortieth point. With
the share as the threshold it dies on the original's own block and
`extra 1/8` closes.

Fixing it made the word's 683 a *different* 683, and both halves are
named now with an address each: `0060fb06` is inside
`Unit::close@0060ee50` and it is the death animation's own
`Random::get` — `DEATH_OBJS`' `cur_anim 17` forces `dtype 2` and
`roll % 2 == 0`, so the dump confirms the outcome and not just the site
— and our own extra pair is §39's puncture, spent because an arrow
whose target died in flight lands on nothing here where the original
**holds** it: the dump's `1/7` takes `19+5` on frame 685, two arrows'
worth, two frames after they should have landed. §9.2's own sentence,
"a target that is no longer active has its `hold_frames` bumped", is the
mechanism, and `hold_frames` is one of the two fields the ledger still
counts as uncompared on `UnitDump`.

I did not chase either. Both are named by their frame and by a printed
field, which is the shape a successor item is supposed to have.
