## The answer

**The term is `val`, through `gfree[KNOWLEDGE]`. Correcting it alone costs
925 frames, so it is not landed.**

`create_units`' scholar arm reaches the free-slot count through
`City::count_gather_slots`, which writes `free[g] += gather_max −
BuildData::num_gatherers(0, 0)`. `num_gatherers` is **two counts, not
one**: the `gather_down` chain, **plus** `count_inside` for the two kinds
whose workers sit inside — the University (scholars) and the Oil Platform
(citizens), and not the Oil Well. This crate's `count_gather_slots` adds
the chain's raw length, so a University's seated scholars never reduced
its free slots and both of the AI's cities answered `k = 7`.

Fix that one call and `k` becomes **6** and **3**, which is what the
original's own `inside_down` chains say: one unit seated in the University
of its city 1, four in the University of its city 0. The per-city term
goes live. The word goes **9510 → 8585**.

## Why it goes down

Because the two defects were **cancelling**, and that is the finding.

With the over-large `gfree`, this crate's `val` overflowed and §45's guard
clamped it to 9,999,999 — and the original happens to clamp on block 9375
too. **We were agreeing there by accident.** Correct `gfree` and the
crossover inverts: at 9375 we give 7,200,000 where the original clamps, at
9381 we clamp where the original gives 5,755,741.

Nothing in the diff could see that, because both sides were internally
consistent and a saturating clamp erases the distance between "correct"
and "enormous". A green frame is evidence that the outputs match, never
that the inputs do — and where an arm ends in a clamp, a matching output
is barely evidence at all.

Post-correction this crate's `val` is 36,000,000 and 18,000,000 against
the original's 5,755,741 and 4,891,136, and our ratio is **2** where the
original's is **1.1768**. So `val ∝ k` is not how the original's per-city
variation works either. At least one more term in that arm is wrong, the
read did not settle it, and the pair has to land together.

## Three method notes

**Check the subtraction before the thing being subtracted.** The arm is
`k = gfree − <call>(1, 0x34)`, and the neighbouring `num_inside` made the
call look like an occupancy count. Vtable `+0x190` on `BuildData` is
`count_queue`, so ours was right and the twenty minutes spent proving it
are what pointed at `gfree` instead of at a rewrite of the wrong half.
Same shape as 432, where checking `make_me`'s two comparison senses is
what redirected the whole item.

**Before deriving a quantity, grep the tree for it.** `Sim::num_gatherers`
already implemented that function, both inside arms included, since
`docs/ORDERS.md` §6.1 — only this one call site asked the wrong question.
That is the third instance in a day, after `run97`'s green queue assertion
and 432's own instrument, and it is a different failure from a missing
implementation: **no test catches it, because both call sites are
internally consistent.** Same family as 423's self-consistent label.

**I caught myself fitting terms to the two numbers three times and
stopped each time.** Two values and a ratio are exactly the conditions
under which a plausible factor appears: 20/17 came out at 1.176 against a
target of 1.1768 and looked like something. None of the three is recorded
anywhere, because §49.2's lesson is that a number downstream of a wrap or
a clamp may have no arithmetic relationship to its inputs at all — and the
day's record says a fitted term would have survived several items before
dying. The brief's constraint was the right one and it earned its place.
