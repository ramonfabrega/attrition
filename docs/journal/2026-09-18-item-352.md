## 2026-09-18 — item 352: the AI musters where neither city says (Opus 5)

The brief asked one question — what is cell (58, 29)? — and said to ask that
before asking which function produced it. That order is the whole item. The
answer came out of arithmetic over a record that names no army, and then the
two predicates behind it fell out of one `grep` and one `llvm-objdump`.

### The muster cell is readable off a `GROUPATTACKTOORDER`

run97 block 8443 gives all nine members of group 64 an order whose
`MoveOrder::orig_x/orig_y` is **(44851, 22480)**. `orig` is the group's own
destination before each unit's formation slot, and §8's `do_forming` builds
it as `cell_centre(muster)` stepped `num_groups × 0xc0` along
`muster_angle` — with §12's tail having already added `0x80000000`, so the
point sits one tile *back* from the muster's centre.

`|(44851, 22480) − cell_centre(58, 29)| = 192.06`. One tile. So the sweep
was worth writing: over every centre `(ax, ay)` and every muster cell
`(mx, my)` in the AI's quarter of the map, and both angle senses, exactly two
pairs reproduce the point and both name **muster (58, 29)** — the second is
the colinear centre (52, 15), the same direction. A residual of one tile
pins a cell uniquely, which is what makes an unrecorded field legible.

And the centre that solves it is (55, 22) — `cell(target.x, target.y +
0x300)` for **London**, which this crate already had. So the target was
never in question and §18's "either the winner or §13's ring" resolved to
the ring on the first measurement.

### The ring: strict `>`, so the first maximum wins

The instrumented walk printed the whole of rings 7 and 8 around (55, 22).
Six candidates tied at the maximum 2,304 — `(50, 27)`, then `(53, 29)`
through `(58, 29)` — and `local_3c < local_18` (`6f6720`, `jle`) is strictly
greater, so the earliest tie wins: `(50, 27)`, entry 10. The original takes
entry 26. Two things had to be true at once for that, and the ring's score
said which:

**The score has to be able to separate two open cells.** It can only do that
through `BUILDING 0x4000`, and the crate never wrote it. `grep`ping the
decompile for `| 0x4000` found `BuildType::mask_me@006312a0`'s **first**
statement — `cells[y / 0x300 * xs + x / 0x300].flags |= 0x4000` on the mark,
`&= 0xbfff` on the unmark — and `docs/CITIES.md` §3.6 had had it in prose
since the first reading. run34's start block is the confirmation from the
other side: thirteen cells carry the bit and each is a starting building's.

With it, ring 7's `y = 29` row scores 1792, 1280, 1024, 1536, 1792, **2048**
from x = 53 to 58, off the AI's own buildings at (54, 29), (55, 29),
(54, 30), (55, 30), (56, 30) and (58, 30). The easternmost cell — the one
with a single building beside it — is the row's best, and that is the
original's answer. An army musters clear of its own town, and this flag is
the entirety of why.

**And `(50, 27)` has to go.** It scores 2,304 and comes first. Nothing in
the terrain excludes it: run34's own cells put its whole 3 × 3 on plain
`BASELAND`, region 1, owner 1, and terrain does not become worse over a
game. What excludes it is the spacing rule, one cell wide:

```
6f6335: call vector_dist
6f633a: cmp  $0x4, %eax
6f633d: jle  0x6f6714        # <= 4 is too near, not < 4
```

The decompiler prints that pair as `SBORROW4(iVar13,4) != iVar5 < 0`
alongside `iVar13 == 4`, which reads as `< 4` if the equality arm is missed
— and the sim, and §13, and a unit test whose own comment said "(9, 18) is
at four", all had `<`. The enemy arm at `6f6313` is the same `jle` on `2`.

The consequence compounds through the pool. Army 1/2's ring had a candidate
exactly four cells from army 1/1's muster and kept it; with the bound right
that army moves to (51, 26) instead, one cell from (50, 27) — so (50, 27) is
out of army 1/1's ring and (58, 29) is its best.

**Neither fix alone arrives.** Measured, not predicted: with only the
spacing bound the muster is still (50, 27); with only the flag it is
(55, 29). That is the make-it-fail for both halves, and it is in the test's
own doc comment.

### Two captures had the answer already, and the gate found them

The gate's own reds were the confirmation this item had written off as
unavailable. Both are world cell-for-cell diffs nobody was thinking about:

- **run13 frame 95** pinned `vec![(52, 22, 0x200, 0x4200)]` as its *one*
  cell-flag exception over 3,600 cells, with the comment "the one `BUILDING`
  bit nothing here sets". It now has **none**, and on run10's map rather
  than Great Lakes'.
- **run93 block 7932** — the only mid-game world dump on disk for Great
  Lakes, 7,932 frames and every building the AI has started — parted on 27
  cells and now parts on **12**. The 15 that went are the whole `0x4000`
  half; what is left is a blocked/solid count one or two short in the same
  base. Its 171 differing **tile** masks are unmoved, which is the other
  half of the claim: the bit is a cell bit and not a tile one.

So the `mask_me` half of this item is diff-backed from two captures and one
map it was not taken on, and §18's "no capture on disk" was wrong when
written. The lesson is the working agreement's own: the gate is a widening
that runs itself, and a claim about what no run reaches should be checked
against the gate before it is written down.
