The booking: on frame 3279 at run1559b's camera, on the client's default draw (buildings as their `texture=`
sprites, item 4076), candidate C read 14 of 23 under the mask, with the capital out of the row and the woodcutter
and four figures beside it low. The hypothesis was to trace the buildings on the photograph as 4071 traced the

## The order of work, by SHA

1. **`cc32bcf88`, the rule**, in `docs/RENDERER.md` §5.3, "A traced region", before any trace or count. One rule
   for every traced object, tanks and figures included. An object's window and ring hold four kinds of pixel:
   - under an `INTERFACE` rectangle: neither ground nor footprint;
   - inside its own trace: its footprint;
   - inside a neighbour's trace: neither;
   - anywhere else: read by colour, with the ground sampled from the ring's pixels of that last kind.

   An object is scored when its ring is inside the frame and 40 or more of its pixels lie outside the interface.
2. **`5516b8a5e`, the traces**, committed with nothing that counts with them: `client::silhouette::TRACED` gains
   0/2000 and 0/2001, and `PLAZA` holds the capital's other reading. I traced on 2× and 4× crops of the photograph
   alone, with a 10-pixel grid, and checked each trace by drawing its outline back over the photograph. I never
   looked at our draw. The crops and overlays are `~/ron-data/lab-experiments/2026-11-10-item-4077/` (`wood4.png`,
   `wood-ov.png`, `cap2.png`, `cap-ov.png`).
3. **`895b43603`, the reader** (`render::silhouette::footprints_traced`, `read_traced`, `Trace`, `Neighbour`,
   `Interface`, `ground_where`), the client's `score_frame_traced`, and the pins.

## Two rules were defensible, and so was a second trace

I chose each one by what the photograph can know, and report the count under every reading:

| reading | why it is or is not the rule | count |
|---|---|---|
| **a neighbour's trace is neither; the interface is neither** (the rule) | The trace says whose a pixel is, so counting it under a neighbour scores the traced object a second time under the neighbour's name. The photograph cannot know what lies under its interface, so those pixels are unknown, not grounds to drop the object. | **27 of 33** |
| a neighbour's trace is the scene | This is what the whole-scene footprint would say, but it scores the building again in each figure's window. | 27 of 33 |
| the interface skips the object, as 4038 to 4076 did | This throws away what the photograph does show of the object. | 19 of 23 (the capital out) |
| the plaza traced as the capital's | The paved diamond lies flat on the terrain plane, as ground does, and the photograph cannot say whether a flat surface is the sprite's or the terrain's. I left it out of the rule's trace. | 27 of 33 (0/2000 0.818) |

The interface rule is most of the change in the denominator: ten objects whose rings met a rectangle are now
scored, and eight of them pass.
