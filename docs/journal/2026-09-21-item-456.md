# Item 456 — which arm refused, and the answer was a proxy nobody added for it

*2026-09-21, Opus 5, `worktree-att-456`. run116, the first
`RON_COLLIDE_PROBE` capture. Great Lakes' long word **10,161 →
10,232**. `docs/COLLISION.md` §9, `docs/AI.md` §54.4 amended,
`docs/RUNS.md` run116.*

## The brief's falsifier, and honouring it

It was written before the run and it was worth having: the bracket must
show one call on 10161 returning 1, and the site inside it must name a
`who/o`. Both held on the first pass, which meant the rest of the item
was arithmetic rather than archaeology.

The "name a `who/o`" clause is the part that shaped the instrument.
Three of the five sites are `__thiscall` on a `UnitData *` and their
arguments name no object — `is_here`'s two are *pointers* to the hit
cell — so a straight proxy build would have produced a log of heap
addresses that could not be put beside a dump keyed on `(who, o)`. The
build therefore emits one record of its own, INFO 15, reading `o` and
`who` off `+0xa`/`+0x9`. Those offsets are not a guess:
`will_be_corner@00609fa0` indexes `units[who][o]` with exactly them, in
its own prologue.

The other shaping decision was `will_be_corner`. `collide_here` carries
the hit cell behind two out pointers, which the proxy mechanism cannot
read; `will_be_corner` is handed the same cell **as values**, and it is
called once and only on a successful probe — so its presence is
`collide_here != 0` and its arguments are the answer. That is the whole
reason it is a site.

## The measurement that nearly went wrong

My first probe of this crate's own sweep read it at the **start** of
frame 10161 and answered `(892, 469)` where the original answers `(892,
471)`. That reads as "the occupancy bitmask", and I spent twenty
minutes tracing a single bit's history before instrumenting the real
call instead.

It was an artefact of *when* I looked. `1/33` steps earlier in the same
frame, vacates unit cell `(893, 469)`, and `move_unit`'s clear pass
takes the column `x = 892` with it; `1/38` then probes a grid that no
longer has the bit. Read at the asking unit's own step, the two sweeps
agree on the cell exactly.

The lesson is the same one 448 wrote down one level up. 448: a key that
collapses a record's fields hides all but the first. This one: **a
sweep compared at a frame boundary and a sweep compared where the
original's bracket sits are different measurements**, and the cheap one
names the wrong mechanism. The fix was to make the diagnostic run
inside `detect_unit_collision` itself, which is also why
`Sim::sweep_verdict` calls the simulation's own `scan_colliders` with a
recording sink rather than being a second copy of the walk.

## Eliminating three clauses with the dump

With the cell, the corner codes and the walk order all agreeing, the
word was §4.3's group arm and one of its four clauses. Two of them turn
on `UnitData::action_type`, and both were settled by a field the dump
already prints: the orders' `flags 5`. `get_action@00608450` returns the
first order that is not moving *or* carries `flags & 4`, so with one
order on the list and bit 2 set, the action **is** that order —
`1/38`'s is `GROUP_ATTACK_TO` (21), not `ATTACK` (10), and `1/31`'s is
`ATTACK_TO` (2), not 10. Both clauses pass. The group is 64 on both.

That left `+0x104`, which the PDB calls `openlist`.

## The answer came from a proxy nobody added for it

`PathFinder::astar_path` is one of the eight *base* sites, in every
build since the pathfinder items. On 10161 two of its calls return −1 —
the suspend — and resume on 10162. Its `stack` argument is
`Stack<PathData> *`, which is `UnitData +0xb8`; subtract the offset,
look the pointer up in the INFO 15 table, and it is `1/31` and `1/32`.

So a capture booked to read `detect_unit_collision` answered its
question out of a proxy that has been in the instrument for weeks,
because the identity record made an unrelated site's pointer
resolvable. That is the argument for the record, and I would not have
predicted it.

Two more witnesses agreed without a run: the dump's own `1/31
start_dist 1872` on block 10162 (§18.6's dumped witness of a suspend,
and this crate writes the same number), and this crate's own
`Unit::search.is_some()` at the moment `1/38` probes.

## The field was never missing

`Sim::same_group_soft` carried `SEAM: UnitData +0x104, the suspended
pathfinder search, which this crate does not keep — read as zero, which
widens the arm`. `Unit::search` **is** `+0x104..0x148` and has been
since `docs/PATHFINDER.md` §18. The seam comment outlived the field by
about a fortnight, and reading the clause as zero widened the arm by
exactly the units that are busy re-planning — which, in a squad walking
into each other, is most of them.

The fix is one clause and it is the *collider's* alone. The word moved
**10,161 → 10,232**, +71; the fifteen rows item 448 pinned on block
10162 go to nought; the endpoint at 24001 goes 53 → 48 `off` and 8 → 13
`unlinked`, 13,769 frames past the word.

The new word's own value diff is 53 rows on four units on block 10233:
`0/5 orders.len: ours 1 theirs 0` — the human's, an order the original
drops — and the squad `1/27`–`1/29` carrying a 44-entry path plan where
the original carries one hop. That is formation pathing, not collision.

## What I re-pinned, and one line I did not

`LONG_WORD_GREAT_LAKES`, the `WIDENINGS` row (item 448 → 456, and the
widening test's window moved with the word rather than being left
naming a block the word has left — parked 449's lesson applied at the
move), the endpoint's two counts, and both run100 word tests.

**`docs/QUEUE.md`'s scoreboard and `Long captures:` lines are the
commander's**, so they still say 10161 and the two guards that read them
are red. That is the half of the gate a worker is not meant to close.

Two small things the item found on the way and fixed because they were
under its hands: the widening test's `RON_DEBUG_ROWS` dump sat **after**
the assertion it exists to re-derive, so it only ever ran on a passing
test; and its sibling's `word block {}` label printed
`LONG_WORD_GREAT_LAKES + 1` for a row measured at
`GREAT_LAKES_SIXTH_SCHOLAR`.
