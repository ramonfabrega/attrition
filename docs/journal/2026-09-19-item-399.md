# Item 399 — who=1's army marches where the dump holds it still

*2026-09-19, Opus 5, the rules track. Golden word **624 → 621**, and the
value diff is why it lands anyway.*

## What the item asked, and what it turned out to be

The brief named a value divergence below the word: at golden frame 618 this
crate walked all three of who=1's hoplites toward `(38664, 13320)` and the
dump holds `1/6` on `(1368, 7992)`. It named the chain —
`Armies::emergency` → `Army::process(·, 1)` → `Group::action_siege_attack_to`
— said the emergency **is** reached (item 395's correction of item 391), and
said the divergence is inside the tick.

It was not inside the tick. **The emergency is not reached**, and 391 had
the right conclusion for the wrong reason while 395's correction was wrong.
That is the twelfth booked mechanism in three days to be wrong with the
frame right, and the thing that found it was the same thing that has found
most of them: widening the record.

## The widening, before anything else

`CLAUDE.md`'s rule — diff the whole record, every unit on the frame, not the
ones the brief names — paid immediately. Tabulating **all nine** of who=1's
units across blocks 616–626 rather than the three the item names turned up a
fact no document held: who=1 has **two** groups, not one. `1/6`, `1/7`, `1/8`
carry `group 64` with `form_mod -1`; `1/0`, the explorer standing a cell from
this crate's own muster centre, carries `group 65` with **`form_mod 50`** — a
live formation slot.

That killed two hypotheses in one read and set up a third that was also
wrong (that 65 was the army's group and the hoplites were orphaned). What it
established for certain was that the group topology was not what the crate
had, and that no record on disk could say which group belonged to the army.

## The capture, and the brief's own error

The item said "a chapter-one re-capture with `ARMY`+`GROUPS` under
`[End Frame]`". **`ARMY` is not an `[End Frame]` category**: there is no
such key in `gamelog.ini`, `GameLog::dump_armies@0092fc50` has no caller in
the export, and `full_dump@00930380`'s per-key list has no armies index —
`ArmiesData::log_data` is reached only from `dump_all`, i.e. `DUMP_ALL=1`,
run25–27's ~70 MB-a-frame shape. So the `GROUPS` half went first, because
`GroupData::log_data` prints both `army` (`+0x8`) and `order_num` (`+0x2c`)
and those two fields answer the question between them.

Forty-two seconds, 2.5 MB, `docs/RUNS.md` run110. Two failures on the way,
both cheap and both worth writing down:

- `golden_capture.sh` does `cd tools/explore` before exec'ing the runner, so
  `--cmd-file` must be **absolute**. The relative path died in a second.
- The first run came back with **zero** `GROUPDATA` and everything else
  present, with `DEATHS` already off — so the documented trap was not it.
  `GuyData::log_data@005de6c0` opens `set_type(0x14, 0)` and walks
  `set_detail(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)`, **`(4)`**, and `set_detail` is called
  whether or not the line is accepted. The last record `dump_units` emits is
  a `GUY` nested in the gaia players' `ANIMALDATA`, so the pool inherits
  `GUYS` at detail 4 and `check_accept` drops it at `GUYS=2`. **The rule is
  not "DEATHS off": the category the pool inherits must be set at or above
  that dumper's highest `set_detail`.** `GUYS=9` — which run31 and run92
  happened to use — and 10,240 records came out.

## The mechanism

`Object::do_damage@0064a480:951`:

```
if (local_30 != 0 && (leaders[param_2].leader_flags & 4) == 0)
    Armies::emergency(param_2)
```

395's gate is the **second** conjunct and it is real: `param_2` is the
victim's owner (`do_damage(A, o, who, …)`), the bit is
`LeaderData::is_human@006ec170`, and the golden dump's own `LEADERDATA`
blocks confirm who is who — who=0 `leader_flags 0x7` (human), who=1
`0x800013` (computer). So it passes at 618.

**`local_30` is the first conjunct**, and it has exactly two writers between
its `= 0` at `0064a8dc` and the test, both inside the **building** branch —
the arm that resolves the target through vslot `0xac` to a `BuildData` and
reads `+0x72 city` — and both beside a city alarm:
`S_CITY_BEING_ATTACKED` and `S_YOUR_CAPITAL_ATTACKED`. So **the emergency
means "a city of mine is under attack"**, and a soldier taking a hit never
reaches it. Run24's 12129, this entry's original evidence, is a blow on the
AI's **capital**.

One reading trap on the way, worth a line: pairing `leader_flags` with the
wrong leader. `LeaderData::log_data` prints the flags **before** opening
`BEGIN LEADERDATA`, so a naive "first `leader_flags` after the `who` line"
read is off by one — and it reads as the human bit being on the computer and
off the human, which is exactly the shape of a real finding. `gamelog.rs`'s
parser had the zip right and its comment says so.

The commander's call, asked and answered mid-item: land it. A word of 624
