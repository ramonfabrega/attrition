**The water's red, worked through `ps_global.hlsl` on the card's own view.** With the vertex's red byte at 0,
the lit branch's `color^1.45 · (diffuse + ambient)` is 0 in red. All the red the water gives is `specular +
reflection`. The view matrix's third column puts the water's normal at (0, 0.866, −0.5). L is (0.5, 0.362, −0.787),
and V = −normalize(ViewPos) is (0, 0, −1) to within 4° across the screen, since z ≈ 67,066. So N·H is 0.638. Beckmann at
roughness 0.3 is then `exp(−16.2) / (π · 0.09 · 0.166)` ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶. At `gSpecular` 40 and HDR 5, that is about 10⁻³,
under a count. The reflection's reach (§3.18) is a floor within about 6 world units of the surface, and the nine's
corners are at −112 to −218. So the water adds no red over the nine, in theirs or in ours.

**What theirs asks for.** Tile (200, 181) through `ps_tonemap.hlsl`'s curve, at the floor's white
(`1 / tonemap(1.4 · 3.5)` = 1.775). Theirs is (71, 134, 156) and ours (56, 134, 148). That is HDR (0.653, 1.50, 1.89)
against (0.497, 1.50, 1.74), a difference of **(+0.156, 0, +0.15)**. The water's own red is 0, so the extra red has
to come from what lies under it, and seen through `1 − a` that is about +0.4 HDR. But green does not move. No mix of the
layers on the card can give that. Sand (0.97, 0.78, 0.45) and coral (0.16, 0.78, 0.83) each carry as much green as
red or blue, and a lighter or darker water alpha moves green and blue in opposite directions. **So the remainder is not a
layer's weight.** An HDR term added equally to every channel, such as a sheen or a reflection, would raise red the most
at this level, because the curve is steepest there. It would also raise green by about 9. Theirs does not.
