## 2026-09-18 — item 350: a pushed group takes its members out of the army (Opus 5)

The brief said 8619 was one extra `Unit::do_idle` draw on `1/39`, that an
idle draw is a **state** question before it is a function question, and that
run97 had the value diff on disk. All three held. What it also said — widen
the whole record, every unit on the frame, not the one the brief names — is
the only reason this item is what it is.

### The record had been nine tenths uncompared

`run97_s_window_clocks_are_the_original_s` compared four fields of every
`GUY` block over 1,320 frames and nothing else. The `UNITDATA` that carries
those blocks prints the unit's own `x_internal`/`y_internal` and the goal of
its leading move order, and `gamelog::FrameUnit` had been parsing both into
`pos` and `goal` since it existed. Nobody had ever compared them.

Adding the two took one patch, and the first run printed this:

```
run97 POS: 36 units ever off — {…, (1, 27): 8442, (1, 28): 8442, (1, 29): 8442,
  (1, 31): 8442, … (1, 42): 8442}
```

Sixteen units — the whole of player 1's army — off **position** from frame
8442. Item 347 had parked that band as a free `walk_slot` choice "from block
8443" costing no draw. The band's frame was the frame the army was
re-ordered, and the units were not choosing a different animation slot for
the same walk: they were walking somewhere else.

### 8442 is an `Army::find_target`, and both sides spend its draws

```
frame 8442: ours 7 theirs 7 — at 0, ours Army::find_target+0x7df theirs Army::find_target+0x7df
```

Two candidate scores on each side, in step. So the RNG was not the question;
who got the order was. The dump answers that in one line per unit:

| dump, block 8443 (sim 8442) | `group` | order `id` | `orig` | units |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1/31`–`1/39` | 64 | 8448408 | (44851, 22480) | 9 |
| `1/27`,`28`,`29`,`40`,`41`,`42` | 65 | 8192502 | (39133, 21131) | 6 |

**Two groups, two order ids.** The army's `GROUPATTACKTOORDER` on that frame
reaches nine units. This crate's reached fifteen — one army, `army Some(1)`
on every one of them.

### The reading had it; the implementation never did

`docs/GROUPS.md` §3.2, third bullet, written at the first reading:

> Then every active member: if its current `+0x80` is a **different** live
> slot, that group's `kill(unit, who, 0, 0)` removes it there first; then
> `+0x80 = the new slot`.

`Groups::push_group@0070f9e0`'s second walk, and `Group::kill@00714110`
through the old group's vtable `+0x10`. `crates/sim`'s `push_group` was six
lines that recorded `pushed_group` for the collision pass and returned.

What had never been written down is what the bullet *means for an army*,
because an army's one group is its `units` list (`docs/ARMY.md` §3.2). A
unit killed out of it stops being `Army::member`, stops being counted by
`Army::normalize`, and every later `Group::action_*` the army issues walks a
list it is no longer on. There is no `remove_unit` and nothing notices.

So §12's probe — Great Lakes 8186, already landed, already diffed, its own
orders already right — is **also** how six units leave the army for good.
`Group::add` brings both squads, `push_group(force = 1)` installs them, and
the army is nine from that frame on. `docs/ARMY.md` has it as §3.4.

`Sim::unseat_group` is the fix: `Group::kill`'s own walk (up to the captain,
down the `o_down` chain — so what leaves is a whole squad, never a figure),
then `army_normalize` on each army that lost anyone.

### The probe that earned its place

`RON_DEBUG_ARMIES=<lo>-<hi>` — every valid army's slot, status, target,
counts and membership. The third of the family (`RON_DEBUG_UNIT`,
`RON_DEBUG_BUILDS`), and the one the dump cannot supply: **no capture on
disk carries an `ARMYDATA` record**, so an army's target, `pos`, `muster`
and `rally_dist` are only ever visible from this side. It is what said the
army was fifteen and then that it was nine.

### What this does not establish

- **A member's previous *stack* group.** The simulation holds one
  `pushed_group` and no pool, so the only group `unseat_group` can empty is
  an army's. In the original a unit pushed twice in a frame is killed out of
  the first pushed slot as well. Nothing in Great Lakes or East Indies
  pushes the same unit twice, and no dump would show it: the unit's `group`
  number is printed, but two consecutive stack pushes leave the same number.
  **The capture that would refuse it**: a frame where one unit takes a
  `get_goody_box` and a `scout_issue` in the same tick, with `UNITS=3`, its
  `group` read either side.
- **`equals_group`'s slot reuse.** `push_group` skips the kill when the
  member's current group **is** the slot being written. With no pool the
  simulation cannot tell a reused slot from a new one, so it always kills.
  The two differ only for a group pushed twice with the same members in the
  same order, which is the case `equals_group` exists for; the visible
  effect would be an army losing members to its *own* re-push, and no army
  re-pushes its own group — `army_group` is never passed to `push_group`.
- **Whether 8186's six ever come back.** `Unit::add_to_army` can re-add
  them (§4), and nothing in this item says the original's do not rejoin
  later. Below the word they do not: the dump has them in group 65 on every
  block from 8443 to 9349.
- **The order the army's own group is emptied in.** `Group::kill` shifts
  the tail down over the removed slot, and `unseat_group` uses `retain`,
  which is the same order. It matters only if anything reads the group's
  list *positionally* — §12's probe does, `get_unit(0)` and
  `get_unit(count − 1)` — and this crate's `units.first()`/`last()` agree
  with the shift. Nothing checks that against a dump.
