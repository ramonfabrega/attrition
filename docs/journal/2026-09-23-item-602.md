## The floor, first (`dd14135`)

**The `rolling` row compared the wrong field.** `AmmoData` is `+0x4
flags` (a byte) and `+0x5 rolling` (a second byte). `Ammo::init`'s flag 4
(COMBAT §42.2) goes into `flags`. `rolling` has two writers: `Ammo::init`
zeroes it on every round (`0067bbf0:830`), and `Ammo::init_crash` gives a
falling aircraft a random roll. The widenings compared this crate's flag
4 with the byte. The dump's own record says `flags 6` on both sides. So
"`rolling` 1 against 0" never parted: bit 4 agrees on every chariot
round. `ammo_flag_rows` now reads `flags & 4`, `flags & 8` and the byte
as three rows, at both `AMMO` comparison sites.

The widening keeps 651 and 652 whole, beside the word's two blocks, and
prints both sides' rounds there. On both captures, 651–652 part only on
the launch point (`sx`, `sy`, `sz`) and on the two fields taken from it,
the bearing `angle` and the arc's `v1z`. The landing (`ex`, `ey`, `ez`),
`total_time`, the target (`whom`, `ox`), `accuracy`, both flag bits and
every other record and figure on the two blocks agree.

## The float, measured on every cell

`sim::pivot::release_offset` is the branch in integers (millionths, §54's
sine). The margin argument alone was not enough: 128 of the 369,664
reachable cells sit within 10⁻⁴ of an integer. So I adapted 603's unicorn
oracle to the pivot branch and ran the original's `get_position` on
**every** cell, in 23 s. Six disagree, each where the float rounds onto
the integer from just below. They are pinned per row. The first cell set
rounded the turret steps with Python's banker's rounding. The packet's
own table fixed that, and it found two more cells.
