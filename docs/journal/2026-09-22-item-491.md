# 491 — the death draw, and the arrow that rolls on

**Booked by** §41.5 as one frame with two sides, and the brief said what
had been true nine times before it: "in this chain the frame has been
right every time the named mechanism was wrong. Re-measure before you
diagnose." One of the two named mechanisms was right and one was not,
and the one that was not was not even about the right object.

Chapter two's word **683 → 695**. The section is `docs/COMBAT.md` §42.

## Widening first, and the widening had already been printed

The brief's step 1 was to re-run chapter two's widening before a line
changed, and it said exactly what §41.5 records: five rows, word at 683,
ours seven draws against six, parting at draw 0. Step 2 was the two keys
`coverage` carries as unread — `hold_frames` and `DEATH_OBJS` — and they
are where the item actually turned, though not the way I expected.

What settled the whole thing in twenty minutes was not either of those.
It was `grep`ping the dump for the **AMMO** records around 683, which
item 485 had opened three days earlier and which nobody had looked at
past the launch table:

```
  block 683   AMMO o 7  cur_time 6/7  whom 1 ox 8  flags 6
  block 683   AMMO o 8  cur_time 6/7  whom 1 ox 8  flags 6
  block 684   AMMO o 8  cur_time 7/7  whom -1 ox -1  flags 14   ← still there
  block 685   AMMO o 8  cur_time 8/7  whom -1 ox -1  flags 14
  block 686   —
```

`0/7`'s arrow lands and kills `1/8`. `0/8`'s **does not land at all**.
Its target is forgotten, `flags |= 8`, and it flies two more frames.
That is `Ammo::inc_time`'s flag-4 arm, `docs/COMBAT.md` §9.2 has
described it since the mechanic was written, and §41.5 reached past it
for §9.2's *other* sentence instead.

## The mechanism that was wrong was wrong about which object

§41.5's candidate was "a target that is no longer active has its
`hold_frames` bumped". The bump is real. It reads

```
if (this->field_0x3c >= 0 && this->field_0x40 >= 0 &&
    (objects[field_0x3c][field_0x40]->flags & 1) == 0)
    *(short *)(that + 0x32) += 1;
```

and `Ammo::init` fills `+0x3c`/`+0x40` from the **shooter**. The target
is `+0x48`/`+0x4c`, four and eight bytes along. `Object::die`'s own scan
matches on the same pair, which is §11's "the slot is held until its
last shot has landed" — a dead archer's slot, not a dead target's.

So the sentence in §9.2 had been backwards since the section was
written, it had never been checked because nothing parsed the field, and
it cost item 485 a hypothesis. `hold_frames` is a row now, and what it
asserts is the thing the corrected reading implies: **every living
unit-frame of both headline windows is zero**, because every writer is
on a dead slot. It was made to fail by starting a unit at 3.

## The half that was named right, and how it stopped being a reading

`0060fb06` is `Unit::close+0xcb6` and §41.5 had it. What it did not have
was `dtype`: it wrote `cur_anim 17` says a normal arrow on a foot unit
is 2, which is a dump agreeing with a reading. Two instruments make it a
derivation, and neither is the reading:

- the trace carries the **seed before the draw** at that site on frame
  683 — `0xadfe4aea`. One LCG step and `((seed & 0xffff) × 0xffff) >> 16`
  is **64832**, which is even, so the roll contributes 0;
- `DEATH_OBJS` carries `cur_anim 17`, and `2 × dtype + 0xd = 17` has one
  solution.

The reading agrees independently, which is the point: `get_damage` opens
at 2 and only EXPLOSIVE, BOMBARD and a building attacker write it. And
all four of run112's deaths carry `cur_anim 17` — four even rolls is not
a coincidence, it is four deaths at `dtype 2`.

The decompiler prints the anim through a float local
(`fVar19 = (float)(param_1 * 2 + 0xd + uVar10)`), which is the shape
`docs/audit/README.md` says to settle in the listing. `llvm-objdump` gave
`lea 0xd(,%ebx,2); add %eax` and the three call sites' return addresses
in a minute.

## Both halves, and the arithmetic said so before the run did

7 − 2 + 1 = 6. That is the whole prediction, and it is why I took both
halves rather than landing one: the count only reaches the original's
if the puncture pair goes *and* the death draw arrives. It did, and the
word went twelve frames rather than one.

## What I could not take, and why it is not a residue

The rolled arrow comes down two frames later and `check_hit` finds
`1/7` — `19+5/16`, a **flank** hit, which is why it is worth more than
double an ordinary arrow (§42.4 has both sides of the arithmetic). This
crate rolls the arrow on and cannot land it: the arc is
`v1z × t + sz + GRAV_Z × t² / 2` in IEEE singles, `v1z` a float the dump
itself prints, and `TerrainOut::find_data_z` a bilinear interpolation
over a float height surface this crate does not carry — a different
quantity from the integer corner grid `crate::terrain` models.

That is a hard-constraint problem, not a residue. It is parked with its
frame and its delta, and the number is the commander's to mint: **686**, `damage 1/7` ours 0 theirs
19, `damage_frac` 0 against 5, `damage_frame` 0 against 685 — and nine
consequent rows at 696-698, where a wounded `1/7` outranks `1/6` on
§33's damage weight and the original's three bowmen retarget.

## What it cost

About two and a half hours, most of it reading and writing rather than
measuring. The measuring was cheap: the AMMO grep was five minutes, the
seed arithmetic was one Python script over `rontrace.log`, and the
widening ran in two and a half seconds each time.

The thing I would tell the next session: **the brief's step 1 and step 2
were both right and neither was where the answer was.** The answer was
in a record family the previous item had opened and not finished
reading. "Grep the dump before booking a reading" is in `CLAUDE.md` and
it is still the cheapest instruction in the file.
